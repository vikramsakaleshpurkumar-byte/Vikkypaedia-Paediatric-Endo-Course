## Authored MCQ

A 6-year-old boy in the pediatric intensive care unit with severe septic shock requiring mechanical ventilation and dopamine infusion undergoes routine thyroid testing. Results show TSH 0.12 mIU/L (reference: 0.5-4.5 mIU/L), total T3 22 ng/dL (reference: 85-170 ng/dL), and free T4 0.6 ng/dL (reference: 0.9-1.8 ng/dL). Serum reverse T3 (rT3) is markedly elevated. Which of the following is the most appropriate management regarding his thyroid status?

A. Initiate intravenous levothyroxine 2 mcg/kg/day to restore normal cellular metabolism
B. Initiate oral liothyronine (T3) replacement to normalize circulating T3 levels
C. Do not administer thyroid hormones; continue intensive management of septic shock and monitor
D. Perform urgent emergent total thyroidectomy

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Do not administer thyroid hormones; continue intensive management of septic shock and monitor

This patient exhibits classic severe non-thyroidal illness syndrome (NTIS, low T4/low T3 with suppressed TSH and elevated rT3) compounded by dopamine infusion, which suppresses TSH secretion. Randomized clinical trials have demonstrated that treating NTIS with either T4 or T3 does not improve clinical outcomes or survival and may be harmful. Current guidelines advise against thyroid hormone replacement and mandate focus on treating the primary systemic pathology.

## Authored MCQ

Which enzymatic alteration is primarily responsible for the rapid reduction of active triiodothyronine (T3) and simultaneous rise of reverse T3 (rT3) in acute non-thyroidal illness?

A. Upregulation of hepatic type 1 deiodinase (DIO1)
B. Upregulation of type 3 deiodinase (DIO3) with downregulation of type 1 (DIO1) and type 2 (DIO2) deiodinases
C. Inactivation of thyroid peroxidase (TPO) by circulating endotoxins
D. Increased activity of thyroxine-binding globulin (TBG) degrading proteases

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Upregulation of type 3 deiodinase (DIO3) with downregulation of type 1 (DIO1) and type 2 (DIO2) deiodinases

In NTIS, inflammatory cytokines downregulate type 1 (DIO1) and type 2 (DIO2) iodothyronine deiodinases, impairing 5'-deiodination of T4 to T3. Concurrently, type 3 deiodinase (DIO3) is induced in peripheral tissues, which actively deiodinates T4 into reverse T3 and degrades T3 into T2, causing the hallmark low T3 and high rT3 pattern.

## Authored MCQ

A 12-year-old girl is recovering from severe bacterial pneumonia in the pediatric ward. Three weeks ago in the ICU, her TSH was 0.2 mIU/L with a low free T3. Today, repeat morning labs show TSH 8.4 mIU/L (reference: 0.5-4.5 mIU/L) with normal free T4 1.2 ng/dL and normal free T3. She is clinically asymptomatic and eating well. What is the most appropriate management?

A. Diagnose permanent primary Hashimoto thyroiditis and initiate levothyroxine 50 mcg daily
B. Recognize this as a transient TSH rebound surge during recovery from non-thyroidal illness and recheck in 4-6 weeks without starting medication
C. Order emergent radioiodine thyroid scintigraphy and thyroid ultrasound
D. Obtain pituitary MRI to evaluate for a TSH-secreting pituitary macroadenoma

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Recognize this as a transient TSH rebound surge during recovery from non-thyroidal illness and recheck in 4-6 weeks without starting medication

During recovery from severe non-thyroidal illness, hypothalamic-pituitary suppression resolves, frequently producing a transient rebound elevation of serum TSH (typically 5-15 mIU/L) with normal or recovering peripheral thyroid hormone levels. This transient physiological surge stimulates the thyroid gland to replenish hormone stores and normalizes spontaneously within 4 to 8 weeks. Levothyroxine is not indicated.

## Authored MCQ

A 4-year-old child with severe septic shock is receiving continuous infusions of dopamine, epinephrine, and high-dose hydrocortisone. Thyroid function testing reveals TSH <0.05 mIU/L and free T4 0.5 ng/dL. Which factor is most directly contributing to the profound suppression of TSH in this patient?

A. Direct central inhibition of pituitary thyrotropes by dopamine and pharmacological glucocorticoids
B. Severe metabolic acidosis accelerating renal excretion of TSH
C. Activation of pituitary TSH receptor antibodies
D. Elevated circulating free fatty acids competing for TSH binding sites

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Direct central inhibition of pituitary thyrotropes by dopamine and pharmacological glucocorticoids

Both dopamine and pharmacological doses of glucocorticoids are potent central inhibitors of pituitary thyrotropes, directly suppressing pulsatile and basal TSH secretion. In critically ill children receiving dopamine and hydrocortisone, TSH is almost universally suppressed to undetectable or near-undetectable levels, simulating severe central hypothyroidism.

## Authored MCQ

When evaluating a critically ill pediatric patient with low free T4 and low-normal TSH, which biochemical test is most useful to help differentiate euthyroid sick syndrome from central (hypopituitary) hypothyroidism?

A. Serum reverse T3 (rT3)
B. Serum thyroid peroxidase (TPO) antibodies
C. Serum thyroglobulin concentration
D. Urinary iodine concentration

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Serum reverse T3 (rT3)

Serum reverse T3 (rT3) is the most valuable differentiator: in non-thyroidal illness syndrome, rT3 is characteristically elevated or high-normal due to DIO3 activation and reduced peripheral clearance. In contrast, in central hypothyroidism, pituitary failure causes a global deficiency of substrate T4 secretion without peripheral shunting, resulting in a low or undetectable serum rT3.
