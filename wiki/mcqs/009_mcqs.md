## Authored MCQ

A 13-year-old boy is brought for evaluation of rapid linear growth. Over the past 18 months, his height has jumped from the 75th percentile to well above the 99th percentile (height SDS +3.4), with a height velocity of 12.5 cm/year. Physical exam reveals coarse facial features, mandibular prognathism, large fleshy hands and feet, and hyperhidrosis. Serum IGF-I is markedly elevated at +4.8 SDS. What test is required to confirm autonomous growth hormone hypersecretion?

A. Standard oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) evaluating for failure of serum growth hormone suppression
B. Arginine plus growth hormone-releasing hormone stimulation test
C. Random morning serum growth hormone measurement following an overnight fast
D. 24-hour urinary free cortisol excretion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Standard oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) evaluating for failure of serum growth hormone suppression

In normal individuals, an oral glucose load suppresses serum GH to <1.0 mcg/L (or <0.4 mcg/L on sensitive assays). Failure to suppress GH during an OGTT confirms autonomous GH hypersecretion (pituitary gigantism/acromegaly).

## Authored MCQ

A 2-year-old female presents with massive accelerated linear growth (height SDS +4.2), generalized soft tissue overgrowth, voracious appetite, and acromegalic facial features that began at 8 months of age. Laboratory workup reveals GH 68 ng/mL, IGF-I SDS +5.1, and prolactin 88 ng/mL (reference <20 ng/mL). Brain MRI shows a homogeneously enhancing pituitary macroadenoma with suprasellar extension. Microarray analysis is most likely to identify which molecular etiology?

A. Microduplication of chromosome Xq26.3 involving the GPR101 gene (X-linked acrogigantism / X-LAG)
B. Inactivating mutation in the NSD1 gene causing Sotos syndrome
C. Heterozygous fibrillin-1 (FBN1) mutation causing Marfan syndrome
D. Paternal 15q11-q13 deletion causing Prader-Willi syndrome

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Microduplication of chromosome Xq26.3 involving the GPR101 gene (X-linked acrogigantism / X-LAG)

X-LAG (X-linked acrogigantism) is caused by microduplication of Xq26.3 encompassing GPR101. It is the classic cause of infancy-onset gigantism (onset <5 years, often <12 months) characterized by extreme growth velocity, high GH and prolactin, and massive pituitary adenomas or hyperplasia.

## Authored MCQ

A 15-year-old male undergoes transsphenoidal surgical debulking for an invasive pituitary somatotroph macroadenoma. Postoperatively, his serum IGF-I remains elevated at +3.2 SDS, and MRI demonstrates residual cavernous sinus invasion. He is initiated on long-acting octreotide, but his IGF-I remains elevated after 6 months. What pharmacological agent with a distinct mechanism of action can be added to block peripheral IGF-I generation?

A. Pegvisomant
B. Mecasermin
C. Somatropin
D. Fludrocortisone

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Pegvisomant

Pegvisomant is a genetically engineered growth hormone receptor antagonist that binds to the GHR and prevents functional receptor dimerization, blocking peripheral IGF-I production without requiring pituitary tumor shrinkage.

## Authored MCQ

A 7-year-old boy presents with accelerated growth (height SDS +3.1), macrocephaly (head circumference >98th percentile), prominent high forehead with frontoparietal balding, down-slanting palpebral fissures, a long narrow face, and a pointed chin. He has mild intellectual disability. Endocrine testing demonstrates a normal basal IGF-I and normal suppression of GH on oral glucose testing. What is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Sotos syndrome (cerebral gigantism)
B. Pituitary gigantism secondary to somatotroph adenoma
C. Precocious puberty secondary to a hypothalamic hamartoma
D. McCune-Albright syndrome with autonomous somatotroph hyperplasia

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Sotos syndrome (cerebral gigantism)

Sotos syndrome (caused by NSD1 haploinsufficiency) is a classic genetic overgrowth disorder presenting with macrocephaly, characteristic facial gestalt (high forehead, pointed chin), intellectual disability, and normal growth hormone regulation.

## Authored MCQ

A 14-year-old girl with Marfan syndrome is evaluated for severe tall stature (height SDS +3.2, current height 184 cm) with projected adult height of 195 cm. She has severe pectus excavatum, arachnodactyly, joint hypermobility, and aortic root dilation (Z-score +2.8). Which management strategy is contraindicated for reducing her linear growth potential?

A. High-dose estrogen therapy, due to the unacceptable risk of accelerating aortic root dilation and thromboembolism
B. Percutaneous bilateral epiphysiodesis of the distal femur and proximal tibia
C. Tight blood pressure control with an angiotensin receptor blocker or beta-blocker
D. Annual surveillance with transthoracic echocardiogram

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

High-dose estrogen therapy, due to the unacceptable risk of accelerating aortic root dilation and thromboembolism

Historically, high-dose supraphysiological estrogen was used to accelerate epiphyseal fusion in tall girls; however, in Marfan syndrome, high-dose estrogens are contraindicated because they can increase cardiovascular risk, accelerate aortic root enlargement, and increase thromboembolic complications. Percutaneous epiphysiodesis is preferred if height reduction is indicated.
