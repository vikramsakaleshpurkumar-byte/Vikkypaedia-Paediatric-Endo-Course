## Authored MCQ

A newborn infant is noted to have short limbs, macrocephaly with frontal bossing, and a depressed nasal bridge. Radiographs demonstrate shortened long bones with flared metaphyses, square iliac wings with horizontal acetabular roofs, and narrowing of the lumbar interpedicular distances caudally. What is the fundamental cellular mechanism causing this skeletal dysplasia?

A. Constitutive activation of FGFR3 signaling that hyperactivates MAPK/ERK, inhibiting chondrocyte proliferation in the growth plate
B. Defective synthesis of type II collagen alpha-1 chains impairing articular cartilage tensile strength
C. Overexpression of parathyroid hormone-related peptide (PTHrP) leading to chondrocyte hyperproliferation
D. Failure of osteoclast proton pump vacuolar ATPase causing generalized dense bone accumulation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Constitutive activation of FGFR3 signaling that hyperactivates MAPK/ERK, inhibiting chondrocyte proliferation in the growth plate

Achondroplasia is caused by a heterozygous gain-of-function mutation in FGFR3, which constitutively activates MAPK/ERK signaling and suppresses endochondral growth plate chondrogenesis.

## Authored MCQ

A 2-month-old infant with achondroplasia undergoes routine health supervision. The infant has hypotonia and head lag. Polysomnography demonstrates recurrent central apneas with desaturations to 82%. What urgent diagnostic test is indicated to guide clinical management?

A. MRI or thin-slice CT scan of the craniocervical junction to evaluate for foramen magnum stenosis
B. Continuous 24-hour video EEG to rule out infantile epileptic spasms
C. Echocardiogram to rule out coarctation of the aorta
D. Barium swallow study to assess for tracheoesophageal fistula

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

MRI or thin-slice CT scan of the craniocervical junction to evaluate for foramen magnum stenosis

In achondroplasia, endochondral ossification failure severely restricts the growth of the cranial base, causing foramen magnum stenosis. In infants, this can compress the brainstem and cervical spinal cord, causing central apneas and sudden death requiring suboccipital surgical decompression.

## Authored MCQ

A 6-year-old boy with genetically confirmed achondroplasia is started on vosoritide (15 mcg/kg subcutaneously daily). By what mechanism does this therapeutic agent counteract his underlying molecular disease process?

A. Binds to the natriuretic peptide receptor-B (NPR-B), increasing intracellular cGMP and inhibiting the hyperactive MAPK/ERK cascade
B. Directly cleaves circulating FGFR3 molecules via monoclonal antibody-mediated proteolysis
C. Stimulates hepatic growth hormone receptors to produce supranormal levels of IGF-I
D. Selectively binds bone hydroxyapatite crystals to prevent osteoclast-mediated bone resorption

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Binds to the natriuretic peptide receptor-B (NPR-B), increasing intracellular cGMP and inhibiting the hyperactive MAPK/ERK cascade

Vosoritide is a recombinant modified C-type natriuretic peptide (CNP) analog that binds the NPR-B receptor, triggering intracellular cGMP generation. cGMP-dependent signals antagonize the overactive MAPK/ERK pathway downstream of mutant FGFR3, restoring growth plate chondrogenesis.

## Authored MCQ

A 7-year-old child presents with mild disproportionate short stature (height SDS -2.8). She does not have the classic macrocephaly, frontal bossing, or trident hand configuration seen in achondroplasia, but radiographs demonstrate mild rhizomelic shortening of the long bones and minimal lumbar interpedicular narrowing. Genetic testing reveals a heterozygous p.Asn540Lys mutation in FGFR3. What is the diagnosis?

A. Hypochondroplasia
B. Thanatophoric dysplasia type I
C. Spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia congenita
D. Campomelic dysplasia

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Hypochondroplasia

Hypochondroplasia is a milder skeletal dysplasia caused by heterozygous FGFR3 mutations (most commonly p.Asn540Lys). Children have rhizomelic short stature but typically lack severe craniofacial dysmorphism.

## Authored MCQ

A 3-year-old girl with recurrent fractures following minor trauma is evaluated. Physical examination reveals blue sclerae, joint hypermobility, and normal teeth (no dentinogenesis imperfecta). Radiographs demonstrate osteopenia, gracile long bones, and healed fractures without wormian bones or metaphyseal flaring. What is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Osteogenesis imperfecta type I
B. Achondroplasia
C. Léri-Weill dyschondrosteosis
D. Hypochondroplasia

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Osteogenesis imperfecta type I

Osteogenesis imperfecta type I (mild non-deforming) is caused by heterozygous null mutations in COL1A1 leading to quantitative reduction of normal type I collagen, presenting with recurrent fractures, blue sclerae, and normal teeth.
