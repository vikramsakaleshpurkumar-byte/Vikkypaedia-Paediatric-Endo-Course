## Authored MCQ

A 16-year-old male presents with persistent frontal headaches and bitemporal hemianopsia on visual field testing. Brain MRI reveals a 2.8-cm sellar and suprasellar mass compressing the optic chiasm. Initial serum prolactin is reported as 62 mcg/L (reference: <20 mcg/L). The neurosurgeon suspects a non-functioning pituitary macroadenoma with stalk compression and schedules emergent surgical resection. However, the pediatric endocrinologist requests that the laboratory re-assay the sample with a 1:100 dilution, which returns a true prolactin concentration of 4800 mcg/L. What phenomenon explains the initial low reading, and what is the proper therapy?

A. Hook effect; initiate medical therapy with cabergoline immediately
B. Macroprolactinemia; proceed with transsphenoidal surgical resection
C. Assay cross-reactivity with GH; administer pegvisomant
D. Secondary adrenal insufficiency; give high-dose dexamethasone

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Hook effect; initiate medical therapy with cabergoline immediately

This patient has a giant macroprolactinoma displaying the 'hook effect' (high-dose hook effect). Immense antigen (prolactin) concentrations saturate both capture and signal antibodies in two-site immunometric assays, causing falsely low readings that mimic stalk compression. A 1:100 dilution reveals the true massive elevation (4800 mcg/L). Macroprolactinomas respond dramatically to medical therapy with dopamine agonists (cabergoline), which rapidly shrinks the tumor and decompresses the optic chiasm, making cabergoline the treatment of choice over immediate surgery.

## Authored MCQ

A 15-year-old girl presents with secondary amenorrhea and spontaneous bilateral milky breast discharge (galactorrhea). Serum prolactin is 145 mcg/L. Sellar MRI reveals a 6-mm well-circumscribed microadenoma in the left pituitary wing. What is the most effective initial management?

A. Oral cabergoline 0.25 mg twice weekly
B. Immediate stereotactic radiosurgery (Gamma Knife)
C. Transsphenoidal microdissection of the left pituitary wing
D. High-dose combined oral contraceptives

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Oral cabergoline 0.25 mg twice weekly

Cabergoline (0.25 to 0.5 mg once or twice weekly) is the established first-line therapy for microprolactinomas in adolescents. Cabergoline normalizes prolactin, resolves galactorrhea, restores regular ovulatory menses, and shrinks or stabilizes tumor size in >90% of patients with minimal adverse effects. Surgery and radiation are reserved for rare refractory cases.

## Authored MCQ

A 14-year-old adolescent with schizophrenia treated with risperidone 3 mg daily for 6 months presents with bilateral breast enlargement and galactorrhea. Serum prolactin is 85 mcg/L. Pituitary MRI is completely normal. What is the pharmacological mechanism causing pathologically elevated prolactin levels in this patient?

A. Antagonism of dopamine D2 receptors on anterior pituitary lactotropes
B. Direct stimulation of hypothalamic TRH secretion
C. Inhibition of hepatic prolactin degradation
D. Downregulation of peripheral estrogen receptors

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Antagonism of dopamine D2 receptors on anterior pituitary lactotropes

Risperidone and other first- and second-generation antipsychotics are potent dopamine D2 receptor antagonists. By blocking D2 receptors on anterior pituitary lactotrope cell membranes, they relieve tonic hypothalamic dopaminergic inhibition, resulting in autonomous prolactin synthesis and secretion with hyperprolactinemia (typically 40-150 mcg/L) and galactorrhea.

## Authored MCQ

A 12-year-old boy presents with severe fatigue, cold intolerance, constipation, growth arrest, and mild galactorrhea. Serum prolactin is 58 mcg/L. Pituitary MRI reveals diffuse, symmetric pituitary enlargement ('pituitary hyperplasia'). Thyroid function testing demonstrates TSH 420 mIU/L and free T4 0.1 ng/dL. What is the most appropriate management?

A. Initiate oral levothyroxine replacement and repeat prolactin and MRI after achieving euthyroidism
B. Initiate high-dose cabergoline and schedule transsphenoidal pituitary resection
C. Perform urgent biopsy of the pituitary mass to rule out craniopharyngioma
D. Order whole-brain external beam radiation therapy

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Initiate oral levothyroxine replacement and repeat prolactin and MRI after achieving euthyroidism

Severe primary hypothyroidism causes extreme compensatory hypersecretion of hypothalamic TRH. TRH acts as a potent prolactin secretagogue, causing secondary hyperprolactinemia. Concurrently, chronic lack of thyroid hormone negative feedback causes profound hypertrophy and hyperplasia of pituitary thyrotropes and lactotropes, mimicking a pituitary macroadenoma on MRI. Treatment with levothyroxine restores euthyroidism, normalizes prolactin, and completely resolves pituitary enlargement, avoiding unnecessary pituitary surgery.

## Authored MCQ

An asymptomatic 17-year-old girl has an incidental serum prolactin of 92 mcg/L measured during a dermatology workup for mild acne. She has completely regular monthly menses and no galactorrhea. Sellar MRI is normal. Serum treatment with polyethylene glycol (PEG) precipitation reveals that 88% of circulating prolactin precipitates as macroprolactin, with normal free monomeric prolactin. What is the appropriate clinical approach?

A. Reassurance and observation without dopamine agonist therapy
B. Initiate cabergoline 0.5 mg weekly to prevent future tumor development
C. Repeat pituitary MRI with dynamic contrast every 6 months
D. Perform bilateral prophylactic mastectomy

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Reassurance and observation without dopamine agonist therapy

Macroprolactinemia is a benign condition where circulating prolactin forms complexes with immunoglobulin G (IgG) autoantibodies. These high-molecular-weight complexes (>150 kDa) cannot cross capillary walls to bind target tissue prolactin receptors, rendering them biologically inactive (hence the patient has regular menses and no galactorrhea). However, standard immunoassays detect macroprolactin, causing elevated reported levels. Polyethylene glycol (PEG) precipitation precipitates the immune complexes, confirming normal monomeric prolactin. No treatment or imaging surveillance is required.
