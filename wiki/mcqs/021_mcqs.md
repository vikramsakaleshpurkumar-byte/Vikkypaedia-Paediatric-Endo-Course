## Authored MCQ

A 14-month-old dark-skinned boy who has been exclusively breastfed without vitamin D supplementation presents with delayed walking, frontal bossing, prominent costochondral enlargement ('rachitic rosary'), and marked widening of both wrists. Serum labs show: calcium 8.9 mg/dL (normal: 8.8-10.2 mg/dL), phosphate 2.8 mg/dL (normal: 4.5-6.5 mg/dL), alkaline phosphatase 1250 U/L (normal: 150-400 U/L), intact PTH 185 pg/mL (normal: 15-65 pg/mL), and 25-hydroxyvitamin D 7 ng/mL (deficiency <12 ng/mL). What biochemical stage of calcipenic nutritional rickets is present?

A. Stage 1 (early rickets with transient hypocalcemia)
B. Stage 2 (secondary hyperparathyroidism with normal calcium and hypophosphatemia)
C. Stage 3 (severe decompensated rickets with hypocalcemia and bone buffering failure)
D. Adynamic bone disease

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Stage 2 (secondary hyperparathyroidism with normal calcium and hypophosphatemia)

This child is in Stage 2 calcipenic rickets. In Stage 2, secondary hyperparathyroidism (PTH 185 pg/mL) successfully normalizes serum calcium (8.9 mg/dL) by mobilizing bone mineral and increasing renal calcium reabsorption. However, excess PTH causes renal phosphate wasting, leading to hypophosphatemia (2.8 mg/dL) and marked alkaline phosphatase elevation with classic rachitic skeletal lesions.

## Authored MCQ

A 2-year-old child with severe nutritional rickets is prescribed high-dose ergocalciferol 50,000 IU weekly. The physician also prescribes oral elemental calcium 500 mg daily. What adverse complication does concurrent calcium supplementation specifically prevent during the initial weeks of therapy?

A. Hungry bone syndrome with precipitous hypocalcemia and tetany
B. Acute renal tubular acidosis
C. Hypercalcemic crisis and nephrocalcinosis
D. Hyperphosphatemic tumoral calcinosis

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Hungry bone syndrome with precipitous hypocalcemia and tetany

When active mineralization is restored by vitamin D administration, large amounts of extracellular calcium and phosphate are rapidly incorporated into the unmineralized osteoid ('hungry bone syndrome'). Without adequate oral calcium supplementation, circulating serum calcium drops precipitously, triggering acute hypocalcemic tetany, seizures, or laryngospasm.

## Authored MCQ

An 8-month-old infant presents with severe rickets, hypocalcemia (6.8 mg/dL), elevated PTH (320 pg/mL), and elevated alkaline phosphatase. Circulating 25-hydroxyvitamin D is normal (45 ng/mL), but serum 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D is undetectable (<5 pg/mL). High-dose parent vitamin D3 produces no clinical or biochemical improvement. What gene mutation is responsible for this autosomal recessive disorder?

A. CYP27B1 (encoding 25-hydroxyvitamin D 1-alpha-hydroxylase)
B. CYP2R1 (encoding hepatic 25-hydroxylase)
C. VDR (encoding vitamin D receptor)
D. PHEX (encoding phosphate-regulating endopeptidase)

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

CYP27B1 (encoding 25-hydroxyvitamin D 1-alpha-hydroxylase)

Vitamin D-Dependent Rickets type 1A (VDDR1A) is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by inactivating mutations in CYP27B1, which encodes renal 25-hydroxyvitamin D 1-alpha-hydroxylase. Patients cannot convert 25-OH-D into active 1,25-(OH)2D, resulting in early severe rickets with normal 25-OH-D and undetectable 1,25-(OH)2D. The condition responds completely to physiological doses of calcitriol (1,25-(OH)2D).

## Authored MCQ

A 10-month-old infant with severe rickets is found to have total alopecia (complete absence of scalp hair, eyebrows, and eyelashes). Laboratory testing shows severe hypocalcemia, secondary hyperparathyroidism, and strikingly elevated 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (>250 pg/mL). What is the underlying disease mechanism?

A. Inactivating mutation in the vitamin D receptor (VDR) causing end-organ hormone resistance (VDDR2A)
B. CYP27B1 1-alpha-hydroxylase deficiency
C. Subcutaneous fat necrosis with hypercalcemia
D. Nutritional zinc deficiency

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Inactivating mutation in the vitamin D receptor (VDR) causing end-organ hormone resistance (VDDR2A)

Vitamin D-Dependent Rickets type 2A (VDDR2A) is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by inactivating mutations in the vitamin D receptor (VDR). Because target tissues cannot respond to 1,25-(OH)2D, serum 1,25-(OH)2D levels rise massively due to loss of negative feedback. Alopecia totalis is a unique phenotypic marker of severe VDR disruption because VDR is essential for hair follicle cycling.

## Authored MCQ

What classic radiographic finding on wrist radiographs confirms healing of rickets following 3 weeks of vitamin D and calcium therapy?

A. Appearance of a well-defined, dense radiopaque line of provisional calcification across the metaphysis
B. Complete disappearance of the distal radial epiphysis
C. Development of diffuse subperiosteal cortical thinning
D. Formation of Looser zones along the ulnar diaphysis

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Appearance of a well-defined, dense radiopaque line of provisional calcification across the metaphysis

The earliest objective radiographic evidence of rachitic healing is the deposition of calcium in the newly mineralizing hypertrophic zone, forming a crisp, linear radiodense band—the line of provisional calcification—across the metaphyseal margin, typically visible within 2 to 4 weeks of starting therapy.
