## Authored MCQ

A 10-year-old girl is evaluated for severe hypertension (blood pressure 152/96 mmHg). Laboratory testing reveals sodium 144 mEq/L, potassium 2.7 mEq/L, and serum bicarbonate 32 mEq/L. Plasma renin activity is profoundly suppressed (<0.1 ng/mL/h), and plasma aldosterone concentration is also undetectable (<1.0 ng/dL). Urinary free cortisol-to-cortisone ratio is normal. Treatment with high-dose spironolactone produces no reduction in blood pressure. However, initiating oral amiloride normalizes her blood pressure and potassium within 72 hours. What is the molecular diagnosis?

A. Liddle syndrome, caused by a gain-of-function mutation in an epithelial sodium channel (ENaC) subunit
B. Primary aldosteronism secondary to a solitary unilateral aldosterone-producing adenoma
C. Glucocorticoid-Remediable Aldosteronism (GRA) secondary to a chimeric CYP11B1/CYP11B2 gene
D. Apparent Mineralocorticoid Excess (AME) secondary to an HSD11B2 gene mutation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Liddle syndrome, caused by a gain-of-function mutation in an epithelial sodium channel (ENaC) subunit

Liddle syndrome is an autosomal dominant channelopathy caused by gain-of-function mutations in SCNN1B or SCNN1G that disrupt the PY motif, preventing ENaC degradation. The constitutive channel activation reabsorbs sodium independently of aldosterone, suppressing both renin and aldosterone. Because the mineralocorticoid receptor is bypassed, spironolactone fails, while direct ENaC blockers (amiloride) are curative.

## Authored MCQ

An 8-year-old boy presents with severe hypertension (148/92 mmHg) and mild hypokalemia (potassium 3.1 mEq/L). His father and paternal grandfather had early-onset hypertension and suffered fatal hemorrhagic strokes in their early 30s. Lab evaluation shows suppressed plasma renin activity (<0.2 ng/mL/h) and elevated plasma aldosterone (38 ng/dL). Administering oral dexamethasone 0.5 mg daily for 4 days results in complete normalization of blood pressure and normalization of plasma aldosterone to <2 ng/dL. What is the genetic basis of this disorder?

A. Unequal crossover fusing the promoter region of 11-beta-hydroxylase (CYP11B1) with the coding region of aldosterone synthase (CYP11B2)
B. Homozygous loss-of-function mutation in the 11-beta-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase type 2 (HSD11B2) gene
C. Activating somatic mutation in the KCNJ5 potassium channel gene in the adrenal cortex
D. Germline loss-of-function mutation in the mineralocorticoid receptor (NR3C2) gene

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Unequal crossover fusing the promoter region of 11-beta-hydroxylase (CYP11B1) with the coding region of aldosterone synthase (CYP11B2)

Glucocorticoid-Remediable Aldosteronism (GRA / Familial Hyperaldosteronism Type I) is an autosomal dominant condition caused by a chimeric CYP11B1/CYP11B2 fusion gene. Aldosterone is ectopically synthesized in the zona fasciculata under the regulation of ACTH rather than angiotensin II. Low-dose dexamethasone suppresses ACTH, completely shutting down aldosterone production and curing the hypertension.

## Authored MCQ

A 4-year-old boy presents with severe hypertension (142/90 mmHg), growth failure, and recurrent hypokalemic weakness (serum potassium 2.4 mEq/L). Plasma renin activity is <0.1 ng/mL/h and plasma aldosterone is undetectable (<1.0 ng/dL). 24-hour urine steroid metabolite profiling by GC-MS demonstrates a urinary ratio of (tetrahydrocortisol + 5alpha-tetrahydrocortisol) to tetrahydrocortisone of 18:1 (normal reference <1.0). What is the diagnosis?

A. Apparent Mineralocorticoid Excess (AME)
B. Liddle syndrome
C. Primary aldosteronism
D. Renal artery stenosis

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Apparent Mineralocorticoid Excess (AME)

Apparent Mineralocorticoid Excess (AME) is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by inactivating mutations in HSD11B2. Cortisol is not inactivated to cortisone in renal tubular cells, flooding the mineralocorticoid receptor (which has equal affinity for cortisol and aldosterone) and producing low-renin, low-aldosterone hypertension with a massively elevated urinary cortisol-to-cortisone metabolite ratio.

## Authored MCQ

A 3-week-old male infant presents with failure to thrive, vomiting, and profound dehydration. Lab testing shows sodium 121 mEq/L, potassium 7.6 mEq/L, and BUN 38 mg/dL. He has normal male genitalia. Urinalysis reveals pyuria, and renal ultrasound shows severe bilateral hydronephrosis with a dilated posterior urethra. Plasma aldosterone is markedly elevated at 280 ng/dL, and plasma renin activity is high. What is the diagnosis?

A. Secondary pseudohypoaldosteronism (PHA3) triggered by urinary tract obstruction and infection
B. Classic salt-wasting 21-hydroxylase deficiency
C. Congenital aldosterone synthase deficiency (CYP11B2)
D. Liddle syndrome

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Secondary pseudohypoaldosteronism (PHA3) triggered by urinary tract obstruction and infection

Secondary (type 3) pseudohypoaldosteronism is a transient resistance of the renal tubules to aldosterone, classically triggered in infants by acute urinary tract infection in the setting of congenital obstructive uropathy (posterior urethral valves). High aldosterone and renin in the presence of severe salt wasting and hyperkalemia confirm mineralocorticoid resistance.

## Authored MCQ

A 16-year-old boy presents with hypertension (150/94 mmHg) and hypokalemia (potassium 2.8 mEq/L). Laboratory testing shows suppressed plasma renin and suppressed aldosterone. On detailed dietary history, he admits to eating large quantities (200 g daily) of traditional black licorice imported from Europe over the past 3 months. What active substance in the licorice is responsible for his presentation?

A. Glycyrrhetinic acid, which reversibly inhibits the 11-beta-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase type 2 enzyme
B. Ephedrine alkaloids that directly stimulate alpha-1 adrenergic vascular receptors
C. Sodium chloride crystals that saturate the proximal tubule sodium-glucose cotransporter
D. Cyanogenic glycosides that block mitochondrial cytochrome c oxidase

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Glycyrrhetinic acid, which reversibly inhibits the 11-beta-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase type 2 enzyme

Black licorice contains glycyrrhizin, which is metabolized to glycyrrhetinic acid. Glycyrrhetinic acid is a potent competitive inhibitor of 11beta-HSD2 in the renal collecting duct, preventing the normal enzymatic conversion of cortisol to inactive cortisone and producing an acquired form of Apparent Mineralocorticoid Excess.
