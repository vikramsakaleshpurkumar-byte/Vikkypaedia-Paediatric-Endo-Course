## Authored MCQ

An 8-year-old child assigned female at birth (AFAB) identifies as a boy and experiences distress regarding his gender. On physical examination, he has no breast budding (Tanner stage 1) and no pubic hair. His parents ask whether he should start medications to block puberty. According to Endocrine Society and WPATH consensus guidelines, what is the most appropriate management at this time?

A. Support social transition and psychological counseling, but withhold all medical or hormonal interventions until physical onset of puberty (Tanner 2)
B. Initiate subcutaneous depot leuprolide immediately to prevent future breast development
C. Initiate low-dose oral testosterone enanthate 25 mg every 2 weeks
D. Obtain bone mineral density DXA and pelvic ultrasound

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Support social transition and psychological counseling, but withhold all medical or hormonal interventions until physical onset of puberty (Tanner 2)

Consensus clinical guidelines from the Endocrine Society and WPATH explicitly state that no medical or hormonal interventions (neither pubertal blockers nor sex steroids) are indicated in prepubertal children (Tanner stage 1). Care for prepubertal youth is entirely social, supportive, and psychological. Pubertal suppression with GnRH agonists is considered only after the physical onset of biological puberty is documented (Tanner stage 2).

## Authored MCQ

A 16-year-old transgender male (assigned female at birth) has been taking intramuscular testosterone cypionate 100 mg every 2 weeks for 12 months. He reports excellent voice deepening, increased muscle mass, and amenorrhea. Routine laboratory monitoring reveals: total testosterone 680 ng/dL (normal male range: 300-1000 ng/dL), AST 24 U/L, ALT 22 U/L, hemoglobin 18.2 g/dL (reference: 12-16 g/dL), and hematocrit 54% (reference: 36-46%). What is the most appropriate clinical action?

A. Reduce the testosterone dosage or increase injection interval and recheck hematocrit in 2-3 months to mitigate erythrocytosis risk
B. Increase testosterone to 150 mg every 2 weeks to maintain amenorrhea
C. Perform emergency phlebotomy and start hydroxyurea
D. Reassure the patient that hematocrit >54% is completely normal for adult males

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Reduce the testosterone dosage or increase injection interval and recheck hematocrit in 2-3 months to mitigate erythrocytosis risk

Testosterone stimulates renal erythropoietin production and bone marrow erythropoiesis. Polycythemia / erythrocytosis (hematocrit >50-52%) is a recognized dose-dependent complication of testosterone therapy that increases blood viscosity and thrombotic risk. When hematocrit exceeds 50-52%, guidelines recommend lowering the testosterone dose, lengthening the interval between injections, or switching from intramuscular injections to transdermal gel to avoid peak levels, rechecking CBC in 2-3 months.

## Authored MCQ

A 15-year-old transgender female (assigned male at birth) who has been on pubertal suppression with depot leuprolide acetate is initiating gender-affirming feminizing hormone therapy. What is the recommended first-line estrogen agent and route?

A. 17-beta estradiol administered orally (micronized) or transdermally via matrix patch
B. Oral ethinyl estradiol (synthetic estrogen found in oral contraceptives)
C. Oral conjugated equine estrogens (Premarin)
D. Intravenous diethylstilbestrol

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

17-beta estradiol administered orally (micronized) or transdermally via matrix patch

Endocrine Society guidelines recommend bioidentical 17-beta estradiol (administered orally as micronized estradiol or transdermally via patch) as the first-line estrogen for gender-affirming feminization. Synthetic estrogens (such as ethinyl estradiol) and conjugated equine estrogens are strictly contraindicated because they carry significantly higher risks of venous thromboembolism, cannot be accurately monitored in serum immunoassays, and promote hepatic coagulopathy.

## Authored MCQ

A 16-year-old transgender female taking oral 17-beta estradiol 4 mg daily and spironolactone 100 mg twice daily presents for routine 6-month laboratory surveillance. Which laboratory test is most critical to evaluate for spironolactone-related toxicity?

A. Serum potassium and renal function (BUN/creatinine)
B. Serum alkaline phosphatase
C. Total serum bilirubin
D. Serum ionized magnesium

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Serum potassium and renal function (BUN/creatinine)

Spironolactone functions as an androgen receptor antagonist and competitive aldosterone receptor antagonist. By blocking aldosterone in the cortical collecting tubule, it impairs renal potassium excretion. Routine monitoring of serum potassium and electrolytes is essential, especially when titrating doses, to detect hyperkalemia and acute renal insufficiency.

## Authored MCQ

What is the primary rationale for recommending discussion of fertility preservation with transgender adolescents prior to initiating GnRH agonist pubertal suppression or gender-affirming hormone therapy?

A. Prolonged pubertal suppression followed directly by gender-affirming hormones can halt or impair gametogenesis (spermatogenesis or oocyte maturation), potentially compromising future biological parenthood
B. Cryopreservation is legally required in all jurisdictions before starting hormones
C. Sex steroids cause permanent genetic mutations in germ cell DNA
D. GnRH agonists induce irreversible atrophy of pelvic bones

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Prolonged pubertal suppression followed directly by gender-affirming hormones can halt or impair gametogenesis (spermatogenesis or oocyte maturation), potentially compromising future biological parenthood

If pubertal suppression is initiated at Tanner stage 2 and followed seamlessly by gender-affirming sex steroids, gonadal tissue never undergoes the full gametogenic maturation required to produce mature sperm or fertilizable oocytes. Although gametogenesis may recover upon prolonged discontinuation of hormones in some individuals, it is not guaranteed. Comprehensive counseling regarding options for cryopreservation of sperm or oocytes prior to medical interventions is mandatory.
