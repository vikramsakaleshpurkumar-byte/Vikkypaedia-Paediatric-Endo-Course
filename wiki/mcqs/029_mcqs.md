## Authored MCQ

A 2-day-old macrosomic male infant (birth weight 4.4 kg) born to a non-diabetic mother presents with lethargy, poor feeding, and jitteriness. Point-of-care blood glucose is 28 mg/dL. A critical blood sample drawn prior to IV dextrose administration reveals: glucose 30 mg/dL, insulin 14 mcU/mL (inappropriately elevated), C-peptide 2.8 ng/mL, beta-hydroxybutyrate 0.2 mmol/L (suppressed), and free fatty acids 0.3 mmol/L (suppressed). Glucagon 1 mg IV is given, and blood glucose rises to 78 mg/dL at 30 minutes. What is the diagnosis?

A. Congenital hyperinsulinism (CHI)
B. Medium-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase (MCAD) deficiency
C. Growth hormone deficiency
D. Glycogen storage disease type I

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Congenital hyperinsulinism (CHI)

This neonate exhibits the classic biochemical signature of congenital hyperinsulinism: hypoglycemia (30 mg/dL) accompanied by detectable/elevated insulin (14 mcU/mL), elevated C-peptide, suppressed ketones (BOHB 0.2 mmol/L), suppressed free fatty acids, and a robust glycemic rise >30 mg/dL following glucagon challenge. Insulin suppresses lipolysis and ketogenesis while locking glycogen in the liver.

## Authored MCQ

A 1-week-old full-term neonate is diagnosed with diffuse congenital hyperinsulinism due to a homozygous ABCC8 mutation. Diazoxide therapy is initiated at 10 mg/kg/day. Which additional medication must be routinely co-prescribed with diazoxide to prevent a well-documented, life-threatening adverse complication?

A. Chlorothiazide (thiazide diuretic) to prevent fluid retention and pulmonary hypertension
B. Levothyroxine to prevent acute hypothyroidism
C. Potassium chloride to prevent severe hypokalemia
D. Sodium bicarbonate to treat proximal renal tubular acidosis

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Chlorothiazide (thiazide diuretic) to prevent fluid retention and pulmonary hypertension

Diazoxide stimulates renal tubular sodium and water retention through antidiuretic mechanisms. In neonates and young infants, this can precipitate massive fluid overload, congestive heart failure, and life-threatening pulmonary hypertension. Co-administration of a thiazide diuretic (such as chlorothiazide 7-10 mg/kg/day) is mandatory to promote natriuresis, prevent edema, and exert a synergistic hyperglycemic effect.

## Authored MCQ

A 14-month-old girl with a mild viral gastroenteritis and 14 hours of poor oral intake presents with lethargy, vomiting, and stupor. Serum glucose is 32 mg/dL. Critical labs show: beta-hydroxybutyrate 0.1 mmol/L (suppressed), free fatty acids 3.2 mmol/L (markedly elevated), insulin <1.0 mcU/mL (undetectable), and serum ammonia 120 mcmol/L. Plasma acylcarnitine analysis reveals a marked elevation of octanoylcarnitine (C8). What is the underlying disorder?

A. Medium-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase (MCAD) deficiency
B. Idiopathic ketotic hypoglycemia
C. Insulinoma
D. Congenital adrenal hyperplasia

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Medium-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase (MCAD) deficiency

MCAD deficiency is the most common fatty acid oxidation defect. During prolonged fasting or catabolic stress, impaired mitochondrial beta-oxidation of medium-chain fatty acids prevents generation of acetyl-CoA and ketone bodies, resulting in hypoketotic hypoglycemia (low BOHB despite elevated FFA) and hyperammonemia. The diagnostic hallmark is marked accumulation of medium-chain acylcarnitines, specifically octanoylcarnitine (C8).

## Authored MCQ

A 3-year-old slender boy presents with morning drowsiness and difficulty waking after having an early dinner and missing breakfast. Point-of-care glucose is 42 mg/dL. Blood drawn at that time reveals: insulin <1 mcU/mL, beta-hydroxybutyrate 4.2 mmol/L, free fatty acids 2.8 mmol/L, cortisol 22 mcg/dL, and growth hormone 12 ng/mL. Following a glass of orange juice, he rapidly returns to his normal playful self. What is the diagnosis?

A. Idiopathic ketotic hypoglycemia of childhood
B. Nesidioblastosis (congenital hyperinsulinism)
C. Carnitine palmitoyltransferase-1 (CPT-1) deficiency
D. Primary adrenal insufficiency

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Idiopathic ketotic hypoglycemia of childhood

Idiopathic ketotic hypoglycemia is the most common cause of hypoglycemia in children between 18 months and 5 years. It represents an exaggerated physiological response to fasting (accelerated starvation) in young, lean children who have limited hepatic glycogen stores and small muscle mass (limiting alanine for gluconeogenesis). The biochemical profile shows robust ketosis (BOHB >3-4 mmol/L), suppressed insulin (<1 mcU/mL), and appropriate counter-regulatory hormone surges (cortisol >18 mcg/dL, GH >10 ng/mL). It resolves spontaneously by age 6-8 years as muscle mass increases.

## Authored MCQ

A newborn male with recurrent persistent neuroglycopenia and low plasma glucose unresponsive to diazoxide undergoes genetic sequencing revealing a single paternally inherited ABCC8 mutation. What diagnostic imaging modality should be performed next to guide surgical planning?

A. 18F-fluoro-L-DOPA PET/CT of the pancreas
B. Abdominal ultrasound with color Doppler
C. Contrast-enhanced abdominal computed tomography
D. Magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP)

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

18F-fluoro-L-DOPA PET/CT of the pancreas

Paternal inheritance of a monoallelic K-ATP channel mutation (ABCC8 or KCNJ11) combined with somatic maternal loss of heterozygosity (LOH) at 11p15 causes focal congenital hyperinsulinism. Because beta cells take up L-DOPA and convert it to dopamine via DOPA decarboxylase, 18F-fluoro-L-DOPA PET/CT specifically visualizes and localizes the adenomatous focal lesion, allowing a curative, organ-sparing partial pancreatectomy rather than near-total pancreatectomy.
