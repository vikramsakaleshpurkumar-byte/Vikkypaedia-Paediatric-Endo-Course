## Authored MCQ

A 12-year-old girl is found to have an asymptomatic 1.4-cm solitary firm nodule in the right thyroid lobe on routine physical examination. Serum TSH is 1.8 mIU/L (normal: 0.5-4.5 mIU/L). Thyroid ultrasound reveals a solid, hypoechoic nodule measuring 1.4 x 1.1 x 1.3 cm with multiple punctate microcalcifications and irregular margins; two ipsilateral jugular lymph nodes show rounded morphology and loss of fatty hila. What is the most appropriate next step in management?

A. Radioiodine (123I) uptake and scan to evaluate nodule functionality
B. Ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration (FNA) of the nodule and suspicious lymph nodes
C. Empirical levothyroxine therapy to suppress TSH and reassess nodule size in 6 months
D. Immediate core needle biopsy of the right lobe under general anesthesia

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration (FNA) of the nodule and suspicious lymph nodes

In a euthyroid child with a solitary solid thyroid nodule >=1 cm displaying high-risk sonographic features (hypoechoic, microcalcifications, irregular borders) and suspicious cervical lymphadenopathy, ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration (FNA) of both the primary nodule and the suspicious lymph nodes is the gold-standard diagnostic step. 123I scan is indicated only if TSH is suppressed. Levothyroxine suppression is ineffective and delays diagnosis. FNA is preferred over core needle biopsy.

## Authored MCQ

A 14-year-old male undergoes total thyroidectomy and central neck dissection for a 2.5-cm papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC). Final pathology reveals multifocal PTC with extrathyroidal extension and 6 of 11 central lymph nodes positive for metastatic carcinoma. Staging chest CT reveals multiple 2-3 mm bilateral pulmonary nodules with radioiodine avidity. Which postoperative adjuvant therapy is indicated?

A. External beam radiotherapy to the neck and thorax
B. Radioactive iodine (131I) therapy following recombinant human TSH (rhTSH) or thyroid hormone withdrawal
C. Systemic multi-agent cytotoxic chemotherapy (doxorubicin and cisplatin)
D. Observation without levothyroxine suppression

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Radioactive iodine (131I) therapy following recombinant human TSH (rhTSH) or thyroid hormone withdrawal

In pediatric differentiated thyroid carcinoma with high-risk features (extrathyroidal extension, multiple lymph node metastases, distant pulmonary metastases), radioactive iodine (131I) ablation and treatment is the established standard adjuvant therapy. Pediatric pulmonary metastases from PTC are overwhelmingly radioiodine-avid and respond favorably to 131I. Chemotherapy and external beam radiation are ineffective and contraindicated.

## Authored MCQ

A newborn infant is found on genetic testing to carry a de novo heterozygous RET proto-oncogene germline mutation (M918T in exon 16), diagnostic of Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia type 2B (MEN2B). When is prophylactic total thyroidectomy recommended to prevent metastatic medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC)?

A. Within the first year of life (optimally in the first months of life)
B. At age 5 to 6 years before entering elementary school
C. At the onset of spontaneous puberty (Tanner stage 2)
D. Only after serum calcitonin rises above 100 pg/mL

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Within the first year of life (optimally in the first months of life)

MEN2B caused by the RET M918T mutation confers the highest risk category ('highest risk / ATA-HST') for medullary thyroid carcinoma. MTC in MEN2B develops within the first year of life and exhibits extremely virulent, early metastatic spread to lymph nodes and liver. American Thyroid Association guidelines recommend prophylactic total thyroidectomy within the first year of life, preferably in the first months of life.

## Authored MCQ

What is the targeted postoperative serum TSH suppression level for a 13-year-old child who has undergone total thyroidectomy for high-risk papillary thyroid carcinoma with gross extrathyroidal extension and lung metastases?

A. TSH maintained in the upper normal range (2.5-4.5 mIU/L)
B. TSH suppressed to <0.1 mIU/L
C. TSH maintained at 1.0-2.0 mIU/L
D. TSH elevated above 30 mIU/L to stimulate residual tissue differentiation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

TSH suppressed to <0.1 mIU/L

In high-risk pediatric differentiated thyroid carcinoma (persistent metastatic disease, extensive nodal involvement, gross extrathyroidal invasion), TSH acts as a trophic growth factor for follicular cells. Guidelines recommend aggressive TSH suppression to <0.1 mIU/L using supra-physiological levothyroxine dosing, while monitoring for iatrogenic thyrotoxicosis and maintaining bone density.

## Authored MCQ

During surveillance of a pediatric patient 1 year after total thyroidectomy and 131I ablation for papillary thyroid cancer, serum thyroglobulin (Tg) is reported as undetectable (<0.1 ng/mL). However, anti-thyroglobulin antibodies (TgAb) are markedly elevated at 450 IU/mL (reference: <20 IU/mL). How should the clinician interpret these laboratory results?

A. The undetectable Tg proves complete cure and absence of any residual thyroid tissue
B. The undetectable Tg result may be falsely low due to antibody interference in immunometric assays
C. High TgAb levels prove that the original tumor was an anaplastic carcinoma
D. Elevated TgAb levels indicate concurrent Graves' disease of the remnant

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

The undetectable Tg result may be falsely low due to antibody interference in immunometric assays

In immunometric assays (sandwich ICMA), endogenous anti-thyroglobulin antibodies (TgAb) bind to serum thyroglobulin and sterically hinder assay antibody binding, producing falsely low or undetectable measured Tg values. Whenever TgAb is positive, a low/undetectable Tg cannot be relied upon as evidence of tumor absence; trending serial TgAb titers (rising titers indicate recurrence) and radioiodine/ultrasound surveillance are required.
