## Authored MCQ

A 3-year-old girl is evaluated for an acute episode of painless vaginal bleeding. Physical examination reveals unilateral Tanner stage 3 breast development, no pubic hair, and a large, jagged light-brown macule on the right buttock with irregular 'coast of Maine' borders that terminates abruptly at the sacral midline. Pelvic ultrasound reveals a solitary 4-cm cystic lesion in the right ovary with an endometrial stripe; the left ovary is normal. Serum estradiol is 165 pg/mL, and baseline and stimulated LH are <0.1 IU/L. What is the diagnosis?

A. McCune-Albright syndrome (MAS)
B. Neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1)
C. Granulosa cell ovarian tumor
D. Idiopathic central precocious puberty

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

McCune-Albright syndrome (MAS)

This patient exhibits classic features of McCune-Albright syndrome (MAS): peripheral precocious puberty (recurrent autonomous estrogen-secreting ovarian cysts with vaginal bleeding, suppressed LH), paired with a pathognomonic large café-au-lait macule displaying jagged 'coast of Maine' borders that respects the midline. MAS is caused by a somatic activating GNAS mutation.

## Authored MCQ

A 10-year-old girl with mosaic activating GNAS mutation and polyostotic fibrous dysplasia presents with chronic bone pain and lower-extremity fatigue. Plain radiographs reveal expansile ground-glass lesions involving the skull, pelvis, and both femurs. Laboratory evaluation reveals: serum calcium 9.2 mg/dL, serum phosphate 1.9 mg/dL (low; reference: 4.0-5.8 mg/dL), alkaline phosphatase 890 U/L, and TmP/GFR 1.6 mg/dL (severely reduced). Serum intact FGF23 is elevated at 95 pg/mL. What is the mechanism of her hypophosphatemia?

A. Direct oversecretion of intact FGF23 by dysplastic osteogenic cells within the fibrous dysplasia bone lesions
B. Parathyroid adenoma secreting excess parathyroid hormone
C. Proximal renal tubular necrosis caused by hypercalciuria
D. Defective intestinal phosphate absorption

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Direct oversecretion of intact FGF23 by dysplastic osteogenic cells within the fibrous dysplasia bone lesions

In patients with extensive polyostotic fibrous dysplasia in McCune-Albright syndrome, the dysplastic osteogenic stromal cells oversecrete intact fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF23). Circulating FGF23 acts on the renal proximal tubule to downregulate sodium-phosphate cotransporters, producing profound renal phosphate wasting, severe hypophosphatemia, and secondary osteomalacia that exacerbates bone pain and fractures.

## Authored MCQ

A 5-year-old girl with polyostotic fibrous dysplasia and coast-of-Maine café-au-lait pigmentation suffers from recurrent autonomous ovarian cysts and multiple episodes of vaginal bleeding. Treatment with depot leuprolide (GnRH agonist) for 6 months produced no reduction in bleeding or cyst frequency. What is the recommended pharmacological therapy to prevent recurrent estrogenic bleeding?

A. Oral letrozole (third-generation aromatase inhibitor) 2.5 mg daily
B. Doubling the dose of depot leuprolide
C. Intravenous bisphosphonate therapy
D. Bilateral total oophorectomy

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Oral letrozole (third-generation aromatase inhibitor) 2.5 mg daily

In young girls with McCune-Albright syndrome, precocious puberty is peripheral (gonadotropin-independent), driven by autonomous Gs-alpha activation in ovarian follicular cells. GnRH agonists are ineffective because pituitary gonadotropins are already suppressed. First-line medical therapy is oral letrozole (2.5 mg daily), a potent aromatase inhibitor that blocks the conversion of androgens to estrogens in the cysts, halting vaginal bleeding and slowing skeletal maturation.

## Authored MCQ

A 14-year-old male with mosaic GNAS activation and craniofacial fibrous dysplasia is noted to have accelerating head circumference, coarse facial features, and expanding jaw lesions. Laboratory testing reveals elevated IGF-1 and unsuppressed growth hormone on oral glucose loading. Why is growth hormone excess particularly dangerous in children with mosaic GNAS activation and fibrous dysplasia?

A. Excess GH and IGF-1 directly stimulate rapid expansion of craniofacial fibrous dysplasia lesions, causing visual loss and hearing impairment
B. GH excess leads to spontaneous malignant transformation of fibrous dysplasia into osteosarcoma in >80% of patients
C. GH excess causes acute rupture of hepatic adenomas
D. GH excess triggers immediate renal failure

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Excess GH and IGF-1 directly stimulate rapid expansion of craniofacial fibrous dysplasia lesions, causing visual loss and hearing impairment

Growth hormone excess occurs in 20-30% of MAS patients due to somatotrope hyperplasia. GH and IGF-1 are potent mitogenic and osteotropic factors that accelerate the rapid growth and expansion of fibrous dysplasia lesions, particularly in the craniofacial bones. This expansile growth can compress the optic canals and auditory canals, leading to permanent optic nerve compression, irreversible blindness, and severe deafness. Aggressive medical management of GH excess is imperative.

## Authored MCQ

A pregnant woman who was diagnosed in childhood with mosaic GNAS activating syndrome (MAS) asks her genetic counselor about the risk of transmitting the condition to her unborn child. What is the accurate recurrence risk?

A. Essentially 0% risk of transmission, because McCune-Albright syndrome is caused exclusively by somatic postzygotic mutations
B. 50% risk, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance
C. 25% risk, consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance
D. 100% risk if the fetus is female

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Essentially 0% risk of transmission, because McCune-Albright syndrome is caused exclusively by somatic postzygotic mutations

McCune-Albright syndrome is caused exclusively by postzygotic somatic mosaic mutations in GNAS that arise spontaneously during embryonic development. Because the mutation is postzygotic and somatic, it is not present in the germline of all cells, and germline transmission to offspring has never been documented. The recurrence risk for the offspring of an affected patient is virtually zero.
