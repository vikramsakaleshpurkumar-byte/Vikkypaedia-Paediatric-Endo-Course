## Authored MCQ

An 18-year-old male with childhood-onset panhypopituitarism secondary to a large craniopharyngioma resected at age 8 has reached epiphyseal fusion (height velocity <1.5 cm/year). He is on stable replacement with levothyroxine, hydrocortisone, desmopressin, and testosterone. Somatropin is withheld for 6 weeks; his repeat serum IGF-I is 45 ng/mL (SDS -3.6). What is the appropriate next step regarding growth hormone management?

A. Resume somatropin at an adult transition starting dose without requiring provocative stimulation testing
B. Perform an insulin tolerance test to confirm severe growth hormone deficiency
C. Discontinue growth hormone permanently because linear growth is completed
D. Perform an oral glucose tolerance test to evaluate for residual somatotroph hypersecretion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Resume somatropin at an adult transition starting dose without requiring provocative stimulation testing

Patients with multiple pituitary hormone deficiencies (≥3) secondary to structural lesions with a low IGF-I (SDS <-2.0) have an extremely high probability of permanent severe GHD; consensus guidelines state they do not require provocative testing.

## Authored MCQ

A 19-year-old female with childhood isolated idiopathic growth hormone deficiency reaches final height. She stops somatropin for 2 months. She is healthy, takes an oral combined ethinyl estradiol/levonorgestrel contraceptive, and has a normal BMI. Her IGF-I is at -1.4 SDS. What is the most appropriate next step to evaluate whether she requires adult GH continuation?

A. Perform a validated provocative growth hormone stimulation test such as the insulin tolerance test or glucagon test
B. Resume pediatric-dose somatropin (30 mcg/kg/day) immediately based on the borderline IGF-I
C. Switch to oral mecasermin monotherapy for lifelong metabolic support
D. Perform a cranial MRI scan and prescribe growth hormone only if a microadenoma is seen

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Perform a validated provocative growth hormone stimulation test such as the insulin tolerance test or glucagon test

In childhood isolated idiopathic GHD, up to 70-80% of patients demonstrate normal somatotroph reserve when re-tested after puberty. A provocative test (ITT or glucagon) is mandatory before diagnosing permanent adult GHD.

## Authored MCQ

A 22-year-old female with confirmed persistent adult growth hormone deficiency is on stable somatropin replacement (0.3 mg daily) with an IGF-I of 0.0 SDS. She begins taking an oral combined estrogen-progestin contraceptive. Three months later, she reports fatigue, and her repeat serum IGF-I has dropped to -2.1 SDS despite strict adherence. What is the mechanism explaining this decline?

A. First-pass hepatic metabolism of oral estrogen induces SOCS2, inhibiting hepatic GH-mediated IGF-I generation
B. Estrogen binds directly to circulating somatropin, causing rapid renal clearance
C. Oral progestins directly destroy pituitary somatotroph secretory granules
D. Estrogen alters laboratory antibody recognition in automated IGF-I immunoassays

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

First-pass hepatic metabolism of oral estrogen induces SOCS2, inhibiting hepatic GH-mediated IGF-I generation

Oral estrogens pass directly through the liver via the portal vein, upregulating suppressor of cytokine signaling 2 (SOCS2) and inhibiting Jak2/STAT5 signaling downstream of the hepatic GH receptor, attenuating IGF-I generation. Switching to transdermal estrogen or increasing the somatropin dose overcomes this effect.

## Authored MCQ

A 24-year-old male with persistent severe adult GHD starts somatropin at 0.8 mg daily (an inappropriately high starting dose). Within 3 weeks, he develops bilateral wrist paresthesias in the median nerve distribution, finger stiffness, and pitting pedal edema. What is the pathophysiological cause of these symptoms?

A. Growth hormone-induced sodium and water retention causing elevated tissue interstitial turgor and carpal tunnel compression
B. Autoimmune demyelinating peripheral polyneuropathy triggered by recombinant protein
C. Acute skeletal overgrowth of the carpal bones causing bony canal stenosis
D. Development of acute hypokalemic periodic paralysis

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Growth hormone-induced sodium and water retention causing elevated tissue interstitial turgor and carpal tunnel compression

Growth hormone stimulates renal tubular sodium reabsorption and extracellular fluid expansion. In adults, excessive starting doses commonly trigger fluid retention, soft tissue swelling, arthralgias, and compression of the median nerve in the carpal tunnel.

## Authored MCQ

Which metabolic parameter has been demonstrated to improve consistently when growth hormone replacement is maintained during adulthood in patients with documented severe persistent GHD?

A. Increase in lean body mass, reduction in visceral fat, and improvement in lumbar spine bone mineral density
B. Complete reversal of central diabetes insipidus allowing cessation of desmopressin
C. Permanent cure of primary panhypopituitarism with spontaneous recovery of other axes
D. Reduction in total blood volume and reduction in left ventricular posterior wall thickness

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Increase in lean body mass, reduction in visceral fat, and improvement in lumbar spine bone mineral density

Adult GH replacement consistently produces favorable somatic and metabolic changes: increased lean body mass, decreased visceral and total body fat, improved bone mineral density accrual, and enhanced quality-of-life scores.
