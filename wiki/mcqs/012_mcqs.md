## Authored MCQ

An 11-year-old girl is brought for evaluation of rapid weight gain. Over the past 2 years, her BMI percentile increased from the 60th to the 98th percentile, while her linear height velocity plummeted from 5.5 cm/year to 1.8 cm/year (height dropping from the 50th to the 10th percentile). She has plethoric moon facies, dark purplish abdominal striae measuring 1.5 cm in width, and a blood pressure of 136/88 mmHg. What is the most critical feature differentiating her condition from simple exogenous obesity?

A. Severe deceleration of linear growth velocity in the presence of advancing weight gain
B. Elevated fasting serum insulin concentrations
C. Development of mild acanthosis nigricans in skin folds
D. Presence of increased subcutaneous adipose tissue over the triceps

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Severe deceleration of linear growth velocity in the presence of advancing weight gain

Simple nutritional obesity stimulates linear growth and bone age maturation, so children are tall for their age. Conversely, Cushing syndrome uniquely combines rapid weight gain with dramatic growth deceleration and bone age delay due to the catabolic effects of glucocorticoids on the growth plate.

## Authored MCQ

A 13-year-old boy has confirmed pathological hypercortisolemia based on elevated 24-hour urinary free cortisol (4-fold above normal) and elevated late-night salivary cortisol. His 08:00 h plasma ACTH is measured at 2.4 pg/mL (reference 10–60 pg/mL, with undetectable repeated value <5 pg/mL). What is the appropriate next diagnostic step?

A. High-resolution dedicated contrast-enhanced adrenal CT or MRI scan
B. Pituitary MRI with dynamic gadolinium enhancement
C. Bilateral inferior petrosal sinus sampling with CRH stimulation
D. High-resolution chest CT scan to evaluate for bronchial carcinoid tumor

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

High-resolution dedicated contrast-enhanced adrenal CT or MRI scan

A suppressed plasma ACTH (<5 pg/mL) indicates ACTH-independent Cushing syndrome, pointing directly to primary autonomous adrenal pathology (adenoma, carcinoma, PPNAD). Imaging of the adrenal glands is the mandatory next step.

## Authored MCQ

A 14-year-old boy with severe Cushing syndrome has elevated 24-hour UFC and a plasma ACTH of 65 pg/mL. Pituitary MRI demonstrates no visible focal adenoma. A high-dose dexamethasone suppression test produces a 75% reduction in morning cortisol, and peripheral CRH administration elicits a 95% rise in ACTH. What diagnostic test should be performed before proceeding with neurosurgical exploration?

A. Bilateral inferior petrosal sinus sampling (BIPSS) with CRH stimulation to confirm central ACTH gradient and lateralization
B. Total bilateral adrenalectomy as first-line definitive management
C. Lifelong high-dose metyrapone monotherapy without further testing
D. Abdominal ultrasound to look for ovarian rest tumors

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Bilateral inferior petrosal sinus sampling (BIPSS) with CRH stimulation to confirm central ACTH gradient and lateralization

When ACTH-dependent Cushing syndrome is diagnosed but pituitary MRI is normal or equivocal (<6 mm lesion), BIPSS with CRH stimulation is the gold standard to prove a pituitary source (central-to-peripheral ACTH ratio ≥2 baseline or ≥3 post-CRH) and aid inter-sinus lateralization.

## Authored MCQ

A 12-year-old girl undergoes successful transsphenoidal selective adenomectomy for a 4 mm corticotroph microadenoma. On postoperative day 2, she feels weak and nauseated. Laboratory testing reveals morning serum cortisol 0.6 mcg/dL (reference 5–20 mcg/dL) and ACTH 4.2 pg/mL. What is the clinical significance of this finding?

A. Indicates complete surgical resection and cure, requiring temporary hydrocortisone replacement therapy
B. Represents acute surgical failure and indicates immediate re-exploration of the sella
C. Reflects permanent panhypopituitarism requiring permanent desmopressin and levothyroxine
D. Indicates transformation of the corticotroph tumor into an aggressive silent carcinoma

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Indicates complete surgical resection and cure, requiring temporary hydrocortisone replacement therapy

Profound transient hypocortisolemia (morning cortisol <1-2 mcg/dL) following transsphenoidal adenomectomy is the hallmark of surgical cure. It reflects suppression of the normal surrounding hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis by prolonged prior hypercortisolemia. Temporary hydrocortisone replacement is required until the HPA axis recovers.

## Authored MCQ

A 10-year-old girl is evaluated for Cushing syndrome. Physical examination reveals pigmented lentigines on her eyelids, lips, and oral mucosa. Echocardiogram demonstrates a benign right atrial myxoma. Endocrine testing reveals ACTH-independent Cushing syndrome. What gene defect is responsible for this condition?

A. Inactivating mutation in PRKAR1A encoding the protein kinase A regulatory subunit type 1A
B. Activating mutation in the GNAS gene encoding G-alpha-s
C. Loss-of-function mutation in the MEN1 gene encoding menin
D. Germline mutation in the RET proto-oncogene

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Inactivating mutation in PRKAR1A encoding the protein kinase A regulatory subunit type 1A

Carney complex is an autosomal dominant syndrome caused by inactivating mutations in PRKAR1A, leading to constitutive PKA activation, cardiac myxomas, spotty skin pigmentation (lentigines), and primary pigmented nodular adrenocortical disease (PPNAD).
