## Authored MCQ

A full-term newborn is found to have ambiguous external genitalia with a 2.5-cm phallus, severe chordee, a single perineal urogenital orifice, and completely fused labioscrotal folds with no palpable gonads in the folds or inguinal canals. Pelvic ultrasound reveals a distinct uterus and normal upper vaginal cavity. Blood pressure is 68/42 mmHg. Which laboratory test is most critical to obtain immediately?

A. Serum 17-hydroxyprogesterone (17-OHP) and serum electrolytes
B. Serum dihydrotestosterone (DHT) concentration
C. Urinary gonadotropin peptides
D. Serum Anti-Müllerian Hormone (AMH)

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Serum 17-hydroxyprogesterone (17-OHP) and serum electrolytes

A newborn presenting with atypical genitalia, bilateral impalpable gonads, and a documented uterus on ultrasound is a virilized 46,XX infant with congenital adrenal hyperplasia (21-hydroxylase deficiency) until proven otherwise. Immediate measurement of 17-OHP and serum electrolytes (sodium, potassium) is mandatory because life-threatening adrenal salt-wasting crisis can develop rapidly.

## Authored MCQ

A 16-year-old phenotypic female presents for evaluation of primary amenorrhea. Physical examination shows excellent Tanner stage 5 breast development, but completely absent pubic and axillary hair (Tanner stage 1). Pelvic examination reveals a blind-ending vagina measuring 2.5 cm in depth. Pelvic ultrasound confirms the absence of a uterus and ovaries. Bilateral masses are palpated in the inguinal canals. Karyotype is 46,XY. Serum testosterone is in the normal adult male range. What is the diagnosis?

A. Complete Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome (CAIS)
B. Müllerian agenesis (Mayer-Rokitansky-Küster-Hauser syndrome)
C. 5-alpha-reductase type 2 deficiency
D. Turner syndrome (45,X)

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Complete Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome (CAIS)

Complete Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome (CAIS) is an X-linked recessive disorder caused by inactivating mutations in the androgen receptor (AR). In 46,XY individuals, testes produce AMH (causing complete Müllerian regression: absent uterus/upper vagina) and testosterone. Because all androgen receptors are completely non-functional, external genitalia develop as phenotypic female, and pubic/axillary hair does not develop. At puberty, high testosterone is peripherally aromatized to estrogen, inducing normal female breast development.

## Authored MCQ

A 14-year-old individual raised as a female in a rural community presents with rapid virilization at puberty: deepening of the voice, marked enlargement of the clitoral phallus into an 8-cm penis, development of facial and body hair, and progressive muscular development. There is no breast development. Karyotype is 46,XY. Physical exam reveals a bifid scrotum containing palpable 12-mL testes and a perineal hypospadias. Baseline testosterone is 650 ng/dL, but serum dihydrotestosterone (DHT) is 14 ng/dL (testosterone-to-DHT ratio >40). What enzyme is deficient?

A. Steroid 5-alpha-reductase type 2 (SRD5A2)
B. Aromatase (CYP19A1)
C. 17-beta-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase type 3 (HSD17B3)
D. P450 oxidoreductase (POR)

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Steroid 5-alpha-reductase type 2 (SRD5A2)

5-alpha-reductase type 2 deficiency is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by mutations in SRD5A2. Impaired conversion of testosterone to DHT in utero impairs external genital virilization, so 46,XY infants are often assigned female at birth. At puberty, massive LH-driven testosterone production virilizes tissues directly through the androgen receptor, causing penile growth, muscle development, and voice deepening without gynecomastia. A markedly elevated testosterone-to-DHT ratio (>20-30:1) is diagnostic.

## Authored MCQ

A newborn infant presents with asymmetric genitalia: a fully descended normal testis in the right hemiscrotum, and an empty left labioscrotal fold. Laparoscopy reveals a dysgenetic streak gonad and a rudimentary fallopian tube and hemi-uterus on the left side, and a normal testis and vas deferens on the right side. Karyotype is 45,X/46,XY. What is the diagnosis and the most critical oncological recommendation?

A. Mixed gonadal dysgenesis; surgical excision of the dysgenetic streak gonad due to high gonadoblastoma risk
B. Complete androgen insensitivity syndrome; retain gonads indefinitely
C. Klinefelter syndrome; initiation of testosterone at birth
D. Congenital adrenal hyperplasia; high-dose hydrocortisone monotherapy

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Mixed gonadal dysgenesis; surgical excision of the dysgenetic streak gonad due to high gonadoblastoma risk

Mixed gonadal dysgenesis (MGD) is typically associated with a 45,X/46,XY mosaic karyotype. It features asymmetric internal and external genitalia: a dysgenetic testis on one side with Wolffian structures, and a streak gonad on the contralateral side with persistent Müllerian structures (hemi-uterus). Intra-abdominal dysgenetic gonads containing Y-chromosomal material carry a 15-40% lifetime risk of malignant transformation into gonadoblastoma or dysgerminoma. Immediate surgical removal of the dysgenetic streak gonad is strongly recommended.

What is the recommended modern management approach regarding immediate sex assignment and irreversible genitoplasty in a newborn with ambiguous genitalia?

A. Perform immediate emergency feminizing genitoplasty within the first 48 hours to relieve parental anxiety
B. Withhold definitive gender assignment until multidisciplinary diagnostic evaluation is complete, and defer non-emergency cosmetic genitoplasty until the child can participate in decision-making
C. Assign male sex automatically to all infants with a phallus >1.5 cm
D. Perform mandatory bilateral gonadectomy in all infants with 46,XX DSD

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Withhold definitive gender assignment until multidisciplinary diagnostic evaluation is complete, and defer non-emergency cosmetic genitoplasty until the child can participate in decision-making

Modern international consensus guidelines on DSD emphasize: (1) withholding definitive gender assignment until rapid comprehensive multidisciplinary evaluation (karyotype, imaging, steroid profiles) is synthesized; (2) fully engaging parents with specialized psychological counseling; and (3) deferring non-urgent, irreversible cosmetic genitoplasty until the patient is old enough to provide informed assent or consent, except for surgeries required to establish unobstructed urinary drainage.
