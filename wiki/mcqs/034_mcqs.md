## Authored MCQ

A 9-year-old boy presents with intense polydipsia, drinking 6 liters of ice water daily, and waking 4 to 5 times per night to urinate. Serum sodium is 147 mEq/L, serum osmolality is 304 mOsm/kg, and random urine osmolality is 95 mOsm/kg. Subcutaneous desmopressin (DDAVP 1 mcg) is administered. Two hours later, repeat urine osmolality rises to 680 mOsm/kg with a sharp decline in urine output. Sellar MRI demonstrates complete absence of the normal T1 posterior pituitary bright spot and thickening of the pituitary stalk. What is the diagnosis?

A. Arginine Vasopressin Deficiency (AVP-D / central DI)
B. Arginine Vasopressin Resistance (AVP-R / nephrogenic DI)
C. Primary psychogenic polydipsia
D. Solute diuresis secondary to type 1 diabetes

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Arginine Vasopressin Deficiency (AVP-D / central DI)

This patient has classic Arginine Vasopressin Deficiency (AVP-D; central DI): hypertonic polyuria with low baseline urine osmolality (<100 mOsm/kg) in the presence of hyperosmolality (>300 mOsm/kg), followed by a >100% surge in urine osmolality (>600 mOsm/kg) after desmopressin administration. Absence of the T1 posterior pituitary bright spot and stalk thickening on MRI confirm hypothalamic-neurohypophyseal pathology (such as germinoma or Langerhans cell histiocytosis).

## Authored MCQ

A 12-year-old girl undergoes gross total resection of a suprasellar craniopharyngioma. On postoperative day 1, her urine output surges to 6 mL/kg/hour, urine specific gravity is 1.002, and serum sodium is 149 mEq/L. She is started on IV desmopressin. On postoperative day 4, while still receiving desmopressin, her urine output abruptly plummets, and serum sodium drops sharply to 121 mEq/L. What physiological phase of the post-hypophysectomy triphasic response has occurred?

A. Phase 2: Transient SIADH secondary to uncontrolled AVP release from degenerating hypothalamic neurons
B. Phase 1: Acute vasopressin deficiency due to surgical axon shock
C. Phase 3: Permanent neurohypophyseal fibrosis and AVP deficiency
D. Acute postoperative adrenal crisis

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Phase 2: Transient SIADH secondary to uncontrolled AVP release from degenerating hypothalamic neurons

The triphasic response occurs following pituitary stalk or hypothalamic neurosurgery. Phase 1 (days 1-2) is acute DI due to mechanical axon shock. Phase 2 (days 3-7) is transient SIADH: dying, degenerating magnocellular axons unregulatedly release their entire remaining stores of AVP into the circulation, precipitous oliguria and severe dilutional hyponatremia if desmopressin or fluid intake is not halted. Phase 3 (day 8 onward) is permanent AVP-D after all stores are exhausted.

## Authored MCQ

A 3-month-old infant boy presents with recurrent unexplained fevers, vomiting, severe dehydration, and failure to thrive. Laboratory evaluation shows serum sodium 168 mEq/L, serum osmolality 342 mOsm/kg, and simultaneous urine osmolality 110 mOsm/kg. Subcutaneous desmopressin 2 mcg is administered; two hours later, urine osmolality is unchanged at 115 mOsm/kg, and baseline plasma copeptin is markedly elevated at 48 pmol/L. What is the most likely genetic diagnosis?

A. X-linked Arginine Vasopressin Resistance (AVPR2 mutation)
B. Autosomal dominant AVP deficiency (AVP-neurophysin II mutation)
C. Congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CYP21A2 mutation)
D. Bartter syndrome type 1 (SLC12A1 mutation)

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

X-linked Arginine Vasopressin Resistance (AVPR2 mutation)

This infant has congenital Arginine Vasopressin Resistance (AVP-R; nephrogenic DI), which is X-linked recessive in >90% of cases due to loss-of-function AVPR2 mutations. Hallmark features are extreme hypernatremic dehydration with dilute urine that fails to concentrate following desmopressin, accompanied by high baseline copeptin (>21.4 pmol/L) reflecting appropriate endogenous hypothalamic AVP hypersecretion acting on a completely resistant collecting duct.

## Authored MCQ

What is the paradoxical mechanism by which thiazide diuretics (e.g., hydrochlorothiazide) reduce urine volume in patients with AVP resistance (nephrogenic DI)?

A. Inhibition of the distal tubule NaCl cotransporter induces mild volume contraction, triggering compensatory proximal tubular reabsorption of sodium and water
B. Direct binding and activation of renal collecting duct V2 receptors
C. Inhibition of renal medullary aquaporin-2 degradation
D. Direct stimulation of hypothalamic vasopressin synthesis

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Inhibition of the distal tubule NaCl cotransporter induces mild volume contraction, triggering compensatory proximal tubular reabsorption of sodium and water

Thiazide diuretics inhibit the Na+-Cl- cotransporter in the distal convoluted tubule, producing mild natriuresis and extracellular volume depletion. In response to hypovolemia, the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system and glomerulotubular balance markedly increase sodium and water reabsorption in the proximal tubule. This reduces the total volume of fluid delivered to the collecting ducts, reducing 24-hour urine output by 30-50% in patients with nephrogenic DI.

## Authored MCQ

A 7-year-old child with Langerhans cell histiocytosis (LCH) and confirmed AVP deficiency is maintained on oral desmopressin 0.1 mg twice daily. The mother is instructed to administer the evening dose only after observing an episode of breakthrough urination. Why is this breakthrough period mandatory?

A. To ensure that water intoxication and life-threatening dilutional hyponatremia do not occur from chronic antidiuresis
B. To prevent rapid development of tachyphylaxis and drug tolerance
C. To avoid medication accumulation in the biliary tract
D. To stimulate endogenous posterior pituitary vasopressin regeneration

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

To ensure that water intoxication and life-threatening dilutional hyponatremia do not occur from chronic antidiuresis

Desmopressin produces potent antidiuresis. If consecutive doses are given on a rigid schedule without allowing the drug to wear off, free water intake will exceed urinary excretion, leading to progressive water retention, expansion of intravascular volume, and severe dilutional hyponatremia with brain edema and fatal seizures. Allowing a period of breakthrough polyuria prior to redosing ensures that the patient clears excess free water.
