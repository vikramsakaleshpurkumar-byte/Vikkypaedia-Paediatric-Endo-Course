## Authored MCQ

A 9-day-old male infant born at 39 weeks of gestation is recalled after newborn screening reveals an elevated TSH of 74 mIU/L. Confirmatory serum testing demonstrates venous TSH >100 mIU/L and free T4 0.3 ng/dL (reference: 1.1-2.2 ng/dL). Examination shows an infant with a 1.5-cm posterior fontanel, mild macroglossia, and an umbilical hernia. Which of the following is the most appropriate initial pharmacological therapy?

A. Levothyroxine oral suspension at 2-5 mcg/kg/day administered with soy formula
B. Oral levothyroxine tablets crushed in breastmilk at 10-15 mcg/kg/day on an empty stomach
C. Intravenous liothyronine (triiodothyronine) bolus followed by maintenance L-T3
D. Observation for 4 weeks with repeat serum TSH before initiating therapy

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Oral levothyroxine tablets crushed in breastmilk at 10-15 mcg/kg/day on an empty stomach

Guidelines from the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and European Society for Paediatric Endocrinology (ESPE) mandate immediate treatment of confirmed primary congenital hypothyroidism with oral levothyroxine at 10-15 mcg/kg/day (typically 37.5-50 mcg/day). The tablet should be crushed in a few milliliters of water or breastmilk. Soy formula impairs absorption and should be avoided. Liothyronine is not standard initial therapy and causes erratic peaks.

## Authored MCQ

A 2-week-old female diagnosed with severe primary neonatal hypothyroidism undergoes diagnostic thyroid imaging. Radionuclide scintigraphy using technetium-99m pertechnetate demonstrates intense focal radioisotope uptake at the base of the tongue with complete absence of uptake in the lower anterior neck. What is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Athyreosis (complete thyroid agenesis)
B. Lingual thyroid (ectopic thyroid dysgenesis)
C. Thyroid dyshormonogenesis due to TPO gene mutation
D. Maternal antithyroid drug-induced goitrous hypothyroidism

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Lingual thyroid (ectopic thyroid dysgenesis)

Ectopic thyroid occurs when embryonic thyroid tissue fails to migrate normally along the thyroglossal duct tract from the foramen cecum to the normal pretracheal cervical position. Scintigraphy demonstrating focal tracer accumulation at the base of the tongue with no tracer in the lower neck is diagnostic of a lingual or sublingual ectopic thyroid, which represents ~60% of thyroid dysgenesis cases.

## Authored MCQ

A full-term infant with severe neonatal hypothyroidism is started on levothyroxine 12 mcg/kg/day at day 10 of life. Which of the following monitoring targets reflects optimal early therapy during the first 6 months of life?

A. Serum TSH maintained between 5 and 10 mIU/L with free T4 in the lower quartile of reference range
B. Serum free T4 rapidly normalized into the upper half of the normal reference range with TSH suppressed <0.01 mIU/L
C. Serum free T4 in the upper half of the reference range and serum TSH normalized <5 mIU/L within 2-4 weeks
D. Discontinuation of levothyroxine every 2 months to assess spontaneous endogenous thyroid recovery

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Serum free T4 in the upper half of the reference range and serum TSH normalized <5 mIU/L within 2-4 weeks

Treatment goals in congenital hypothyroidism emphasize restoring serum free T4 into the upper half of the normal age-specific reference interval (e.g., 1.4-2.3 ng/dL) within 1-2 weeks and lowering TSH to <5 mIU/L within 2-4 weeks. Over-suppression (TSH <0.01 mIU/L) carries risks of craniosynostosis and behavior disturbance, while under-treatment compromises brain development.

## Authored MCQ

An infant with sensorineural deafness and primary neonatal hypothyroidism is found to have an enlarged in situ goitrous thyroid on ultrasound. Scintigraphy demonstrates normal pertechnetate trapping, but rapid discharge of iodide occurs upon administration of sodium perchlorate (positive perchlorate discharge test >30%). What gene mutation is most likely responsible for this condition?

A. SLC26A4 (Pendrin)
B. PAX8 (Paired box gene 8)
C. TSHR (Thyroid-stimulating hormone receptor)
D. NKX2-1 (Thyroid transcription factor 1)

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

SLC26A4 (Pendrin)

Pendred syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by biallelic mutations in SLC26A4, which encodes pendrin, an anion exchanger involved in iodide transport at the apical follicular membrane. It is characterized by sensorineural hearing loss (associated with enlarged vestibular aqueducts on temporal bone CT), goitrous dyshormonogenesis, and an abnormal perchlorate discharge test reflecting impaired iodide organification.

## Authored MCQ

A female infant born at 39 weeks has an initial newborn screen revealing low total T4 with a normal TSH of 4.2 mIU/L. Repeat venous testing confirms low free T4 (0.6 ng/dL) with persistently normal TSH (3.1 mIU/L). Which of the following evaluations is most urgently indicated?

A. Diagnostic radioiodine uptake and perchlorate discharge test
B. Evaluation of the hypothalamic-pituitary axis for central hypopituitarism and cortisol deficiency
C. Measurement of maternal antithyroglobulin and anti-TPO antibodies
D. Observation without treatment until 6 months of age

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Evaluation of the hypothalamic-pituitary axis for central hypopituitarism and cortisol deficiency

Low free T4 in the presence of an inappropriately normal or low TSH indicates central (hypothalamic-pituitary) hypothyroidism. Central hypothyroidism is rarely isolated and is frequently associated with congenital hypopituitarism (multiple pituitary hormone deficiencies), including potentially fatal central adrenal insufficiency (ACTH deficiency) and growth hormone deficiency. An urgent pituitary axis evaluation and pituitary MRI are required. If hydrocortisone deficiency is present, glucocorticoid replacement must precede or accompany levothyroxine to prevent acute adrenal crisis.
