## Authored MCQ

A 10-year-old girl is evaluated for short stature, rounded facies, and obesity. Physical exam reveals shortening of the 4th and 5th digits of both hands; when she makes a fist, dimples appear in place of the 4th and 5th knuckles (Archibald sign). Laboratory evaluation demonstrates: serum calcium 7.2 mg/dL (normal: 8.8-10.2 mg/dL), phosphate 6.8 mg/dL (normal: 4.0-5.8 mg/dL), intact PTH 380 pg/mL (normal: 15-65 pg/mL), TSH 9.2 mIU/L (normal: 0.5-4.5 mIU/L), and normal free T4. What is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Pseudohypoparathyroidism type 1A (PHP-1A)
B. Pseudo-pseudohypoparathyroidism (PPHP)
C. Primary hypoparathyroidism
D. Nutritional vitamin D deficiency rickets

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Pseudohypoparathyroidism type 1A (PHP-1A)

This patient exhibits the classic triad of Pseudohypoparathyroidism type 1A (PHP-1A): Albright Hereditary Osteodystrophy physical phenotype (short stature, obesity, round facies, brachydactyly with Archibald sign), renal PTH resistance (hypocalcemia, hyperphosphatemia, markedly elevated PTH), and subclinical TSH resistance (elevated TSH). PHP-1A is caused by maternal inheritance of an inactivating GNAS mutation.

## Authored MCQ

A 12-year-old boy presents with short stature, round face, and shortened 4th and 5th metacarpals identical to his sister who has PHP-1A. However, his laboratory evaluation reveals: serum calcium 9.4 mg/dL (normal: 8.8-10.2 mg/dL), phosphate 4.2 mg/dL (normal: 4.0-5.8 mg/dL), intact PTH 32 pg/mL (normal: 15-65 pg/mL), and TSH 2.1 mIU/L. From whom did this boy inherit the GNAS mutation?

A. From his father (paternal inheritance)
B. From his mother (maternal inheritance)
C. A de novo somatic postzygotic mutation in bone progenitor cells
D. Mitochondrial maternal transmission

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

From his father (paternal inheritance)

Pseudo-pseudohypoparathyroidism (PPHP) is caused by paternal inheritance of an inactivating GNAS mutation. Because Gs-alpha expression in the renal proximal tubule and thyroid thyrotrope is derived almost exclusively from the maternal allele, paternal mutations leave the functioning maternal allele intact, preserving normal renal and thyroid hormone signaling (normal calcium, phosphate, PTH, TSH). However, because growth plates express Gs-alpha biallelically, Gs-alpha haploinsufficiency causes AHO physical features.

## Authored MCQ

What active metabolite must be prescribed to correct hypocalcemia and lower parathyroid hormone levels in a child with PHP type 1A (Albright hereditary osteodystrophy with hormone resistance)?

A. Calcitriol (1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D)
B. Ergocalciferol (vitamin D2)
C. Cholecalciferol (vitamin D3)
D. Recombinant human PTH (teriparatide)

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Calcitriol (1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D)

In PHP-1A, proximal tubular resistance to PTH impairs PTH-stimulated 1-alpha-hydroxylase (CYP27B1) activity, resulting in deficient synthesis of active 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (calcitriol). Unhydroxylated native vitamin D (D2 or D3) is ineffective. Calcitriol bypasses the enzymatic block, directly restoring intestinal calcium absorption, normalizing serum calcium, and suppressing parathyroid PTH overdrive.

## Authored MCQ

A 16-year-old girl with PHP-1A has well-controlled calcium and phosphate on calcitriol and calcium carbonate. However, her height has plateaued at the 1st percentile with growth velocity <2 cm/year. Detailed endocrine testing demonstrates impaired GH peak on clonidine/arginine stimulation tests with low IGF-1. What is the mechanism of growth hormone deficiency in PHP-1A?

A. Pituitary somatotrope resistance to growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) due to maternal Gs-alpha deficiency
B. Structural destruction of the anterior pituitary gland by ectopic ossifications
C. Autoimmune lymphocytic hypophysitis
D. Accelerated hepatic clearance of circulating growth hormone

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Pituitary somatotrope resistance to growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) due to maternal Gs-alpha deficiency

Pituitary somatotropes express Gs-alpha predominantly from the maternal allele. In PHP-1A, maternally inherited GNAS mutations cause end-organ resistance to GHRH, which signals through Gs-alpha to stimulate GH synthesis and secretion. GHRH resistance causes secondary growth hormone deficiency, contributing significantly to short stature. Recombinant human GH therapy improves linear growth in these patients.

## Authored MCQ

A 9-year-old boy presents with hypocalcemia (7.4 mg/dL), hyperphosphatemia (6.2 mg/dL), and markedly elevated PTH (290 pg/mL). Thyroid function tests show mildly elevated TSH (6.5 mIU/L). On physical examination, he has normal height (50th percentile), normal body weight, no brachydactyly, and no subcutaneous ossifications. Genetic analysis reveals loss of methylation at the GNAS exon A/B differentially methylated region on the maternal allele. What is the diagnosis?

A. Pseudohypoparathyroidism type 1B (PHP-1B)
B. Pseudohypoparathyroidism type 1A (PHP-1A)
C. Pseudo-pseudohypoparathyroidism (PPHP)
D. Progressive osseous heteroplasia (POH)

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Pseudohypoparathyroidism type 1B (PHP-1B)

Pseudohypoparathyroidism type 1B (PHP-1B) is characterized by isolated renal PTH resistance (hypocalcemia, hyperphosphatemia, elevated PTH) and mild TSH resistance, without the physical features of Albright Hereditary Osteodystrophy (normal stature, no brachydactyly or subcutaneous ossifications). It is caused by epigenetic loss of methylation at maternal GNAS DMRs (specifically exon A/B), leading to tissue-specific suppression of Gs-alpha in renal proximal tubules.
