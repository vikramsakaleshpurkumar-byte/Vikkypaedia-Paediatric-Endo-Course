## Authored MCQ

A 2-week-old male infant born at term presents with lethargy, a blood glucose of 28 mg/dL, and a total bilirubin of 9.2 mg/dL with a direct fraction of 4.1 mg/dL. Physical examination reveals a stretched penile length of 1.6 cm (reference >2.5 cm) and bilateral descended testes. Thyroid-stimulating hormone is 1.8 mIU/L with free T4 0.6 ng/dL (reference 0.9-2.0 ng/dL). What is the most appropriate next step in management?

A. Immediate critical blood draw for cortisol, GH, and insulin, administration of IV dextrose and hydrocortisone, followed by pituitary MRI
B. High-dose oral diazoxide therapy for presumed primary congenital hyperinsulinism
C. Recombinant human growth hormone monotherapy at 50 mcg/kg/day
D. Observation with frequent feeding intervals and repeat bilirubin in 4 weeks

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Immediate critical blood draw for cortisol, GH, and insulin, administration of IV dextrose and hydrocortisone, followed by pituitary MRI

The infant exhibits the classic presentation of congenital hypopituitarism: neonatal hypoglycemia, conjugated hyperbilirubinemia, micropenis, and central hypothyroidism. Prompt evaluation of adrenal status and hydrocortisone replacement are lifesaving before initiating other hormone therapies.

## Authored MCQ

An 11-year-old boy with severe short stature (height SDS -3.2) and growth velocity of 3.2 cm/year undergoes evaluation. Baseline IGF-I is below the 5th percentile. His bone age is 8.5 years and testicular volume is 2 mL bilaterally. Before performing provocative testing with arginine and clonidine, what step should be taken to optimize diagnostic accuracy?

A. Priming with intramuscular testosterone enanthate or oral estrogen 2-3 days prior to testing
B. Performing an oral glucose tolerance test to rule out reactive hypoglycemia
C. Withholding evaluation until chronological age 14 years regardless of growth velocity
D. Administering a trial of oral levothyroxine for 6 months before biochemical testing

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Priming with intramuscular testosterone enanthate or oral estrogen 2-3 days prior to testing

In prepubertal boys older than 11 years with bone age delay, sex-steroid priming primes the somatotroph axis, preventing false-positive subnormal GH peaks that occur because of constitutional delay of puberty.

## Authored MCQ

A 7-year-old girl with confirmed isolated somatotroph deficiency begins subcutaneous somatropin at 30 mcg/kg/day. At her 3-month follow-up, she has grown 3.0 cm, but routine lab testing reveals a drop in free T4 from 1.2 ng/dL to 0.7 ng/dL (reference 0.8-1.8 ng/dL) with an unchanged normal TSH of 2.1 mIU/L. What is the physiological mechanism explaining this finding?

A. Enhanced peripheral conversion of T4 to T3 and unmasking of incipient central hypothyroidism
B. Autoimmune destruction of thyroid follicular cells induced by recombinant protein antibodies
C. Competitive inhibition of thyroid iodine uptake by circulating somatropin molecules
D. False laboratory assay interference between growth hormone and thyroglobulin

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Enhanced peripheral conversion of T4 to T3 and unmasking of incipient central hypothyroidism

Growth hormone replacement stimulates hepatic and peripheral 5'-deiodinase activity, accelerating conversion of thyroxine (T4) to triiodothyronine (T3). In patients with underlying pituitary pathology, this can unmask central hypothyroidism requiring levothyroxine supplementation.

## Authored MCQ

A 12-year-old boy who received 24 Gy of prophylactic cranial irradiation for acute lymphoblastic leukemia at age 5 is evaluated for linear growth failure. His height velocity is 2.8 cm/year. What is the pathophysiology of his radiation-induced somatotroph impairment?

A. Hypothalamic radiation injury with loss of pulsatile GHRH secretion and decreased somatotroph reserve
B. Direct total destruction of pituitary thyrotrophs with sparing of somatotrophs
C. Primary gonadal failure causing premature epiphyseal closure without pituitary involvement
D. Peripheral end-organ resistance due to radiation-induced growth hormone receptor mutations

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Hypothalamic radiation injury with loss of pulsatile GHRH secretion and decreased somatotroph reserve

Cranial irradiation at doses ≥18-24 Gy preferentially damages hypothalamic regulatory neurons, causing blunted GHRH secretion and diminished somatotroph pulse amplitude (neurosecretory dysfunction), making somatotroph deficiency the earliest and most frequent post-radiation endocrinopathy.

## Authored MCQ

A 13-year-old boy with isolated short stature and delayed bone age undergoes genetic evaluation. Sequencing demonstrates a homozygous loss-of-function mutation in the POU1F1 (PIT1) gene. Which constellation of anterior pituitary hormone deficiencies is characteristically produced by this defect?

A. Deficiencies of Growth Hormone, Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone, and Prolactin, with normal ACTH and LH/FSH
B. Panhypopituitarism involving all anterior hormones plus central diabetes insipidus
C. Isolated adrenocorticotropic hormone deficiency with preserved somatotroph function
D. Combined LH and FSH deficiency with intact GH, TSH, and ACTH

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Deficiencies of Growth Hormone, Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone, and Prolactin, with normal ACTH and LH/FSH

POU1F1 (PIT1) is a POU-domain homeobox transcription factor required for the differentiation and maintenance of somatotrophs, thyrotrophs, and lactotrophs. Mutations cause combined GH, TSH, and PRL deficiency, while corticotrophs and gonadotrophs remain intact.
