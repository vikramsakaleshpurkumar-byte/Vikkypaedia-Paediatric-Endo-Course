## Authored MCQ

A 4-year-old boy presents with severe proportional short stature (height SDS -5.4), frontal bossing, a depressed nasal bridge, small hands and feet, and high-pitched voice. Laboratory investigation shows basal serum GH 38 ng/mL (reference <5 ng/mL) and peak stimulated GH 64 ng/mL. Serum IGF-I is <15 ng/mL (reference 50-250 ng/mL) and IGFBP-3 is undetectable. A 4-day IGF-I generation test with high-dose rhGH yields no increase in serum IGF-I. What is the most likely molecular diagnosis?

A. Homozygous inactivating mutation in the Growth Hormone Receptor (GHR) gene
B. Pituitary adenoma secreting bioinactive growth hormone
C. Homozygous mutation in the GHRH receptor gene
D. Isolated neurosecretory growth hormone deficiency

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Homozygous inactivating mutation in the Growth Hormone Receptor (GHR) gene

High circulating GH with undetectable IGF-I, unresponsiveness to exogenous rhGH in an IGF-I generation test, and classic craniofacial dysmorphism are diagnostic of severe growth hormone insensitivity (Laron syndrome) due to GHR mutations.

## Authored MCQ

A 6-year-old girl with confirmed severe primary IGF-I deficiency due to GHR exon deletion is prescribed mecasermin. Which instruction is mandatory for her parents to minimize the most frequent serious adverse effect of this therapy?

A. Administer each subcutaneous injection within 20 minutes before or after a meal or carbohydrate snack
B. Administer the injection strictly while fasting first thing in the morning
C. Co-administer a sodium polystyrene sulfonate resin to bind potassium
D. Limit fluid intake to less than 500 mL per day to prevent acute hypernatremia

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Administer each subcutaneous injection within 20 minutes before or after a meal or carbohydrate snack

Because IGF-I possesses approximately 10% of the insulin receptor-activating potency of insulin, rhIGF-I induces rapid hypoglycemia. Injections must be paired with carbohydrate-containing food.

## Authored MCQ

A 5-year-old boy presents with severe short stature (height SDS -4.8), severe generalized eczema, autoimmune thyroiditis, and recurrent bacterial pneumonia. Endocrine labs show peak GH 42 ng/mL and undetectable IGF-I. Genetic testing is most likely to identify a mutation in which gene?

A. STAT5B
B. GH1
C. POU1F1
D. GHRHR

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

STAT5B

STAT5B is essential both for growth hormone receptor signal transduction in hepatocytes and for IL-2 receptor signaling in regulatory T cells. Inactivating STAT5B mutations uniquely combine severe growth failure with profound immune dysregulation.

## Authored MCQ

An 8-year-old boy with mild short stature (height SDS -2.3) is evaluated. Laboratory testing reveals serum IGF-I SDS -3.4 and IGFBP-3 SDS -3.8. Surprisingly, his serum free IGF-I concentration is within normal limits, and he has never experienced hypoglycemia. What molecular defect accounts for this dissociation?

A. Inactivating mutation in the IGFALS gene encoding the acid-labile subunit
B. Complete deletion of the extracellular domain of the growth hormone receptor
C. Homozygous missense mutation in the insulin receptor gene (INSR)
D. Severe pituitary stalk transection with panhypopituitarism

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Inactivating mutation in the IGFALS gene encoding the acid-labile subunit

Acid-labile subunit (ALS) deficiency prevents formation of the 150-kDa ternary complex (IGF-I, IGFBP-3, ALS), accelerating clearance of bound IGF-I and leading to markedly reduced total IGF-I and IGFBP-3. However, free active IGF-I is preserved, resulting in only mild height reduction without hypoglycemia.

## Authored MCQ

A 9-year-old boy with severe Laron syndrome has been treated with mecasermin (120 mcg/kg twice daily) for 2 years. His parents note new-onset loud snoring and daytime sleepiness. Examination demonstrates marked enlargement of the palatine tonsils and adenoidal facies. What is the underlying cause of this finding?

A. IGF-I-mediated stimulation of lymphoid tissue proliferation in Waldeyer's ring
B. Recurrent streptococcal pharyngitis secondary to acquired agammaglobulinemia
C. Skeletal compression of the nasopharynx by acromegalic bone overgrowth
D. Allergic reaction to the preservative in the mecasermin formulation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

IGF-I-mediated stimulation of lymphoid tissue proliferation in Waldeyer's ring

Mecasermin therapy commonly stimulates the growth of lymphoid tissues, particularly the tonsils and adenoids, which express high densities of IGF-I receptors. This can lead to upper airway obstruction and obstructive sleep apnea requiring surgical adenotonsillectomy.
