## Authored MCQ

An 11-year-old boy undergoes routine universal lipid screening. Fasting lipid profile reveals: total cholesterol 284 mg/dL, triglycerides 95 mg/dL, HDL-C 48 mg/dL, and LDL-C 217 mg/dL. His father suffered a fatal myocardial infarction at age 38. Physical examination reveals nodular thickening of both Achilles tendons. After 6 months of strict dietary modification (CHILD-2 diet), his repeat fasting LDL-C remains 208 mg/dL. What is the most appropriate next step in management?

A. Initiate HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor (statin) therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, targeting LDL-C <130 mg/dL
B. Continue dietary modification alone without medications until age 18
C. Initiate fibrate therapy with fenofibrate
D. Perform emergency coronary artery bypass grafting

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Initiate HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor (statin) therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, targeting LDL-C <130 mg/dL

This patient has heterozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia (HeFH), documented by severe LDL elevation (>190 mg/dL), tendon xanthomas, and a strong family history of premature CAD. NHLBI and AAP guidelines recommend initiating statin pharmacotherapy (e.g., atorvastatin 10 mg or rosuvastatin 5-10 mg daily) in children >=10 years whose LDL-C remains persistently >=190 mg/dL (or >=160 mg/dL with premature CVD family history) after lifestyle intervention. The therapeutic target is LDL-C <130 mg/dL.

## Authored MCQ

A 4-year-old girl is brought to the clinic for yellow-orange papules with erythematous bases on her buttocks, elbows, and knees (eruptive xanthomas). Her blood sample drawn in the laboratory separates into a supernatant that resembles heavy cream over clear infranatant. Fasting triglycerides are 2450 mg/dL, while total cholesterol is 210 mg/dL. Genetic testing confirms biallelic loss-of-function mutations in the LPL gene. What acute medical emergency is she at highest risk for developing?

A. Acute necrotizing pancreatitis
B. Acute myocardial infarction
C. Hepatic encephalopathy
D. Rhabdomyolysis

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Acute necrotizing pancreatitis

Familial Chylomicronemia Syndrome (FCS) caused by LPL deficiency results in massive accumulation of circulating chylomicrons, eruptive xanthomas, lipemia retinalis, and triglyceride concentrations exceeding 1000-2000 mg/dL. The most dangerous acute complication of severe hypertriglyceridemia is acute necrotizing pancreatitis, triggered when pancreatic lipase hydrolyzes massive chylomicron triglycerides, generating toxic free fatty acids that cause capillary thrombosis and acinar necrosis.

## Authored MCQ

What is the mechanism of action of ezetimibe, an adjunctive medication used in children with refractory familial hypercholesterolemia who do not meet LDL targets on statins alone?

A. Selective inhibition of the Niemann-Pick C1-Like 1 (NPC1L1) transporter in the jejunal brush border, blocking intestinal cholesterol absorption
B. Inhibition of hepatic HMG-CoA reductase
C. Binding and sequestration of intestinal bile acids
D. Monoclonal inhibition of proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9)

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Selective inhibition of the Niemann-Pick C1-Like 1 (NPC1L1) transporter in the jejunal brush border, blocking intestinal cholesterol absorption

Ezetimibe localizes to the brush border of the small intestine and selectively inhibits the Niemann-Pick C1-Like 1 (NPC1L1) sterol transporter. This inhibits the absorption of biliary and dietary cholesterol into enterocytes, reducing intestinal cholesterol delivery to the liver, upregulating hepatic LDL receptors, and lowering circulating LDL-C by an additional 15-20% when added to a statin.

## Authored MCQ

An 18-month-old child presents with extensive planar xanthomas in the interdigital webs of the hands, popliteal fossae, and buttocks. Fasting lipid panel reveals total cholesterol 840 mg/dL, LDL-C 780 mg/dL, and normal triglycerides. Echocardiogram reveals supravalvular aortic stenosis and aortic root thickening. Both parents have mild hypercholesterolemia (LDL-C ~175 mg/dL). What is the diagnosis?

A. Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia (HoFH)
B. Familial dysbetalipoproteinemia (Type III hyperlipidemia)
C. Niemann-Pick disease type C
D. Familial combined hyperlipidemia

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia (HoFH)

Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia (HoFH) is a rare, severe condition (1:300,000) caused by biallelic LDLR (or APOB/PCSK9) mutations inherited from both heterozygous parents. Hallmark features include extreme LDL-C elevations (>400-1000 mg/dL), early planar cutaneous xanthomas in infancy/toddlerhood, and rapid development of aortic root atheroma/supravalvular aortic stenosis and fatal myocardial infarction in early childhood without aggressive treatment (such as LDL apheresis and lomitapide/evinacumab).

## Authored MCQ

A 13-year-old obese male with BMI at the 98th percentile has a fasting lipid profile showing: total cholesterol 195 mg/dL, triglycerides 280 mg/dL, HDL-C 32 mg/dL, and calculated LDL-C 107 mg/dL. Fasting ALT is 55 U/L. What is the most likely etiology of this lipid abnormality?

A. Obesity-associated dyslipidemia secondary to insulin resistance and increased hepatic VLDL production
B. Lipoprotein lipase deficiency
C. Heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia
D. Abetalipoproteinemia

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Obesity-associated dyslipidemia secondary to insulin resistance and increased hepatic VLDL production

The most common lipid abnormality in pediatric practice is secondary dyslipidemia related to obesity and insulin resistance. The characteristic atherogenic lipid triad consists of: (1) elevated fasting triglycerides (typically 150-400 mg/dL) driven by excessive hepatic VLDL synthesis and impaired LPL clearance; (2) low HDL cholesterol (<40 mg/dL); and (3) normal or borderline LDL-C with an increased proportion of atherogenic small dense LDL particles. It is frequently accompanied by metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (elevated ALT).
