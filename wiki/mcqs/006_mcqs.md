## Authored MCQ

A 5-year-old girl is evaluated for progressive growth deceleration. Her height is at the 1st percentile (height SDS -2.4). Examination reveals a low posterior hairline, high-arched palate, broad chest with widely spaced nipples, and mild cubitus valgus. Cardiovascular exam reveals a grade 2/6 systolic murmur. Echocardiogram demonstrates a bicuspid aortic valve with normal ventricular function. What is the most definitive test to confirm her diagnosis?

A. 30-cell peripheral blood karyotype or chromosomal microarray
B. Provocative growth hormone stimulation testing with clonidine and arginine
C. Targeted sequencing of the PTPN11 gene
D. 24-hour ambulatory urinary growth hormone excretion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

30-cell peripheral blood karyotype or chromosomal microarray

A karyotype or chromosomal microarray is the gold standard to establish Turner syndrome (45,X or mosaicism), which accounts for her short stature, somatic stigmas, and bicuspid aortic valve.

## Authored MCQ

A 10-year-old girl with confirmed 45,X Turner syndrome has received somatropin (0.375 mg/kg/week) for 3 years, but her height SDS remains -2.7 despite good adherence. Her bone age is 8.5 years. To augment her final adult height, which adjunctive medication is supported by clinical consensus guidelines?

A. Low-dose oral oxandrolone at 0.03 to 0.05 mg/kg/day
B. High-dose transdermal estradiol at adult feminizing doses
C. Continuous subcutaneous GnRH antagonist therapy
D. Daily oral fludrocortisone acetate

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Low-dose oral oxandrolone at 0.03 to 0.05 mg/kg/day

In girls with Turner syndrome aged ≥9-10 years with significant height deficits, adding low-dose oxandrolone (0.03-0.05 mg/kg/day) to somatropin synergistically enhances linear height velocity and adult height without causing unacceptable virilization.

## Authored MCQ

A 6-year-old boy presents with short stature (height SDS -2.6), hypertelorism, downslanting palpebral fissures, low-set ears, a webbed neck, and pectus excavatum. Echocardiogram reveals dysplastic pulmonary valve stenosis with mild right ventricular outflow tract obstruction. Genetic testing is most likely to reveal a pathogenic gain-of-function mutation in which gene?

A. PTPN11
B. GHR
C. FGFR3
D. GH1

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

PTPN11

Noonan syndrome is most commonly (approx. 50%) caused by gain-of-function mutations in PTPN11, encoding the SHP2 protein tyrosine phosphatase in the RAS-MAPK pathway, classically presenting with pulmonary valve stenosis and dysmorphism.

## Authored MCQ

A 12-year-old girl is evaluated for short stature and bilateral wrist pain. Physical exam reveals forearm shortening (mesomelia), prominent ulnar styloids, and limited wrist extension and supination. Wrist radiographs show bilateral curvature of the distal radius with triangular carpal bones and dorsal dislocation of the distal ulna (Madelung deformity). What is the underlying molecular defect?

A. Haploinsufficiency of the SHOX gene on the pseudoautosomal region of chromosome Xp22.3/Yp11.3
B. Activating mutation in the PTH1R gene encoding the parathyroid hormone receptor
C. Inactivating mutation in the CASR gene encoding the calcium-sensing receptor
D. Homozygous loss-of-function mutation in the SOX9 gene

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Haploinsufficiency of the SHOX gene on the pseudoautosomal region of chromosome Xp22.3/Yp11.3

Madelung deformity in association with mesomelic short stature is the hallmark of Léri-Weill dyschondrosteosis, caused by heterozygous deletion or point mutation of the SHOX gene in the pseudoautosomal region (PAR1).

## Authored MCQ

A 16-year-old female with Turner syndrome who is completing somatropin therapy presents for transition planning. Her blood pressure is 138/86 mmHg. Transthoracic echocardiogram demonstrates an aortic root diameter index of 2.6 cm/m2 (normal <2.0 cm/m2). What is the primary clinical significance of this finding?

A. High lifetime risk of catastrophic aortic dissection requiring aggressive blood pressure control and cardiac MRI monitoring
B. Normal physiological adaptation to growth hormone therapy requiring no intervention
C. Transient post-pubertal remodeling that will spontaneously regress by age 21
D. Indication for immediate cardiac transplantation within 30 days

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

High lifetime risk of catastrophic aortic dissection requiring aggressive blood pressure control and cardiac MRI monitoring

Aortic dilation in Turner syndrome carries a catastrophic risk of fatal aortic dissection and rupture. Aortic diameter index ≥2.5 cm/m2 requires tight blood pressure control (typically beta-blockers/ARBs), restriction of competitive isometric exertion, and serial surveillance by cardiac MRI.
