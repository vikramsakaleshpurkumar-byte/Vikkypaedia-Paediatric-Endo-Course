## Authored MCQ

A 14-year-old boy whose mother has MEN1 is brought for routine screening. Serum calcium is elevated at 10.8 mg/dL (reference: 8.8-10.2 mg/dL), ionized calcium is 1.42 mmol/L, and intact PTH is 95 pg/mL (reference: 15-65 pg/mL). 24-hour urine calcium is 340 mg (elevated). Neck ultrasound reveals four enlarged, hypoechoic parathyroid glands. What is the most appropriate surgical management for primary hyperparathyroidism in MEN1?

A. Subtotal parathyroidectomy (resecting 3.5 glands) or total parathyroidectomy with heterotopic forearm autotransplantation
B. Resection of a single parathyroid adenoma alone
C. Conservative observation with oral cinacalcet until age 25
D. Total thyroidectomy without parathyroid exploration

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Subtotal parathyroidectomy (resecting 3.5 glands) or total parathyroidectomy with heterotopic forearm autotransplantation

Primary hyperparathyroidism in MEN1 is characterized by asymmetric multiglandular parathyroid hyperplasia involving all four glands, rather than a solitary adenoma. Resection of a single enlarged gland results in virtually 100% recurrence. Consensus guidelines recommend subtotal parathyroidectomy (resection of 3.5 glands with marking of the remnant) or total parathyroidectomy with autotransplantation of parathyroid tissue into the forearm musculature to treat hypercalcemia while minimizing permanent hypoparathyroidism.

## Authored MCQ

A 15-year-old girl with a documented germline RET codon 634 mutation (MEN2A) is scheduled to undergo total thyroidectomy for an elevated basal calcitonin. Before proceeding to the operating room, which diagnostic evaluation is mandatory to prevent an intraoperative mortality?

A. Measurement of plasma free metanephrines or 24-hour urinary fractionated metanephrines to rule out pheochromocytoma
B. Evaluation of serum gastrin and chromogranin A
C. Brain MRI to rule out a prolactinoma
D. Technetium-99m sestamibi parathyroid scan

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Measurement of plasma free metanephrines or 24-hour urinary fractionated metanephrines to rule out pheochromocytoma

In patients with MEN2A or MEN2B, pheochromocytoma occurs in up to 50% of cases. It is a cardinal rule of endocrine surgery that pheochromocytoma must ALWAYS be excluded (using plasma or urinary free fractionated metanephrines) or surgically resected prior to any planned thyroid or neck surgery. Anesthesia or intraoperative manipulation in a patient with an occult pheochromocytoma can trigger a massive catecholamine surge, fatal hypertensive crisis, and malignant arrhythmia.

## Authored MCQ

A 2-month-old infant is evaluated for chronic severe constipation, abdominal distension, and poor weight gain. Physical examination reveals prominent fleshy bumps along the mucosal margins of the tongue and inner lips, thickened eyelids with everted margins, and long, slender limbs. Genetic testing reveals a de novo heterozygous RET M918T mutation in exon 16. What is the diagnosis and the most urgent surgical recommendation?

A. Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia Type 2B (MEN2B); prophylactic total thyroidectomy within the first months of life
B. Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia Type 1 (MEN1); subtotal pancreatectomy
C. Neurofibromatosis Type 1; optic pathway glioma resection
D. Cowden syndrome; prophylactic bilateral mastectomy

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia Type 2B (MEN2B); prophylactic total thyroidectomy within the first months of life

This infant has classic Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia Type 2B (MEN2B), characterized by mucosal neuromas of the tongue/lips, intestinal ganglioneuromatosis (causing chronic megacolon/constipation), marfanoid habitus, and the pathognomonic RET M918T mutation. Medullary thyroid carcinoma in MEN2B develops in early infancy and metastasizes aggressively. ATA guidelines recommend prophylactic total thyroidectomy within the first year of life, preferably in the first months of life.

## Authored MCQ

A 16-year-old male presents with extreme tall stature (height 204 cm, >99.9th percentile, +4.2 SD), coarse facial features, prognathism, and massive hands and feet. Sellar MRI reveals a 3.2-cm invasive pituitary macroadenoma invading both cavernous sinuses. Serum IGF-1 is markedly elevated. Genetic analysis reveals a heterozygous germline mutation in the AIP gene. Which family condition is this patient suffering from?

A. Familial Isolated Pituitary Adenoma (FIPA) with pituitary gigantism
B. Carney complex due to PRKAR1A mutation
C. McCune-Albright syndrome due to GNAS mutation
D. Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia Type 2A

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Familial Isolated Pituitary Adenoma (FIPA) with pituitary gigantism

Inactivating germline mutations in AIP (aryl hydrocarbon receptor-interacting protein) account for ~15-20% of Familial Isolated Pituitary Adenoma (FIPA) kindreds. AIP-associated pituitary neuroendocrine tumors characteristically manifest in children and adolescents as aggressive, macroadenomatous GH-secreting tumors (somatotropinomas) that cause severe pituitary gigantism, invade parasellar structures, and respond poorly to somatostatin analog medical therapy.

## Authored MCQ

A 14-month-old female infant is evaluated for rapid, dramatic linear overgrowth. Her height velocity has exceeded 20 cm/year, and her height is +5.5 SD above the mean. Laboratory testing demonstrates elevated serum growth hormone, unsuppressed GH on oral glucose testing, and high serum prolactin. Pituitary MRI demonstrates diffuse pituitary enlargement with macroadenoma. Chromosomal microarray reveals a microduplication of chromosome Xq26.3. What is the diagnosis?

A. X-linked acrogigantism (X-LAG syndrome) involving GPR101 duplication
B. Sotos syndrome due to NSD1 mutation
C. Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome due to 11p15 imprinting defect
D. Marfan syndrome due to FBN1 mutation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

X-linked acrogigantism (X-LAG syndrome) involving GPR101 duplication

X-linked acrogigantism (X-LAG) syndrome is caused by germline or somatic mosaic microduplications on chromosome Xq26.3 encompassing the GPR101 gene. Affected children present in early infancy (<12-24 months of age) with the earliest onset and most severe form of pituitary gigantism known, characterized by marked hypersecretion of both growth hormone and prolactin due to diffuse pituitary hyperplasia or mixed somato-lactotrope macroadenomas.
