## Authored MCQ

A 12-year-old boy with steroid-dependent nephrotic syndrome who has received oral prednisone (1-2 mg/kg/day) for 14 months complains of mild mid-back ache. Lateral thoracolumbar spine radiograph demonstrates a 30% anterior height loss (wedge deformity) of the L1 vertebral body after jumping from a curb. Lumbar spine DXA reveals a bone mineral density Z-score of -1.4. Which statement is correct regarding his bone status?

A. He meets criteria for pediatric osteoporosis based on the presence of a low-trauma vertebral fracture, regardless of the DXA Z-score
B. He cannot be diagnosed with osteoporosis because his DXA Z-score is greater than -2.0
C. He has osteopenia, but osteoporosis cannot be diagnosed until he sustains three additional peripheral fractures
D. His vertebral collapse represents Scheuermann disease rather than osteoporosis

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

He meets criteria for pediatric osteoporosis based on the presence of a low-trauma vertebral fracture, regardless of the DXA Z-score

According to the official pediatric positions of the International Society for Clinical Densitometry (ISCD), the finding of one or more low-trauma vertebral compression fractures is sufficient on its own to establish the diagnosis of osteoporosis in children and adolescents, regardless of the measured bone mineral density Z-score. A DXA Z-score <= -2.0 is not required when a fragility vertebral fracture is documented.

## Authored MCQ

A 9-year-old girl with severe osteogenesis imperfecta type III is admitted to receive her first planned intravenous infusion of pamidronate (1 mg/kg/dose). Which of the following prophylactic clinical measures is most essential prior to initiating the infusion?

A. Verification and repletion of 25-hydroxyvitamin D to >20-30 ng/mL and provision of oral calcium and antipyretics
B. Administration of high-dose intravenous hydrocortisone 100 mg/m2
C. Placement of a permanent tunneled central venous line
D. Restriction of oral fluids to prevent hypervolemia

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Verification and repletion of 25-hydroxyvitamin D to >20-30 ng/mL and provision of oral calcium and antipyretics

The first intravenous bisphosphonate infusion universally triggers an acute phase reaction (fever, flu-like symptoms, myalgias) and rapid influx of extracellular calcium into skeleton. If a patient is vitamin D deficient or hypocalcemic prior to infusion, severe life-threatening hypocalcemia and tetany can occur. Pre-infusion repletion of 25-OH-D (>20-30 ng/mL), normal ionized calcium, oral calcium supplementation, and antipyretic prophylaxis (acetaminophen/ibuprofen) are mandatory standard of care.

## Authored MCQ

What is the primary cellular and molecular mechanism by which intravenous bisphosphonates (such as pamidronate and zoledronic acid) increase bone mass and vertebral height in children with osteoporosis?

A. Inhibition of osteoclast farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase, inducing osteoclast apoptosis and suppressing bone resorption while growing physis adds new bone
B. Direct stimulation of osteoblast proliferation and collagen synthesis through the parathyroid hormone receptor
C. Activation of osteocytic sclerostin secretion to promote Wnt signaling
D. Inhibition of renal tubular calcium and phosphate excretion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Inhibition of osteoclast farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase, inducing osteoclast apoptosis and suppressing bone resorption while growing physis adds new bone

Nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates (pamidronate, zoledronate) are taken up selectively by active osteoclasts during bone resorption and inhibit farnesyl pyrophosphate (FPP) synthase in the mevalonate pathway. This prevents prenylation of small GTPases essential for osteoclast cytoskeletal function and survival, inducing osteoclast apoptosis. By arresting resorption, ongoing endochondral bone formation at growing physeal growth plates continuously deposits new bone, progressively thickening cortical and trabecular architecture and reshaping collapsed vertebral bodies.

## Authored MCQ

A 14-year-old non-ambulatory adolescent male with Duchenne muscular dystrophy on deflazacort therapy sustains his third low-trauma femur fracture. His lumbar spine DXA Z-score adjusted for height is -3.2. He is initiated on intravenous zoledronic acid 0.05 mg/kg every 6 months. What is the expected physiological impact of bisphosphonate therapy on his vertebral and long-bone health?

A. Reduction in bone pain, prevention of future vertebral compression fractures, and partial or complete vertebral body remodeling
B. Accelerated progression of scoliosis requiring immediate spinal fusion
C. Permanent arrest of longitudinal growth at epiphyseal growth plates
D. Spontaneous cure of Duchenne muscular dystrophy muscle weakness

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Reduction in bone pain, prevention of future vertebral compression fractures, and partial or complete vertebral body remodeling

In glucocorticoid-treated children with secondary osteoporosis (such as Duchenne muscular dystrophy), intravenous zoledronic acid markedly reduces bone pain, prevents new vertebral and long-bone fractures, increases areal and volumetric bone density, and allows growing vertebral bodies to remodel and restore height. It does not arrest growth plate growth and has no curative effect on underlying dystrophin deficiency.

## Authored MCQ

A 7-year-old boy presents with multiple recurrent fractures following minimal falls, blue-tinted sclerae, triangular facies, and opalescent amber-brown teeth that chip easily (dentinogenesis imperfecta). Hearing screening shows conductive hearing loss. What is the most likely mode of inheritance and underlying molecular defect?

A. Autosomal dominant mutations in COL1A1 or COL1A2 (Osteogenesis Imperfecta)
B. Autosomal recessive mutations in the VDR gene
C. X-linked recessive mutation in the DMD gene
D. Autosomal dominant activating mutation in the FGFR3 gene

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Autosomal dominant mutations in COL1A1 or COL1A2 (Osteogenesis Imperfecta)

The clinical constellation of recurrent fragility fractures, blue sclerae, dentinogenesis imperfecta, and hearing impairment is pathognomonic for Osteogenesis Imperfecta (types I-IV), caused in over 85-90% of cases by heterozygous autosomal dominant mutations in COL1A1 or COL1A2, which encode the alpha chains of type I collagen.
