## Authored MCQ

A 3-year-old girl is evaluated for short stature. She was born at 39 weeks gestation with a birth weight of 2100 g (SDS -2.4) and length of 44 cm (SDS -2.5). Her current height is at the 0.2nd percentile (height SDS -2.8). Extensive evaluation reveals normal systemic, renal, and thyroid function. Which statement regarding her management is correct according to clinical guidelines?

A. She meets FDA criteria for recombinant human growth hormone therapy because she is older than 2 years with persistent height SDS <-2.0
B. Growth hormone therapy is contraindicated until bone age reaches chronological age 10 years
C. She should be managed with high-calorie nutritional supplementation alone until pubertal onset
D. Growth hormone therapy should be withheld until provocative GH stimulation testing confirms peak GH <5 ng/mL

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

She meets FDA criteria for recombinant human growth hormone therapy because she is older than 2 years with persistent height SDS <-2.0

Children born SGA who fail to manifest catch-up growth by age 2 years with persistent height SDS <-2.0 are eligible for FDA-approved somatropin therapy regardless of GH stimulation test results.

## Authored MCQ

A 4-year-old boy born SGA at 38 weeks with birth weight 1950 g presents with height SDS -3.2. Physical exam reveals a prominent forehead, triangular facies, down-turned mouth, and significant hemihypotrophy of the left lower extremity. What is the most appropriate molecular testing to establish the diagnosis?

A. Methylation-specific multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification (MS-MLPA) for chromosome 11p15.5 and maternal UPD7
B. Sequencing of the GHR gene for Laron syndrome
C. Karyotype to evaluate for Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY)
D. Fluorescent in situ hybridization (FISH) for 22q11.2 deletion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Methylation-specific multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification (MS-MLPA) for chromosome 11p15.5 and maternal UPD7

Triangular facies, relative macrocephaly, body asymmetry/hemihypotrophy, and severe intrauterine growth failure are the classic features of Silver-Russell syndrome, caused by loss of methylation at 11p15.5 (50%) or maternal uniparental disomy of chromosome 7 (10%).

## Authored MCQ

A 5-year-old boy born SGA without catch-up growth begins somatropin at 45 mcg/kg/day. At baseline, his fasting blood glucose is 88 mg/dL and fasting insulin is 8 mcIU/mL. After 12 months, his growth velocity has accelerated from 4.0 to 9.2 cm/year. Repeat fasting labs show glucose 92 mg/dL and fasting insulin 16 mcIU/mL. What is the correct interpretation of these metabolic changes?

A. Physiological compensatory hyperinsulinemia secondary to the known anti-insulin actions of somatropin
B. Development of overt autoimmune type 1 diabetes requiring insulin initiation
C. Acute drug-induced ketoacidosis requiring immediate emergency hospitalization
D. Primary adrenal failure causing secondary reactive insulin hypersecretion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Physiological compensatory hyperinsulinemia secondary to the known anti-insulin actions of somatropin

Growth hormone possesses physiological anti-insulin (lipolytic and glycogenolytic) actions that mildly decrease peripheral insulin sensitivity, which is compensated for by an appropriate increase in fasting insulin without overt hyperglycemia.

## Authored MCQ

A pediatric endocrinologist plans somatropin therapy for a 3-year-old child born SGA (height SDS -2.8). Which dosing strategy is supported by international consensus guidelines for optimizing adult height outcomes?

A. 0.24 to 0.48 mg/kg/week (35 to 70 mcg/kg/day), titrating according to growth response and serum IGF-I
B. Low-dose pulse therapy at 5 mcg/kg/day administered once weekly
C. A fixed adult dose of 4.0 mg per day regardless of patient weight
D. High-dose intramuscular injections of 200 mcg/kg administered every 3 months

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

0.24 to 0.48 mg/kg/week (35 to 70 mcg/kg/day), titrating according to growth response and serum IGF-I

Standard consensus guidelines recommend somatropin at 35 to 70 mcg/kg/day (0.24 to 0.48 mg/kg/week) as daily subcutaneous injections, adjusting the dose to maintain serum IGF-I in the upper normal range (+1 to +2 SDS).

## Authored MCQ

During routine follow-up of a 7-year-old girl born SGA receiving somatropin (40 mcg/kg/day), her serum IGF-I is measured at +3.2 SDS for age and sex. Her linear growth rate is excellent at 8.5 cm/year. What is the most appropriate management decision?

A. Reduce the somatropin dosage by 15-25% and recheck serum IGF-I in 3 to 6 months
B. Double the dose of somatropin to maximize peak pubertal height velocity
C. Discontinue therapy permanently and tell the parents no further growth is possible
D. Initiate high-dose hydrocortisone to suppress circulating IGF-I concentrations

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Reduce the somatropin dosage by 15-25% and recheck serum IGF-I in 3 to 6 months

When serum IGF-I exceeds +2.0 to +2.5 SDS during somatropin therapy, consensus guidelines recommend dose reduction (typically 15-25%) to maintain IGF-I within safe physiological bounds while monitoring growth velocity.
