## Authored MCQ

A 16-year-old girl with severe anorexia nervosa has lost 18 kg over the past 8 months. Her current BMI is 14.8 kg/m2. She has been amenorrheic for 7 months. Endocrine laboratory evaluation reveals: serum LH 0.4 IU/L, FSH 1.2 IU/L, estradiol <10 pg/mL, basal growth hormone 18 ng/mL (elevated), IGF-1 92 ng/mL (severely low), total T3 45 ng/dL (low), free T4 0.8 ng/dL (low-normal), and TSH 1.4 mIU/L. What is the physiological mechanism underlying her elevated growth hormone and low IGF-1?

A. Starvation-induced hepatic growth hormone resistance secondary to downregulation of cell-surface GH receptors
B. A growth hormone-secreting pituitary somatotrope adenoma
C. Ectopic production of GHRH by retroperitoneal adipose tissue
D. Excessive renal tubular reabsorption of pituitary growth hormone

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Starvation-induced hepatic growth hormone resistance secondary to downregulation of cell-surface GH receptors

In anorexia nervosa and severe under-nutrition, starvation triggers acquired peripheral growth hormone resistance. Nutrient and insulin deprivation causes marked downregulation of hepatic growth hormone receptors and post-receptor signaling pathways (JAK2/STAT5b). Consequently, hepatic synthesis of IGF-1 falls precipitously. The absence of negative feedback inhibition by circulating IGF-1 on the pituitary somatotropes leads to compensatory hypersecretion of growth hormone.

## Authored MCQ

A 17-year-old female with anorexia nervosa who has been amenorrheic for 14 months undergoes dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA). Her lumbar spine bone mineral density Z-score is -2.8. In addition to nutritional rehabilitation and calcium/vitamin D repletion, what pharmacological intervention has been shown in randomized controlled trials to effectively increase bone mineral density in this setting?

A. Physiological replacement with transdermal 17-beta estradiol patches combined with cyclic oral micronized progesterone
B. Combined oral contraceptive pills containing 35 mcg ethinyl estradiol
C. Intravenous zoledronic acid infusion every 6 months
D. Subcutaneous teriparatide (PTH 1-34) monotherapy

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Physiological replacement with transdermal 17-beta estradiol patches combined with cyclic oral micronized progesterone

Randomized controlled trials have demonstrated that physiological transdermal 17-beta estradiol (100 mcg patches applied twice weekly) combined with cyclic oral micronized progesterone (200 mg/day for 12 days/month) significantly increases lumbar spine and femoral bone mineral density in adolescents with anorexia nervosa. In contrast, standard oral contraceptive pills (ethinyl estradiol) do not improve bone density because hepatic first-pass metabolism suppresses hepatic IGF-1 synthesis.

## Authored MCQ

A 15-year-old girl with anorexia nervosa is found on routine screening to have total T3 42 ng/dL (reference: 85-170 ng/dL), free T4 0.9 ng/dL (reference: 0.9-1.8 ng/dL), and TSH 1.6 mIU/L (reference: 0.5-4.5 mIU/L). Reverse T3 is elevated. She complains of cold intolerance and has bradycardia (pulse 48 bpm). What is the appropriate clinical approach to her thyroid status?

A. Withhold thyroid hormone replacement; this represents an adaptive non-thyroidal illness syndrome that will resolve with nutritional refeeding
B. Initiate oral levothyroxine 50 mcg daily to restore normal metabolic rate
C. Initiate oral liothyronine (T3) 25 mcg daily to normalize circulating T3
D. Perform radioiodine uptake scan to rule out toxic adenoma

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Withhold thyroid hormone replacement; this represents an adaptive non-thyroidal illness syndrome that will resolve with nutritional refeeding

The thyroid hormone pattern in anorexia nervosa represents non-thyroidal illness syndrome ('low T3 syndrome'), an adaptive hypometabolic state mediated by down-regulation of 5'-deiodinase (reducing T4 to T3 conversion) and upregulation of DIO3 (increasing reverse T3). This adaptation minimizes energy expenditure and tissue catabolism during severe energy deficit. Administering exogenous thyroid hormone (T4 or T3) is strictly contraindicated because it accelerates protein breakdown, induces cardiac arrhythmias, and worsens cachexia.

## Authored MCQ

A 9-year-old boy with severe generalized obesity (BMI 29 kg/m2, >99th percentile) undergoes endocrine evaluation. His linear growth velocity is 7.2 cm/year (accelerated, 85th percentile for age), and his bone age is 10.5 years. Provocative growth hormone testing with clonidine reveals a peak GH of 4.2 ng/mL (subnormal; cut-off <7-10 ng/mL). Serum IGF-1 is normal (240 ng/mL). How should these findings be interpreted?

A. Pseudo-GH deficiency secondary to obesity-induced blunted somatotrope responsiveness, with normal tissue IGF-1 action and accelerated growth
B. Isolated organic pituitary growth hormone deficiency requiring daily rhGH therapy
C. Craniopharyngioma with panhypopituitarism
D. Growth hormone receptor insensitivity (Laron syndrome)

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Pseudo-GH deficiency secondary to obesity-induced blunted somatotrope responsiveness, with normal tissue IGF-1 action and accelerated growth

Children with simple obesity characteristically demonstrate blunted stimulated growth hormone secretion on provocative testing ('pseudo-GH deficiency'). Despite low GH peaks, these children maintain normal or elevated serum IGF-1 concentrations, accelerated prepubertal linear growth velocity, and normal or mildly advanced skeletal maturation. This is mediated by hyperinsulinemia and altered neuroendocrine feedback. Daily rhGH therapy is not indicated.

## Authored MCQ

What is the primary mechanism by which visceral obesity and hyperinsulinemia contribute to clinical hyperandrogenism and hirsutism in adolescent girls?

A. Hyperinsulinemia directly suppresses hepatic production of sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG), markedly elevating the circulating free, unbound testosterone fraction
B. Visceral adipocytes directly synthesize testosterone from cholesterol via CYP17A1
C. Insulin blocks adrenal 21-hydroxylase activity
D. Leptin stimulates pituitary LH secretion to adult male levels

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Hyperinsulinemia directly suppresses hepatic production of sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG), markedly elevating the circulating free, unbound testosterone fraction

Hyperinsulinemia resulting from obesity-induced insulin resistance acts directly on hepatocytes to suppress the gene expression and synthesis of sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG). Reduced circulating SHBG lowers the binding capacity of serum, resulting in a marked increase in the biologically active free, unbound testosterone fraction. Concurrently, hyperinsulinemia synergizes with LH in ovarian theca cells to augment androgen production.
