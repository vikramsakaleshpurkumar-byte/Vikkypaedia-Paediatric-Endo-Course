## Authored MCQ

A 14.5-year-old boy is brought for evaluation of short stature and lack of pubertal development. His height is at the 1st percentile (height SDS -2.3). His father had a pubertal growth spurt at age 17 and his mother entered menarche at 14. Physical exam shows prepubertal Tanner stage 1 pubic hair and testicular volume of 3 mL bilaterally. Radiograph of the left hand and wrist reveals a bone age of 11.5 years. Provocative GH testing with clonidine yields a peak GH of 14 ng/mL. What is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Constitutional Delay of Growth and Puberty (CDGP)
B. Familial Short Stature (FSS)
C. Classical Isolated Growth Hormone Deficiency
D. Primary Hypogonadotropic Hypogonadism with Kallmann Syndrome

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Constitutional Delay of Growth and Puberty (CDGP)

A family history of late maturation, delayed bone age (>2 years behind chronological age), prepubertal status at age 14.5, and normal peak GH (>10 ng/mL) characterize constitutional delay of growth and puberty.

## Authored MCQ

An 8-year-old girl is evaluated for short stature. Her height is at the 0.5th percentile (height SDS -2.6). Her midparental target height is at the 25th percentile. Past medical history and systemic review are negative. Laboratory workup reveals normal CBC, ESR, tissue transglutaminase IgA, thyroid panel, and electrolyte panel. Bone age is 8.0 years. What diagnostic step must be completed before categorizing her condition as idiopathic short stature?

A. Karyotype or chromosomal microarray to exclude Turner syndrome
B. Immediate 24-hour urinary free cortisol measurement
C. Skeletal survey to look for osteogenesis imperfecta type IV
D. Diagnostic renal biopsy to evaluate for occult nephritis

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Karyotype or chromosomal microarray to exclude Turner syndrome

Guidelines recommend karyotype or chromosomal microarray in all girls presenting with unexplained short stature, as mosaic Turner syndrome frequently presents without overt classical physical stigmas.

## Authored MCQ

A 14-year-old boy with constitutional delay of growth and puberty is experiencing severe depression and bullying due to short stature (height SDS -2.4, bone age 11.5 years). He has prepubertal genitalia. Which management strategy provides safe, targeted acceleration of linear growth without causing premature epiphyseal fusion?

A. Short-course intramuscular testosterone enanthate 50 mg monthly for 3 to 6 months
B. Oral ethinyl estradiol 50 mcg daily for 1 year
C. High-dose recombinant human growth hormone at 0.48 mg/kg/week until adult height
D. Continuous GnRH agonist therapy for 2 years

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Short-course intramuscular testosterone enanthate 50 mg monthly for 3 to 6 months

Low-dose testosterone (50-100 mg IM monthly for 3-6 months) is a safe, established intervention in adolescent boys ≥14 years with constitutional delay to promote linear growth and virilization without prematurely advancing epiphyseal fusion.

## Authored MCQ

A 9-year-old boy has non-familial idiopathic short stature with height SDS -2.7 and a Bayley-Pinneau predicted adult height of 156 cm (SDS -2.8). The family elects to initiate FDA-approved somatropin therapy. Based on clinical trial evidence, what is the expected average adult height gain associated with somatropin therapy in this setting?

A. Approximately 3.5 to 7.5 cm (1.4 to 3 inches) above untreated predicted height
B. Approximately 15 to 20 cm (6 to 8 inches) above untreated predicted height
C. Zero change in adult height compared to untreated controls
D. A net decrease in adult height due to accelerated skeletal maturation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Approximately 3.5 to 7.5 cm (1.4 to 3 inches) above untreated predicted height

Meta-analyses and randomized trials of somatropin in idiopathic short stature demonstrate an average adult height gain of 3.5 to 7.5 cm, with significant individual variation depending on starting age, dose, and adherence.

## Authored MCQ

An 11-year-old boy presents for short stature evaluation. His height is at the 3rd percentile, his father is 162 cm (5th percentile), and his mother is 148 cm (3rd percentile). His bone age is 11.0 years, matching his chronological age. His height velocity has been 5.2 cm/year over the past 2 years. Which diagnostic entity best accounts for these findings?

A. Familial Short Stature
B. Constitutional Delay of Growth and Puberty
C. Craniopharyngioma with panhypopituitarism
D. Primary Growth Hormone Insensitivity

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Familial Short Stature

Familial short stature is characterized by short parents, child's height tracking near midparental target, normal annual height velocity (5 cm/year), and a bone age that matches chronological age.
