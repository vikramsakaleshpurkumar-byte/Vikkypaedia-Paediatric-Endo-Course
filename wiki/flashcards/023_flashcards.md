<details><summary>What two clinical scenarios satisfy the ISCD pediatric diagnostic definition of osteoporosis?</summary>

(1) One or more low-trauma vertebral compression fractures alone; OR (2) A clinically significant fracture history (>=2 long-bone fractures by 10 yr, or >=3 by 19 yr) PLUS a DXA Z-score <= -2.0 adjusted for size.

</details>

<details><summary>Can a low DXA bone mineral density Z-score (&lt;= -2.0) alone be used to diagnose osteoporosis in a child?</summary>

No. The diagnosis requires a documented low-trauma fracture history alongside the low DXA score (or a vertebral fracture regardless of DXA); a low Z-score alone represents 'low bone mineral mass for chronological age'.

PDF p. 612
</details>

<details><summary>What is the typical dose and administration schedule for intravenous zoledronic acid in pediatric osteoporosis?</summary>

0.025 to 0.05 mg/kg per dose (maximum 4 mg) administered as an intravenous infusion over 30-45 minutes once every 6 months.

</details>

<details><summary>What is the acute phase reaction associated with the first dose of intravenous bisphosphonates in children?</summary>

Transient high fever, influenza-like myalgias, nausea, and mild hypocalcemia within 24-48 hours of infusion, mediated by inflammatory cytokine release from gamma-delta T cells.

</details>

<details><summary>What collagen genes are mutated in over 85-90% of individuals with classic osteogenesis imperfecta?</summary>

COL1A1 and COL1A2, which encode the pro-alpha1(I) and pro-alpha2(I) chains of type I collagen, inherited in an autosomal dominant manner.

PDF p. 616
</details>

<details><summary>Why do growing children with vertebral compression fractures respond more favorably to bisphosphonate therapy than adults?</summary>

Growing vertebrae undergo endochondral ossification at the superior and inferior growth plates; bisphosphonate-stabilized trabeculae allow newly formed bone to reshape and rebuild vertebral height.

PDF p. 633
</details>

<details><summary>What grading system is standardly used on lateral spine imaging to evaluate pediatric vertebral compression fractures?</summary>

The modified Genant semi-quantitative visual scale, assessing anterior, middle, and posterior vertebral height reductions (grade 1: 20-25%; grade 2: 25-40%; grade 3: >40%).
