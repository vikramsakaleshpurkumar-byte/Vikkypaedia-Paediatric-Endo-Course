<details><summary>Why is propylthiouracil (PTU) strictly contraindicated as first-line medical therapy for hyperthyroidism in children?</summary>

PTU carries a black-box FDA warning in pediatric patients due to high risks of idiosyncratic fulminant hepatic necrosis, liver failure, and death requiring emergent liver transplantation. Methimazole is the only recommended antithyroid drug.

</details>

<details><summary>What life-threatening adverse reaction to methimazole requires immediate complete blood count monitoring and drug cessation if fever or severe pharyngitis develops?</summary>

Agranulocytosis (absolute neutrophil count <500/mcL), which occurs in ~0.3-0.5% of treated patients and predisposes to rapid, fatal sepsis.

</details>

<details><summary>What is Van Wyk-Grumbach syndrome, and what is its pathophysiological mechanism?</summary>

A syndrome of severe, long-standing primary juvenile hypothyroidism presenting paradoxically with precocious puberty (isosexual precocity in girls, macrorchidism without virilization in boys) caused by extreme TSH elevation cross-activating gonadal FSH receptors.

PDF p. 399
</details>

<details><summary>What causes neonatal Graves&#x27; disease, and how long does the clinical condition typically persist in the infant?</summary>

Transplacental passage of maternal TSH-receptor-stimulating immunoglobulins (TSI / TRAb); the hyperthyroidism persists for 2 to 4 months until circulating maternal IgG antibodies are cleared from neonatal circulation.

PDF p. 407
</details>

<details><summary>How does childhood Graves&#x27; disease affect linear growth and skeletal maturation compared to childhood hypothyroidism?</summary>

Graves' disease accelerates linear growth velocity and advances skeletal maturation (accelerated bone age), whereas hypothyroidism profoundly decelerates linear growth velocity and delays bone age.

</details>

<details><summary>Which circulating autoantibodies are most sensitive for confirming the diagnosis of Hashimoto thyroiditis in a child with a goiter?</summary>

Anti-thyroid peroxidase (anti-TPO) antibodies (>90-95% sensitive) and anti-thyroglobulin (anti-TG) antibodies.

</details>

<details><summary>What is the typical maintenance dosage of levothyroxine in an adolescent with overt Hashimoto hypothyroidism?</summary>

Approximately 1.6-1.8 mcg/kg/day (typically 75 to 125 mcg once daily orally on an empty stomach).
