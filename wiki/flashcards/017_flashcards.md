<details><summary>What gene is mutated in the classic form of resistance to thyroid hormone (RTH-beta), and what is its mode of inheritance?</summary>

THRB gene (encoding thyroid hormone receptor beta), inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern with a dominant-negative effect (or occurring as de novo mutations).

</details>

<details><summary>What is the hallmark circulating thyroid hormone pattern in resistance to thyroid hormone beta (RTH-beta)?</summary>

Elevated serum free T4 and free T3 accompanied by an inappropriately normal or mildly elevated serum TSH concentration.

PDF p. 433
</details>

<details><summary>What neuroendocrine tumor must be differentiated from RTH-beta, and what biochemical ratio helps confirm the tumor?</summary>

TSH-secreting pituitary neuroendocrine tumor (TSHoma); an elevated glycoprotein alpha-subunit concentration and alpha-subunit/TSH molar ratio (>1.0) strongly favor a TSHoma.

PDF p. 441
</details>

<details><summary>Why do patients with RTH-beta frequently suffer from resting tachycardia despite generalized peripheral tissue hormone resistance?</summary>

Cardiac myocytes predominantly express the TR-alpha isoform (encoded by THRA), which is normal in RTH-beta; high circulating thyroid hormones therefore over-stimulate cardiac TR-alpha, causing tachycardia and palpitations.

PDF p. 434
</details>

<details><summary>What is the biochemical and clinical phenotype of Allan-Herndon-Dudley syndrome (MCT8 transporter deficiency)?</summary>

X-linked severe neurodevelopmental disability, central hypotonia, progressive spastic quadriplegia, and dystonia, with a biochemical profile of markedly elevated free T3, low free T4, and normal/slightly elevated TSH.

PDF p. 438
</details>

<details><summary>How does the thyroid hormone profile in RTH-alpha (THRA mutation) differ from classic RTH-beta?</summary>

RTH-alpha features normal or near-normal TSH, low or low-normal free T4, high or high-normal free T3 (markedly low FT4/FT3 ratio), and very low reverse T3, accompanied by severe skeletal dysplasia and constipation.

PDF p. 436
</details>

<details><summary>What thyroid hormone analog with high affinity for TR-beta can be used in symptomatic RTH-beta to suppress elevated TSH and reduce goiter size?</summary>

Tiratricol (TRIAC / 3,5,3'-triiodothyroacetic acid), which binds TR-beta with higher relative affinity than T3 to inhibit pituitary TSH secretion without aggravating peripheral hyperthyroidism.

</details>
