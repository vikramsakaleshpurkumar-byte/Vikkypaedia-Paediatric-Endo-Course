<details><summary>What neuroendocrine distinction differentiates the acute phase of critical illness from the chronic phase in pediatric intensive care?</summary>

The acute phase features hypercortisolemia and sympathetic overdrive with adaptive downregulation of anabolic axes; the chronic phase features neuroendocrine exhaustion and persistent central anterior pituitary hypopituitarism.

<details><summary>What is the primary clinical presentation that prompts initiation of stress-dose hydrocortisone for suspected CIRCI in pediatric intensive care?</summary>

Vasopressor-refractory septic shock (persistent hypotension and poor perfusion requiring escalating doses of norepinephrine or epinephrine despite adequate fluid resuscitation).

PDF p. 1068
</details>

<details><summary>What is the recommended empiric stress dosage of intravenous hydrocortisone for vasopressor-refractory pediatric septic shock?</summary>

50 mg/m2 (or 2 mg/kg) IV bolus, followed by 50 to 100 mg/m2/day as a continuous infusion or divided every 6 hours, tapered as vasopressors are weaned.

</details>

<details><summary>What blood glucose target range is internationally recommended for critically ill children in the PICU receiving insulin for stress hyperglycemia?</summary>

Moderate glycemic control targeting blood glucose 140 to 180 mg/dL (7.8 to 10.0 mmol/L), avoiding strict normoglycemia (80-110 mg/dL) due to high hypoglycemia risk.

</details>

<details><summary>What adverse complication was demonstrated in pediatric clinical trials (e.g., CHiP trial) evaluating tight glycemic control (80-110 mg/dL)?</summary>

A high incidence of severe iatrogenic hypoglycemia (<40 mg/dL), which caused secondary brain injury and increased adverse outcomes.

</details>

<details><summary>Is routine thyroid hormone replacement (T4 or T3) recommended for low T3/T4 levels in critically ill children?</summary>

No. Randomized trials show no clinical benefit in mortality or ICU length of stay, and guidelines recommend against routine replacement therapy.

</details>

<details><summary>What pattern of circulating growth hormone and IGF-1 characterizes the acute phase of critical illness?</summary>

Elevated circulating growth hormone accompanied by markedly depressed serum IGF-1 concentrations, reflecting acute peripheral hepatic growth hormone resistance.
