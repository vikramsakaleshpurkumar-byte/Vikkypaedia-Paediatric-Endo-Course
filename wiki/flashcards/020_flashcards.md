<details><summary>What gene is mutated in X-linked hypophosphatemic rickets (XLH), and what is its mode of inheritance?</summary>

PHEX gene (phosphate-regulating endopeptidase homolog, X-linked) on chromosome Xp22.1; inherited in an X-linked dominant manner.

</details>

<details><summary>How does elevated FGF23 affect renal tubular phosphate reabsorption and vitamin D metabolism?</summary>

FGF23 downregulates NaPi-IIa and NaPi-IIc cotransporters, causing renal phosphate wasting, and simultaneously inhibits 1-alpha-hydroxylase (CYP27B1) while inducing 24-hydroxylase (CYP24A1), lowering 1,25-(OH)2D.

PDF p. 508
</details>

<details><summary>What is burosumab, and what is its dosing schedule in pediatric patients with XLH?</summary>

A fully human IgG1 monoclonal antibody targeting intact FGF23; administered subcutaneously at 0.8 mg/kg every 2 weeks (titrated up to 2.0 mg/kg every 2 weeks based on serum phosphate).

PDF p. 516
</details>

<details><summary>Why is calcitriol contraindicated in hereditary hypophosphatemic rickets with hypercalciuria (HHRH; SLC34A3 mutation)?</summary>

In HHRH, FGF23 is suppressed, causing endogenous 1,25-(OH)2D to be markedly elevated; exogenous calcitriol would cause severe hypercalcemia, torrential hypercalciuria, and nephrolithiasis.

PDF p. 520
</details>

<details><summary>What distinctive dental pathology frequently leads to the initial diagnosis of XLH in early childhood?</summary>

Spontaneous non-traumatic periapical dental abscesses occurring in caries-free primary teeth, caused by defective dentin mineralization with enlarged pulp chambers allowing bacterial tracking.

PDF p. 514
</details>

<details><summary>What diagnostic calculation establishes renal phosphate wasting in a hypophosphatemic child?</summary>

Reduced tubular maximum reabsorption of phosphate per unit GFR (TmP/GFR), calculated from fasting simultaneous serum and urine phosphate and creatinine concentrations.

PDF p. 507
</details>

<details><summary>What molecular defects cause Hyperphosphatemic Familial Tumoral Calcinosis (HFTC)?</summary>

Inactivating biallelic mutations in FGF23, GALNT3 (polypeptide N-acetylgalactosaminyltransferase 3), or KL (Klotho), resulting in FGF23 deficiency/resistance, severe hyperphosphatemia, and ectopic periarticular calcifications.

</details>
