<details><summary>What three biochemical criteria define diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) in a pediatric patient?</summary>

Hyperglycemia (blood glucose >200 mg/dL), metabolic acidosis (venous pH <7.30 or bicarbonate <15-18 mmol/L), and ketosis (blood BOHB >=3.0 mmol/L or moderate/large ketonuria).

</details>

<details><summary>Why is an intravenous bolus of regular insulin strictly contraindicated when initiating DKA therapy?</summary>

An IV insulin bolus precipitates rapid osmotic fluid shifts and acute hypokalemia, significantly increasing the risk of fatal cerebral edema; continuous infusion without a bolus is mandatory.

PDF p. 823
</details>

<details><summary>What is the emergency medical therapy for acute cerebral edema complicating pediatric DKA?</summary>

Intravenous 3% hypertonic saline (3 to 5 mL/kg over 10-15 minutes) or intravenous 20% mannitol (0.5 to 1.0 g/kg over 20 minutes), administered immediately without awaiting head CT.

PDF p. 826
</details>

<details><summary>Which islet autoantibodies are standardly evaluated to confirm the autoimmune etiology of type 1 diabetes?</summary>

GAD65 (glutamic acid decarboxylase-65), IA-2 (insulinoma-associated protein-2), IAA (insulin autoantibodies), and ZnT8 (zinc transporter 8).

PDF p. 810
</details>

<details><summary>At what circulating blood glucose threshold should dextrose (5-10%) be added to intravenous fluids during DKA management?</summary>

When blood glucose decreases to 250 to 300 mg/dL, in order to maintain glucose between 150-200 mg/dL while continuing insulin to clear acidosis and ketosis.

PDF p. 824
</details>

<details><summary>What are the internationally recommended Continuous Glucose Monitoring (CGM) targets for youth with type 1 diabetes?</summary>

Time in Range (TIR 70-180 mg/dL) >70%, Time Below Range (<70 mg/dL) <4%, and Time Below 54 mg/dL <1%.

PDF p. 817
</details>

<details><summary>Which two autoimmune conditions require regular routine screening in all children with type 1 diabetes?</summary>

Autoimmune thyroid disease (Hashimoto thyroiditis, screening with TSH and anti-TPO) and Celiac disease (screening with tissue transglutaminase IgA [tTG-IgA] and total IgA).

</details>
