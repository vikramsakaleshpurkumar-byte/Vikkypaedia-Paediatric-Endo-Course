<details><summary>What is the recommended dose and administration protocol for intravenous calcium gluconate in acute symptomatic hypocalcemia with tetany or seizures?</summary>

Intravenous 10% calcium gluconate at 1-2 mL/kg (100-200 mg/kg; maximum 1-2 g or 10-20 mL) infused slowly over 10 to 20 minutes under continuous ECG monitoring to avoid bradycardia or cardiac arrest.

<details><summary>What is the targeted serum calcium level in children with chronic hypoparathyroidism on calcitriol and oral calcium therapy?</summary>

Low-normal serum calcium (8.5-9.0 mg/dL), keeping the 24-hour urine calcium excretion <4 mg/kg/day (urine Ca/Cr ratio <0.2) to prevent nephrocalcinosis and renal impairment.

<details><summary>What biochemical index distinguishes familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia (FHH) from primary hyperparathyroidism, and what is its cutoff value?</summary>

The urinary calcium-to-creatinine clearance ratio (CCCR); a ratio <0.01 strongly indicates FHH, whereas primary hyperparathyroidism typically displays CCCR >0.02.

</details>

<details><summary>What receptor mutation is responsible for familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia (FHH) and neonatal severe primary hyperparathyroidism (NSHPT)?</summary>

Heterozygous loss-of-function/inactivating mutations in the CaSR gene cause FHH; homozygous or compound heterozygous inactivating mutations cause life-threatening NSHPT.

<details><summary>What characteristic electrocardiographic intervals reflect acute hypocalcemia and severe acute hypercalcemia, respectively?</summary>

Acute hypocalcemia produces prolonged QTc interval (prolonged ST segment), whereas hypercalcemia produces shortened QTc interval.

<details><summary>What is the mechanism of hypercalcemia associated with subcutaneous fat necrosis of the newborn?</summary>

Unregulated extra-renal production of 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (calcitriol) by activated macrophages within the inflammatory subcutaneous granulomas.

</details>

<details><summary>Which classes of medications are utilized in the acute inpatient treatment of severe symptomatic hypercalcemia (calcium &gt;14 mg/dL)?</summary>

Vigorous IV 0.9% normal saline hyperhydration, loop diuretics (furosemide, only after volume restoration), IV bisphosphonates (zoledronic acid or pamidronate), and calcitonin.
