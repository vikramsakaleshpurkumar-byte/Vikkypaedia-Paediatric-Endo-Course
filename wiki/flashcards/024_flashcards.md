<details><summary>What chronological age cutoffs define delayed puberty in girls and boys, respectively?</summary>

Lack of breast development (Tanner 2 thelarche) by age 13.0 years in girls; lack of testicular enlargement (volume <4 mL) by age 14.0 years in boys.

<details><summary>What clinical and radiographic findings characterize constitutional delay of growth and puberty (CDGP)?</summary>

Family history of delayed puberty, delayed bone age matching height age, prepubertal growth velocity appropriate for bone age, normal predicted adult height, and eventual spontaneous pubertal completion.

</details>

<details><summary>What clinical feature uniquely differentiates Kallmann syndrome from normosmic congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism?</summary>

Anosmia or hyposmia (loss of smell), caused by embryonic failure of GnRH neurons and olfactory axons to migrate together from the olfactory placode to the hypothalamus.

</details>

<details><summary>What are the classic karyotypes and gonadal features of Turner syndrome and Klinefelter syndrome?</summary>

Turner syndrome is 45,X with streak gonads and ovarian failure; Klinefelter syndrome is 47,XXY with small, firm testes (<4 mL) and hypergonadotropic azoospermia.

</details>

<details><summary>Why must progesterone therapy be delayed for at least 18-24 months when inducing puberty with estrogen in girls with primary hypogonadism?</summary>

Premature progesterone exposure antagonizes estrogen-stimulated ductile branching, resulting in abnormal, tubular breast morphology, and interrupts maximal uterine growth.

</details>

<details><summary>What is the initial low-dose depot testosterone regimen used to promote pubertal onset in adolescent boys with constitutional delay?</summary>

Intramuscular testosterone enanthate or cypionate 25 to 50 mg once monthly for 3 to 6 months, which accelerates linear growth and secondary sex characteristics without premature epiphyseal fusion.

</details>

<details><summary>How does serum gonadotropin (LH/FSH) measurement differentiate primary from secondary hypogonadism?</summary>

Elevated LH and FSH indicate primary (hypergonadotropic) gonadal failure, whereas low or inappropriately normal LH and FSH indicate central (hypogonadotropic) failure.
