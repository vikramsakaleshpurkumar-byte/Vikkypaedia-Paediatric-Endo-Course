<details><summary>What statistical criteria define an infant as born Small for Gestational Age (SGA)?</summary>

Birth weight and/or birth length at least 2 standard deviations below the mean (SDS ≤ -2.0, <2.3rd percentile) for gestational age and sex.

PDF p. 113
</details>

<details><summary>What percentage of children born SGA experience spontaneous catch-up growth to normal height by 2 years of age?</summary>

Approximately 85% to 90%; the remaining 10% to 15% fail to catch up and are candidates for evaluation.

PDF p. 113
</details>

<details><summary>What age and height criteria are required by the US FDA for initiating somatropin in a child born SGA?</summary>

Age ≥ 2 years with height SDS ≤ -2.0 and documented failure to manifest catch-up growth.

PDF p. 122
</details>

<details><summary>What is the approved somatropin dosage range for children born SGA without catch-up growth?</summary>

0.24 to 0.48 mg/kg/week (approximately 35 to 70 mcg/kg/day) given by daily subcutaneous injection.

PDF p. 123
</details>

<details><summary>Which specific genetic syndrome presents with severe intrauterine growth restriction, body asymmetry, triangular facies, and fifth finger clinodactyly?</summary>

Silver-Russell syndrome, associated with loss of methylation at 11p15.5 or maternal uniparental disomy for chromosome 7.

PDF p. 116
</details>

<details><summary>What long-term adult metabolic risks are increased in individuals born SGA who experience rapid post-infancy weight catch-up?</summary>

Metabolic syndrome, insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes mellitus, visceral obesity, and premature cardiovascular disease.

PDF p. 126
</details>

<details><summary>What circulating biochemical marker is routinely monitored during somatropin therapy in SGA children to adjust dosage safely?</summary>

Serum IGF-I concentration, maintaining levels within +1 to +2 SDS for age and pubertal stage.

PDF p. 124
</details>
