<details><summary>What is the most frequent overall developmental cause of permanent congenital hypothyroidism, and what is its most common anatomical subtype?</summary>

Thyroid dysgenesis accounts for 80-85% of permanent primary congenital hypothyroidism, with ectopic thyroid (sublingual/lingual gland located along the migratory path) being the most common subtype (responsible for ~60% of dysgenesis cases).

<details><summary>What is the recommended initial daily starting dose of oral levothyroxine in neonates with confirmed congenital hypothyroidism?</summary>

10-15 mcg/kg/day (equivalent to 37.5 to 50 mcg/day for most term infants), aimed at rapidly restoring serum free T4 into the upper half of the normal reference range within 3-7 days.

</details>

<details><summary>Why is routine neonatal blood-spot screening for congenital hypothyroidism ideally obtained between 24 and 48 hours of life rather than immediately at birth?</summary>

A physiological surge in neonatal TSH occurs within 30 minutes of birth, peaking up to 60-80 mIU/L and declining over the subsequent 24-48 hours. Testing during this initial surge produces high rates of false-positive TSH elevations.

PDF p. 384
</details>

<details><summary>Which infant dietary components and nutritional supplements significantly impair the gastrointestinal absorption of crushed oral levothyroxine?</summary>

Soy-based infant formulas, oral iron supplements (ferrous sulfate), oral calcium carbonate/citrate, and aluminum-containing medications chelate levothyroxine in the gut and must not be administered concurrently.

</details>

<details><summary>What diagnostic radiologic sign on a newborn knee film indicates significant intra-uterine prenatal thyroid hormone deficiency?</summary>

Absence of the distal femoral epiphysis (which normally ossifies by 36-37 weeks of gestation) reflects severe, long-standing prenatal hypothyroidism in term neonates.

PDF p. 386
</details>

<details><summary>What maternal autoimmune factor causes transient primary congenital hypothyroidism in offspring of mothers with autoimmune thyroid disease?</summary>

Transplacental passage of maternal TSH receptor-blocking antibodies (TRBAb), which inhibit fetal TSH receptor activation and resolve spontaneously over 2-4 months as maternal IgG antibodies clear.

</details>

<details><summary>At what age and under what conditions should a trial off levothyroxine be performed to establish whether congenital hypothyroidism is permanent?</summary>

At age 3 years, provided thyroid tissue was identified in situ on neonatal imaging and dysgenesis/permanent genetic mutations have not been confirmed; levothyroxine is discontinued for 30 days to measure serum TSH and free T4.
