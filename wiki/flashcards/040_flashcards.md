<details><summary>What is the emergency intravenous hydrocortisone bolus dose for an acute adrenal crisis in children based on age?</summary>

<3 years: 25 mg; 3 to 12 years: 50 mg; >12 years: 100 mg IV bolus (or 50-100 mg/m2 IV), administered immediately alongside normal saline and dextrose.

<details><summary>Why must Lugol&#x27;s iodine solution be given at least 1 hour AFTER methimazole when treating thyroid storm?</summary>

To ensure that methimazole has blocked thyroid peroxidase first; administering iodine before organification is blocked provides substrate that synthesizes more thyroid hormone (Jod-Basedow effect).

PDF p. 1054
</details>

<details><summary>Why is aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) strictly contraindicated in the management of hyperpyrexia during thyroid storm?</summary>

Aspirin displaces thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3) from thyroxine-binding globulin (TBG), sharply increasing free active thyroid hormone concentrations and worsening thyrotoxic storm.

PDF p. 1054
</details>

<details><summary>Why is 10% calcium gluconate preferred over 10% calcium chloride for peripheral intravenous treatment of acute hypocalcemia?</summary>

Calcium chloride is highly irritating to veins and causes severe full-thickness tissue necrosis and sloughing if extravasation occurs; calcium gluconate carries a much lower tissue toxicity profile.

</details>

<details><summary>What is the immediate bedside pharmacological treatment for cerebral edema complicating pediatric DKA?</summary>

Intravenous 3% hypertonic saline (3-5 mL/kg over 10-15 minutes) or 20% mannitol (0.5-1.0 g/kg over 20 minutes) given immediately without awaiting imaging.

</details>

<details><summary>What is the emergency rescue dose of glucagon for severe hypoglycemia in an unconscious child with type 1 diabetes?</summary>

0.5 mg SC/IM for children <25 kg (or <6 years) and 1.0 mg SC/IM for children >=25 kg (or >=6 years); alternatively, 3 mg nasal glucagon for age >=4 years.

</details>

<details><summary>What is the first and most critical resuscitation step in the management of acute hypercalcemic crisis (calcium &gt;14 mg/dL)?</summary>

Vigorous volume expansion with intravenous 0.9% normal saline at 2 to 3 times maintenance to restore renal perfusion and induce calciuresis.
