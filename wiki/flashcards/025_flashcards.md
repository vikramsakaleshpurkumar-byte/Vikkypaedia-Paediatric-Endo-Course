<details><summary>What chronological age cutoffs define precocious puberty in girls and boys, respectively?</summary>

Onset of secondary sexual characteristics before age 8.0 years in girls (thelarche or pubarche) or before age 9.0 years in boys (testicular volume >=4 mL).

<details><summary>How does the likelihood of an identifiable central nervous system etiology in central precocious puberty differ between boys and girls?</summary>

In girls, >85-90% of CPP is idiopathic; in boys, 40-75% of CPP is secondary to an identifiable intracranial lesion, mandating routine brain MRI in all affected males.

</details>

<details><summary>What biochemical result on a GnRH stimulation test confirms the diagnosis of central precocious puberty?</summary>

A post-stimulation peak LH concentration >5.0 IU/L (or an ultrasensitive basal morning LH >0.2-0.3 IU/L), demonstrating activation of the pituitary gonadotropes.

PDF p. 708
</details>

<details><summary>What classic neurological seizure type is uniquely associated with hypothalamic hamartomas in young children with central precocious puberty?</summary>

Gelastic seizures, characterized by involuntary, paroxysmal, unprovoked bouts of hollow laughter or smirking, often emerging in infancy.

</details>

<details><summary>What gene mutation is the most frequent cause of familial central precocious puberty, and what is its mode of transmission?</summary>

Loss-of-function mutations in MKRN3 (makorin ring finger protein 3), a maternally imprinted gene that causes CPP only when inherited from the father (paternal transmission).

PDF p. 699
</details>

<details><summary>What is the primary mechanism of action of long-acting GnRH agonists (e.g., leuprolide, histrelin) in treating central precocious puberty?</summary>

Continuous, non-pulsatile stimulation of pituitary GnRH receptors, causing receptor downregulation, uncoupling, and profound suppression of LH and FSH secretion.

PDF p. 712
</details>

<details><summary>What triad of clinical features defines McCune-Albright syndrome presenting with peripheral precocious puberty?</summary>

Autonomous recurrent estrogen-secreting ovarian follicular cysts, large café-au-lait macules with irregular 'coast of Maine' borders, and polyostotic fibrous dysplasia of bone.
