<details><summary>What two mandatory criteria are required to establish the diagnosis of PCOS in an adolescent?</summary>

Both (1) Ovulatory dysfunction / menstrual irregularity abnormal for gynecological age AND (2) Clinical and/or biochemical hyperandrogenism; ultrasound morphology is excluded.

PDF p. 758
</details>

<details><summary>Why is polycystic ovarian morphology on ultrasound excluded from the diagnostic criteria for PCOS in adolescents?</summary>

Multifollicular ovarian morphology is a normal, physiological finding in up to 30-40% of healthy, ovulating post-menarchal teenagers and has high false-positive rates.

PDF p. 758
</details>

<details><summary>What is the first-line medical therapy for menstrual irregularity and hyperandrogenism in adolescent PCOS?</summary>

Combined oral contraceptives (COCs) containing low-dose ethinyl estradiol and a non-androgenic progestin, which restore regular bleeding, raise SHBG, and lower free testosterone.

PDF p. 766
</details>

<details><summary>What baseline test is used to rule out non-classic congenital adrenal hyperplasia (NCAH) in an adolescent with hyperandrogenism?</summary>

Early morning (8 AM) follicular-phase serum 17-hydroxyprogesterone (17-OHP); a value <200 ng/dL reliably excludes 21-hydroxylase deficiency NCAH.

PDF p. 761
</details>

<details><summary>What antiandrogenic medication is added to COCs if moderate-to-severe hirsutism does not improve after 6 months, and what is its dosage?</summary>

Spironolactone at 50 to 100 mg orally twice daily (must be paired with contraception due to the risk of feminization of a male fetus).

PDF p. 767
</details>

<details><summary>What are the acceptable lower and upper limits of menstrual cycle length in adolescents during the first 3 years after menarche?</summary>

21 to 45 days; any cycle shorter than 21 days or longer than 45 days (or an interval >90 days) is clinically abnormal.

PDF p. 752
</details>

<details><summary>What clinical and biochemical &#x27;red flags&#x27; suggest an androgen-secreting neoplasm rather than adolescent PCOS?</summary>

Rapid virilization (clitoromegaly, voice deepening, temporal balding) accompanied by serum total testosterone >150-200 ng/dL or DHEAS >700-800 mcg/dL.

</details>
