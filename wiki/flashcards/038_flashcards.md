<details><summary>What is the most radio-sensitive anterior pituitary hormone axis, exhibiting deficiency at the lowest cranial radiation doses (&gt;=18-24 Gy)?</summary>

Growth hormone (GH) axis, resulting in radiation-induced GH deficiency in >80-90% of exposed children within 2-5 years.

</details>

<details><summary>What pubertal disorder can paradoxically occur in young girls following low-to-moderate doses (18-24 Gy) of cranial irradiation?</summary>

Central precocious puberty, caused by radiation-induced disruption of cortical and hypothalamic inhibitory pathways that normally suppress the GnRH pulse generator.

PDF p. 1005
</details>

<details><summary>What metric is used to calculate cumulative alkylating chemotherapy exposure, and what threshold confers high risk of permanent azoospermia?</summary>

Cyclophosphamide Equivalent Dose (CED); a cumulative CED >=4000 to 8000 mg/m2 is associated with high risk of permanent germ cell failure and azoospermia.

PDF p. 1009
</details>

<details><summary>What is the most common secondary solid endocrine malignancy occurring in survivors of childhood cancer exposed to neck radiation?</summary>

Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC), which has an increased incidence beginning 5-10 years post-radiation and peaking 15-25 years later.

PDF p. 1006
</details>

<details><summary>Does recombinant human growth hormone replacement increase the risk of primary disease recurrence in childhood cancer survivors in remission?</summary>

No. Large-scale international cohort studies show no increased risk of primary cancer recurrence or leukemia relapse in survivors treated with rhGH.

PDF p. 1012
</details>

<details><summary>How does radiation sensitivity differ between testicular germ cells (spermatogenesis) and Leydig cells (testosterone production)?</summary>

Germ cells are extraordinarily sensitive (damaged by >=0.1-1.2 Gy), whereas Leydig cells are much more radioresistant (tolerating up to 20-24 Gy before primary hypogonadism develops).

PDF p. 1009
</details>

<details><summary>Why is lifelong surveillance of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis required in survivors exposed to high-dose cranial radiation (&gt;=40-50 Gy)?</summary>

Central ACTH deficiency can develop insidiously 10 to 15+ years after radiotherapy, predisposing survivors to fatal adrenal crisis during intercurrent illness.

</details>
