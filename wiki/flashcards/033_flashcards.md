<details><summary>What characteristic discordance occurs in the growth hormone axis in patients with anorexia nervosa?</summary>

Elevated circulating growth hormone (GH) levels paired with low serum IGF-1 concentrations, reflecting starvation-induced hepatic growth hormone resistance.

<details><summary>What thyroid hormone pattern defines the non-thyroidal illness syndrome seen in anorexia nervosa, and should it be treated?</summary>

Low total and free T3, elevated reverse T3, and normal/low-normal T4 and TSH ('low T3 syndrome'); it is an adaptive conservation state and thyroid hormone replacement is contraindicated.

</details>

<details><summary>Why is transdermal 17-beta estradiol superior to combined oral contraceptive pills for restoring bone density in adolescents with anorexia nervosa?</summary>

Transdermal estradiol bypasses hepatic first-pass metabolism, preserving liver IGF-1 synthesis, whereas oral ethinyl estradiol suppresses hepatic IGF-1, preventing bone accretion.

</details>

<details><summary>How does &#x27;pseudo-GH deficiency&#x27; in childhood obesity differ biochemically from true organic growth hormone deficiency?</summary>

Obese children show blunted GH peaks on provocative stimulation testing, but have normal serum IGF-1 levels and normal or accelerated linear growth velocity.

</details>

<details><summary>What circulating alterations in leptin and adiponectin characterize obesity-associated metabolic dysfunction?</summary>

Elevated serum leptin (reflecting adipocyte expansion and central leptin resistance) and decreased serum adiponectin.

<details><summary>How does the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis respond to chronic starvation in anorexia nervosa?</summary>

Hypercortisolemia develops with elevated 24-hour urinary free cortisol and loss of diurnal rhythm, driven by increased hypothalamic CRH drive and impaired hepatic cortisol breakdown.

</details>
