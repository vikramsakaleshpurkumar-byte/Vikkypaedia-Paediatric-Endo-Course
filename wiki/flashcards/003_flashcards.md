<details><summary>How does bone age typically differ between Constitutional Delay of Growth and Puberty (CDGP) and Familial Short Stature (FSS)?</summary>

In CDGP, bone age is significantly delayed relative to chronological age; in FSS, bone age is consistent with chronological age.

<details><summary>How is midparental target height calculated for a boy and a girl?</summary>

Boy: [Father's height + (Mother's height + 13 cm)] / 2. Girl: [(Father's height - 13 cm) + Mother's height] / 2.

</details>

<details><summary>What height threshold defines Idiopathic Short Stature (ISS) for consideration of growth hormone therapy?</summary>

Height more than 2.25 standard deviations below the mean (height SDS <-2.25 or approximately below the 1.2th percentile) with poor predicted adult height.

PDF p. 97
</details>

<details><summary>What regimen of low-dose androgen is used to jumpstart growth in adolescent boys with constitutional delay and severe psychosocial distress?</summary>

Testosterone enanthate or cypionate 50 to 100 mg intramuscularly once monthly for 3 to 6 months.

</details>

<details><summary>What is the typical FDA-approved somatropin dose range for idiopathic short stature?</summary>

0.30 to 0.37 mg/kg/week (approximately 43 to 53 mcg/kg/day) given by daily subcutaneous injection.

</details>

<details><summary>What class of medication is sometimes combined with GH in adolescent males with short stature to slow bone age maturation?</summary>

Aromatase inhibitors (e.g., anastrozole, letrozole), which block the conversion of androgens to estrogens in growth plate chondrocytes.

</details>

<details><summary>Why is a karyotype or chromosomal microarray indicated in every girl presenting with unexplained short stature, even without dysmorphic features?</summary>

To rule out Turner syndrome (such as mosaic 45,X/46,XX), where short stature may be the only presenting physical feature in up to 10% of cases.
