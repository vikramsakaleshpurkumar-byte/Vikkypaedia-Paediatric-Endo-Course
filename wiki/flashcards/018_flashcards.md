<details><summary>How does the malignancy risk of a discrete thyroid nodule in a pediatric patient compare with that in an adult?</summary>

A discrete pediatric thyroid nodule carries a significantly higher malignancy rate: ~22-26% in children compared to only 5-10% in adults.

</details>

<details><summary>Which ultrasound characteristics of a thyroid nodule are most strongly associated with malignancy?</summary>

Marked hypoechogenicity, microcalcifications, irregular/infiltrative margins, taller-than-wide geometry, absence of a halo, extrathyroidal extension, and abnormal cervical lymph nodes.

PDF p. 456
</details>

<details><summary>When is ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration (FNA) biopsy indicated for a pediatric thyroid nodule?</summary>

For any solid nodule >=1 cm with suspicious sonographic features, any nodule associated with suspicious cervical lymph nodes, or a growing nodule in high-risk patients (e.g., prior head/neck radiation).

PDF p. 459
</details>

<details><summary>What is the most prevalent histological type of differentiated thyroid cancer in pediatric patients, and what is its typical pattern of metastatic spread?</summary>

Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC, 80-90% of cases); it characteristically spreads early to regional cervical lymph nodes (40-80%) and hematogenously to the lungs (10-20%).

PDF p. 462
</details>

<details><summary>What germline mutation causes hereditary medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) in MEN2A and MEN2B, and what is the timing for prophylactic thyroidectomy in MEN2B?</summary>

Gain-of-function RET proto-oncogene mutations; in MEN2B (codon M918T), prophylactic total thyroidectomy is recommended during the first year of life (within the first months of life) due to virulent early metastatic spread.

PDF p. 472
</details>

<details><summary>What is the recommended surgical extent for pediatric differentiated thyroid cancer to minimize recurrence and facilitate radioiodine ablation?</summary>

Total or near-total thyroidectomy coupled with central neck (level VI) lymph node dissection performed by a high-volume pediatric thyroid surgeon.

PDF p. 466
</details>

<details><summary>What serum protein serves as the primary tumor marker for surveillance following total thyroidectomy and 131I ablation in differentiated thyroid cancer?</summary>

Serum thyroglobulin (Tg), monitored alongside anti-thyroglobulin (anti-Tg) antibodies (which can falsely suppress immunoassay measurements of Tg).

</details>
