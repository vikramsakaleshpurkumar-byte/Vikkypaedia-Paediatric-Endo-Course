<details><summary>At what two specific pediatric age intervals is universal lipid screening officially recommended by the AAP and NHLBI?</summary>

Once between ages 9 and 11 years, and once between ages 17 and 21 years.

<details><summary>What is the defined threshold for an &#x27;elevated&#x27; LDL cholesterol level in children and adolescents?</summary>

LDL-C >=130 mg/dL (>=3.4 mmol/L); acceptable is <110 mg/dL, and borderline is 110-129 mg/dL.

</details>

<details><summary>At what persistent LDL-C level is statin pharmacotherapy indicated in a 12-year-old child without other cardiovascular risk factors?</summary>

LDL-C persistently >=190 mg/dL despite a 6-month trial of dietary and lifestyle modification.

PDF p. 874
</details>

<details><summary>What circulating triglyceride threshold indicates high risk for acute pancreatitis requiring immediate medical and dietary intervention?</summary>

Serum triglycerides >=1000 mg/dL (and especially >2000 mg/dL), representing massive chylomicronemia.

PDF p. 868
</details>

<details><summary>What is the most common gene mutated in autosomal dominant heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH)?</summary>

The LDLR gene (encoding the low-density lipoprotein receptor), accounting for >85-90% of identified mutations.

PDF p. 865
</details>

<details><summary>What physical examination finding is pathognomonic for severe heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia in older children?</summary>

Tendon xanthomas, particularly nodular thickening of the Achilles tendons and extensor tendons of the hands.

PDF p. 866
</details>

<details><summary>What is the foundational dietary therapy for children with familial chylomicronemia syndrome (LPL deficiency)?</summary>

Extreme restriction of dietary fat to less than 10-15% of total daily caloric intake (typically <15-20 grams of fat per day).
