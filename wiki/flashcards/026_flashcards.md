<details><summary>What is the most urgent medical emergency to exclude in a newborn infant presenting with atypical genitalia and bilateral non-palpable gonads?</summary>

Salt-wasting congenital adrenal hyperplasia (21-hydroxylase deficiency) in a virilized 46,XX infant, which can precipitate fatal salt-wasting shock within 1-2 weeks of life.

<details><summary>Which testicular hormone is responsible for the regression of Müllerian ducts (uterus, fallopian tubes, upper vagina) in male fetal development?</summary>

<details><summary>What biochemical test and ratio confirm the diagnosis of 5-alpha-reductase type 2 deficiency in an infant with 46,XY DSD?</summary>

An hCG stimulation test demonstrating an abnormally elevated plasma testosterone to dihydrotestosterone (T:DHT) ratio (>20-30:1).

<details><summary>What internal and external anatomical findings characterize an adolescent with Complete Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome (CAIS)?</summary>

Phenotypic female external genitalia with a blind-ending vagina, absent uterus and fallopian tubes, absent pubic/axillary hair, normal female breast development, and testes located in the abdomen or labia.

</details>

<details><summary>Why is prophylactic gonadectomy recommended in individuals with 45,X/46,XY mixed gonadal dysgenesis who have intra-abdominal dysgenetic gonads?</summary>

Dysgenetic gonadal tissue containing Y-chromosomal material (TSPY) carries a 15-40% lifetime risk of developing malignant germ cell tumors (gonadoblastoma and dysgerminoma).

</details>

<details><summary>What clinical staging scale is standardly utilized to quantify the degree of genital virilization in female infants with CAH?</summary>

The Prader staging scale (Prader stages 1 to 5, ranging from mild clitoromegaly [stage 1] to a completely virilized penile urethra with fused scrotum and absent palpable gonads [stage 5]).

</details>
