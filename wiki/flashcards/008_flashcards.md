<details><summary>What specific genetic mutation accounts for more than 98% of cases of achondroplasia?</summary>

A heterozygous gain-of-function mutation in FGFR3, most commonly c.1138G>A (p.Gly380Arg), resulting in constitutive activation of the receptor tyrosine kinase.

</details>

<details><summary>What pattern of limb shortening is characteristically observed in achondroplasia?</summary>

Rhizomelic shortening, where proximal segments (femur and humerus) are disproportionately shorter than middle or distal segments.

PDF p. 234
</details>

<details><summary>What is the mechanism of action of vosoritide, the FDA-approved medical therapy for achondroplasia?</summary>

It is a recombinant C-type natriuretic peptide (CNP) analog that binds NPR-B, stimulating cGMP to inhibit the hyperactive MAPK/ERK pathway downstream of mutated FGFR3.

</details>

<details><summary>What anatomical narrowing in infants with achondroplasia carries a significant risk of cervicomedullary compression and sudden death?</summary>

Foramen magnum stenosis, requiring baseline polysomnography and high-resolution neuroimaging (CT/MRI) of the craniocervical junction.

</details>

<details><summary>What pathognomonic finding is seen on anteroposterior lumbar spine radiography in achondroplasia?</summary>

Progressive caudal narrowing of the interpedicular distance from L1 to L5 (in contrast to normal widening).

</details>

<details><summary>Mutations in COL1A1 and COL1A2 cause what skeletal disorder characterized by bone fragility and blue sclerae?</summary>

Osteogenesis imperfecta (OI), ranging from perinatal lethal type II to mild non-deforming type I.

</details>

<details><summary>What distinct physical feature of the hands is typical in achondroplasia?</summary>

A 'trident hand' configuration, characterized by short, broad digits with persistent divergence between the third and fourth fingers.
