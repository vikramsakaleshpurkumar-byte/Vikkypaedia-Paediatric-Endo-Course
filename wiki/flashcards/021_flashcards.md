<details><summary>Why is serum calcium characteristically normal in Stage 2 calcipenic nutritional rickets?</summary>

Secondary hyperparathyroidism mobilizes calcium from bone and increases renal tubular reabsorption to normalize serum calcium, at the expense of PTH-induced renal phosphate wasting and hypophosphatemia.

</details>

<details><summary>What are the cardinal radiographic signs of active rickets seen at the distal radial and ulnar metaphyses?</summary>

Widening of the physis, fraying (ragged margins), cupping (concave metaphyseal contour), and splaying (transverse expansion) of the metaphysis, with loss of the provisional zone of calcification.

PDF p. 543
</details>

<details><summary>What is the recommended daily dosage and duration of oral vitamin D for treating nutritional rickets in children over 12 months of age?</summary>

3000 to 6000 IU daily of oral cholecalciferol (D3) or ergocalciferol (D2) for 8 to 12 weeks, combined with 500 mg daily of elemental calcium, followed by 400-600 IU daily maintenance.

PDF p. 548
</details>

<details><summary>Why must oral elemental calcium supplementation always accompany vitamin D therapy when initiating treatment for nutritional rickets?</summary>

Rapid skeletal remineralization ('hungry bone syndrome') drives circulating calcium into healing osteoid, which can precipitate severe, symptomatic hypocalcemia and tetany if calcium is not co-administered.

PDF p. 549
</details>

<details><summary>What enzymatic defect causes Vitamin D-Dependent Rickets Type 1A (VDDR1A), and what is its specific pharmacotherapy?</summary>

Inactivating biallelic mutations in CYP27B1 (renal 25-hydroxyvitamin D 1-alpha-hydroxylase); treated with physiological doses of active calcitriol (1,25-(OH)2D, 20-40 ng/kg/day).

PDF p. 545
</details>

<details><summary>What is craniotabes, and in what pediatric age group is it the earliest physical manifestation of rickets?</summary>

Softening and ping-pong ball-like indentability of the cranial vault bones (parietal/occipital); it is the earliest clinical sign in young infants under 3-6 months of age.

</details>

<details><summary>What is the earliest radiographic indicator of therapeutic response during medical treatment of rickets?</summary>

Appearance of a dense radiopaque line of provisional calcification across the metaphyseal growth plate margin, typically visible within 2 to 4 weeks.
