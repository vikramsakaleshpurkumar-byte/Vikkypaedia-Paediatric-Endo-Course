<details><summary>Contrast the classic congenital cardiovascular anomalies characteristic of Turner syndrome versus Noonan syndrome.</summary>

Turner syndrome features left-sided lesions (bicuspid aortic valve, coarctation of the aorta, aortic root dilation); Noonan syndrome features right-sided and myocardial lesions (pulmonary valve stenosis, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy).

<details><summary>What specific gene located in the pseudoautosomal region (PAR1) of the X and Y chromosomes is responsible for the short stature in Turner syndrome and Léri-Weill dyschondrosteosis?</summary>

SHOX (Short Stature Homeobox gene), whose haploinsufficiency causes disproportionate short stature and mesomelia.

</details>

<details><summary>Mutations in which pathway cause Noonan syndrome and explain the relative resistance to growth hormone?</summary>

The RAS-MAPK signal transduction pathway (most commonly PTPN11 gain-of-function mutations encoding SHP2), which impairs post-receptor IGF-I generation.

</details>

<details><summary>What non-aromatizable androgen can be co-administered with somatropin in Turner syndrome girls older than 9-10 years to augment final adult height?</summary>

Oxandrolone, typically administered at a low dose of 0.03 to 0.05 mg/kg/day to minimize virilizing side effects.

<details><summary>What characteristic wrist abnormality is observed in adolescent females with SHOX mutations and Léri-Weill dyschondrosteosis?</summary>

Madelung deformity, featuring bowing of the distal radius, dorsal subluxation of the distal ulna, and triangular carpal wedging.

<details><summary>Which autoimmune conditions occur with increased frequency in girls with Turner syndrome, requiring annual laboratory screening?</summary>

Autoimmune Hashimoto thyroiditis (hypothyroidism) and celiac disease.
