<details><summary>What is the first-line pharmacological treatment for pediatric macroprolactinomas, and what is its dosing schedule?</summary>

Cabergoline, a selective D2 dopamine receptor agonist, administered orally at 0.25 to 0.5 mg once or twice weekly.

<details><summary>What is the &#x27;hook effect&#x27; in prolactin immunoassays, and how is it resolved in patients with large pituitary macroadenomas?</summary>

Extremely high prolactin levels (>10,000 mcg/L) saturate assay antibodies, causing falsely low measured prolactin; it is resolved by performing assay testing on a 1:100 diluted serum sample.

</details>

<details><summary>What is the typical upper limit of serum prolactin elevation caused by the &#x27;stalk effect&#x27; (pituitary stalk compression by non-prolactinoma tumors)?</summary>

Serum prolactin is almost universally less than 100 to 150 mcg/L (ng/mL); prolactin concentrations >200-250 mcg/L indicate a true prolactinoma.

</details>

<details><summary>What is macroprolactinemia, and what chemical laboratory method is used to diagnose it?</summary>

Circulating biologically inactive prolactin-IgG autoantibody complexes; diagnosed by polyethylene glycol (PEG) serum precipitation to measure free monomeric prolactin.

</details>

<details><summary>Why does severe untreated primary hypothyroidism frequently cause hyperprolactinemia?</summary>

Elevated hypothalamic thyrotropin-releasing hormone (TRH) acts as a prolactin-releasing factor, directly stimulating pituitary lactotropes to synthesize and release prolactin.

</details>

<details><summary>What are the most frequent presenting manifestations of macroprolactinomas in adolescent males?</summary>

Headaches, bitemporal visual field loss, delayed puberty, arrested linear growth, and hypogonadism; galactorrhea is uncommon in males.

</details>

<details><summary>When is transsphenoidal neurosurgery indicated in pediatric prolactinomas instead of dopamine agonist therapy?</summary>

When tumors are completely refractory or intolerant to maximal dopamine agonist therapy, or in cases of acute pituitary apoplexy with sudden severe visual failure.
