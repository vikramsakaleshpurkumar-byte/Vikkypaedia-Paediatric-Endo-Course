<details><summary>Why is McCune-Albright syndrome never inherited or transmitted vertically from parent to child?</summary>

It is caused by postzygotic somatic mosaic mutations in GNAS; a germline activating GNAS mutation would cause generalized adenylyl cyclase activation that is embryonic lethal.

</details>

<details><summary>What specific biochemical defect results from the GNAS R201 codon mutation in McCune-Albright syndrome?</summary>

Loss of intrinsic GTPase activity in Gs-alpha, trapping the protein in an active GTP-bound conformation that constitutively drives adenylyl cyclase and cAMP production.

</details>

<details><summary>How do the café-au-lait macules of McCune-Albright syndrome differ morphologically from those of Neurofibromatosis type 1?</summary>

MAS macules are large, unilateral, respect the midline, and feature jagged, irregular 'coast of Maine' borders, unlike the smooth 'coast of California' borders of NF1.

</details>

<details><summary>Why are standard GnRH agonists ineffective as initial therapy for precocious puberty in young girls with McCune-Albright syndrome?</summary>

The precocious puberty is peripheral (gonadotropin-independent), driven by autonomous ovarian follicular cysts secreting estrogen independent of pituitary LH and FSH.

</details>

<details><summary>What metabolic electrolyte complication can severe, extensive polyostotic fibrous dysplasia cause via paracrine tumor secretion?</summary>

Renal phosphate wasting and hypophosphatemic rickets/osteomalacia, caused by excessive secretion of intact FGF23 by dysplastic bone stromal cells.

</details>

<details><summary>What pathognomonic femoral deformity frequently develops in children with severe proximal femoral fibrous dysplasia?</summary>

'Shepherd's crook' deformity (severe coxa vara with bowing of the proximal femoral shaft under weight-bearing stress).

</details>

<details><summary>What pharmacological class is the first-line medical treatment for peripheral precocious puberty in girls with McCune-Albright syndrome?</summary>

Aromatase inhibitors, such as oral letrozole (2.5 mg daily), which block estrogen biosynthesis by autonomous ovarian cysts.
