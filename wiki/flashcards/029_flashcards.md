Plasma glucose, insulin, C-peptide, beta-hydroxybutyrate (BOHB), free fatty acids (FFA), lactate, cortisol, growth hormone, acylcarnitine profile, and carnitine, plus urine for organic acids and ketones.

<details><summary>What glycemic response during a glucagon stimulation test at the time of hypoglycemia confirms hyperinsulinism?</summary>

An increase in plasma glucose >30 mg/dL within 40 minutes of glucagon administration (1 mg IV/IM), reflecting inappropriate hepatic glycogen retention mediated by insulin.

<details><summary>What is the mechanism of action of diazoxide in congenital hyperinsulinism, and what diuretic must be co-prescribed?</summary>

Diazoxide opens beta-cell K-ATP channels to hyperpolarize the membrane and inhibit insulin exocytosis; chlorothiazide must be co-administered to prevent fluid retention and pulmonary hypertension.

<details><summary>What genes encode the beta-cell K-ATP channel implicated in the most common forms of congenital hyperinsulinism?</summary>

ABCC8 (encoding the SUR1 regulatory subunit) and KCNJ11 (encoding the Kir6.2 pore-forming subunit) on chromosome 11p15.1.

<details><summary>How does fatty acid oxidation disorder (e.g., MCAD deficiency) present during fasting compared to ketotic hypoglycemia?</summary>

FAOD presents with hypoketotic hypoglycemia (low ketones despite fasting) with elevated free fatty acids and dicarboxylic aciduria, whereas ketotic hypoglycemia displays high ketones (BOHB >2.5-3.0 mmol/L).

</details>

<details><summary>What is the initial intravenous dextrose bolus dose and fluid choice for acute pediatric hypoglycemia?</summary>

Intravenous 10% dextrose (D10W) at 2 mL/kg (equivalent to 200 mg/kg elemental dextrose) infused over 2-3 minutes.
