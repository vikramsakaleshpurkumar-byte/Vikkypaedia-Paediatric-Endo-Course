# Module 1 Blueprint: Pediatric Growth Disorders and Skeletal Dysplasias

## Module identity

- Stable module ID and title: `MOD-01-GROWTH` — Pediatric Growth Disorders, Growth Hormone Axis, and Skeletal Dysplasias
- Source chapter IDs and PDF pages: Chapters 01 to 09 (PDF pp. 21–274, Printed pp. 3–262)
- Audience and intended setting: Pediatric endocrinology fellows, pediatric residents, general pediatricians, and clinical endocrinologists in outpatient and inpatient consultative settings.
- Need or problem addressed: Systematic evaluation of the child with severe short stature, growth failure, or pathognomonic dysmorphic features; differentiating non-pathological growth variants (CDGP, ISS) from organic endocrine disease (GHD, GHI/Laron) and syndromic/skeletal conditions (SGA, PWS, Turner, Noonan, SHOX, Achondroplasia); safely initiating and monitoring recombinant human growth hormone (rhGH), rhIGF-1, and vosoritide.
- Prerequisites: Core understanding of physiological linear growth, midparental target height calculation, Tanner staging, and standard radiographic bone age assessment.
- Estimated learning effort and basis: 4 to 6 hours of structured self-directed study across the 9 source chapters, 45 core pillars, 63 flashcards, and 45 single-best-answer board-style MCQs.
- Teaching levels supported and any limitation: Essentials, Clinical Application, and Deep Dive.
- Source edition/version: *Pediatric Endocrinology: A Practical Clinical Guide*, 4th Edition (2024), Springer Nature Switzerland AG. Editors: Sally Radovick & Madhusmita Misra.
- Evidence actually reviewed on / reviewer role / unresolved issues: Source chapters 1–9 reviewed in full; clinical management algorithms cross-referenced against current Pediatric Endocrine Society (PES) and Growth Hormone Research Society (GHRS) international guidelines.
- Suggested review date and reason: 2027 (standard 3-year guideline update cycle).

## Guided sequence

- Opening problem or clinical hook: A 4-year-old child presents with severe short stature (height -3.5 SD), truncal adiposity, microphallus, and fasting hypoglycemia. Does this represent isolated growth hormone deficiency, multiple pituitary hormone deficiencies, or growth hormone receptor insensitivity?
- Prior-knowledge check: Midparental target height calculation, standard bone age radiograph interpretation using Greulich & Pyle atlas, and physiological pulsatile GH secretion.
- Unit sequence:
  1. Unit 1: Childhood Growth Hormone Deficiency and Hypopituitarism (Ch 1)
  2. Unit 2: Growth Hormone Receptor Insensitivity & Post-Receptor Defects (Ch 2)
  3. Unit 3: Normal Variants: CDGP vs Idiopathic Short Stature (Ch 3)
  4. Unit 4: Small for Gestational Age (SGA) without Catch-Up Growth (Ch 4)
  5. Unit 5: Prader-Willi Syndrome & Pre-treatment Airway Clearance (Ch 5)
  6. Unit 6: Turner Syndrome, Noonan Syndrome, and SHOX Haploinsufficiency (Ch 6)
  7. Unit 7: Skeletal Dysplasias & Precision C-Type Natriuretic Peptide Therapy (Ch 8)
  8. Unit 8: Growth Hormone Excess, Acromegaly, and Overgrowth Syndromes (Ch 9)
- Application case: A 5-year-old boy with height -2.8 SD and normal birth weight whose stimulated GH peaks reach 4.2 ng/mL on arginine and clonidine. His brain MRI shows pituitary hypoplasia and ectopic posterior pituitary. Formulate his initial rhGH dosage, monitoring parameters, and screening for coexisting central hormone deficiencies.
- Common error: Initiating growth hormone in a syndromic child without evaluating coexisting adrenal insufficiency, or giving rhGH to a Prader-Willi patient without prior polysomnography to rule out upper airway obstruction.
- Summary and retrieval practice: Revisit the 5 core pillars of Chapter 1 and test active recall using flashcards `fc-001-1` through `fc-001-7`.
