# Module 6 Blueprint: Pediatric Metabolic Disorders and Diabetes

## Module identity

- Stable module ID and title: `MOD-06-METABOLIC` — Pediatric Metabolic Disorders, Hypoglycemia, Diabetes Mellitus, and Dyslipidemia
- Source chapter IDs and PDF pages: Chapters 29 to 33 (PDF pp. 792–920, Printed pp. 791–922)
- Audience and intended setting: Pediatric endocrinologists, emergency physicians, pediatric intensivists, primary care pediatricians, and certified diabetes educators.
- Need or problem addressed: Rapid diagnostic evaluation of infant hypoglycemia using critical samples; managing pediatric DKA while preventing fatal cerebral edema; treating youth-onset T2D and distinguishing it from T1D; screening and treating pediatric familial dyslipidemias; managing neuroendocrine sequelae of anorexia nervosa and childhood obesity.
- Prerequisites: Pancreatic islet physiology (insulin, glucagon), carbohydrate and fatty acid metabolism, counter-regulatory hormone responses, and lipid lipoprotein transport.
- Estimated learning effort and basis: 4 to 6 hours of structured self-directed study across 5 source chapters, 25 pillars, 35 flashcards, and 25 authored single-best-answer MCQs.
- Teaching levels supported: Essentials, Clinical Application, and Deep Dive.
- Source edition/version: *Pediatric Endocrinology: A Practical Clinical Guide*, 4th Edition (2024), Springer Nature Switzerland AG. Editors: Sally Radovick & Madhusmita Misra.
- Evidence actually reviewed on / reviewer role / unresolved issues: Chapters 29–33 reviewed against ISPAD Clinical Practice Consensus Guidelines on DKA, Type 1 Diabetes, and Type 2 Diabetes, as well as AAP/NHLBI Pediatric Dyslipidemia Guidelines.
- Suggested review date: 2027.

## Guided sequence

- Opening problem or clinical hook: An 8-year-old child presents with Kussmaul breathing, glucose 520 mg/dL, venous pH 7.12, and blood beta-hydroxybutyrate 6.2 mmol/L. Why is an intravenous insulin bolus strictly contraindicated in this child?
- Prior-knowledge check: DKA diagnostic triad, the 500 Rule for ICR, the 1800 Rule for ISF, and the pathophysiology of the beta-cell K-ATP channel.
- Unit sequence:
  1. Unit 1: Pediatric Hypoglycemia: Critical Sampling, CHI, and Inborn Errors (Ch 29)
  2. Unit 2: Type 1 Diabetes: Intensive Insulin Delivery, Automated Pumps, and DKA Resuscitation (Ch 30)
  3. Unit 3: Type 2 Diabetes in Youth: Rapid Beta-Cell Decline and HHS Emergencies (Ch 31)
  4. Unit 4: Disorders of Lipid Metabolism: Universal Screening and Familial Hypercholesterolemia (Ch 32)
  5. Unit 5: Neuroendocrine Sequelae of Anorexia Nervosa and Childhood Obesity (Ch 33)
- Application case: A 15-year-old girl with severe anorexia nervosa (BMI 14.8 kg/m2) has secondary amenorrhea and a lumbar spine DXA Z-score of -2.8. Why are standard oral contraceptive pills ineffective for restoring her bone density, and what specific estrogen-progesterone regimen should be prescribed?
- Common error: Administering an IV insulin bolus in pediatric DKA, or giving thyroid hormone to a patient with anorexia nervosa who has low T3.
- Summary and retrieval practice: Revisit the 5 core pillars of Chapters 29 and 30; test active recall with flashcards `fc-029-1` through `fc-030-7`.
