# Module 5 Blueprint: Pediatric Pubertal and Reproductive Disorders

## Module identity

- Stable module ID and title: `MOD-05-PUBERTY` — Pediatric Pubertal, Reproductive, and Gonadal Disorders
- Source chapter IDs and PDF pages: Chapters 24 to 28 (PDF pp. 667–790, Printed pp. 663–787)
- Audience and intended setting: Pediatric endocrinology fellows, adolescent medicine physicians, pediatric gynecologists, and pediatric residents.
- Need or problem addressed: Systematic workup of delayed vs precocious puberty; urgent evaluation and sex assignment principles in newborns with differences of sex development (DSD); differentiating adolescent PCOS from normal physiological anovulation; managing macroprolactinomas and pituitary stalk compression.
- Prerequisites: Embryology of sexual differentiation (SRY, AMH, testosterone, 5-alpha-reductase), hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis physiology, and Tanner pubertal staging.
- Estimated learning effort and basis: 4 to 5 hours of structured self-directed study across 5 source chapters, 25 pillars, 35 flashcards, and 25 authored single-best-answer MCQs.
- Teaching levels supported: Essentials, Clinical Application, and Deep Dive.
- Source edition/version: *Pediatric Endocrinology: A Practical Clinical Guide*, 4th Edition (2024), Springer Nature Switzerland AG. Editors: Sally Radovick & Madhusmita Misra.
- Evidence actually reviewed on / reviewer role / unresolved issues: Chapters 24–28 reviewed against Chicago Consensus on DSD, International Evidence-based Guideline for the Assessment and Management of PCOS, and Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guidelines on Hyperprolactinemia.
- Suggested review date: 2027.

## Guided sequence

- Opening problem or clinical hook: A newborn infant presents with ambiguous genitalia, non-palpable gonads, and a documented uterus on ultrasound. Why does this represent an endocrine emergency, and what specific steps must precede gender assignment?
- Prior-knowledge check: Normal Tanner staging benchmarks (girls: thelarche 8-13 yr; boys: testicular enlargement 9-14 yr) and the roles of SRY, SOX9, and AMH in fetal sex development.
- Unit sequence:
  1. Unit 1: Delayed Puberty: Constitutional Delay vs Hyper-/Hypogonadotropic Hypogonadism (Ch 24)
  2. Unit 2: Precocious Puberty: Central GnRH Activation vs Peripheral Steroid Autonomy (Ch 25)
  3. Unit 3: Disorders / Differences of Sex Development (DSD): Emergency Evaluation & Ethics (Ch 26)
  4. Unit 4: Menstrual Disorders and Hyperandrogenism in Adolescence (Adolescent PCOS) (Ch 27)
  5. Unit 5: Hyperprolactinemia: Pituitary Macroadenomas, Stalk Effect, and Dopamine Agonists (Ch 28)
- Application case: A 15-year-old girl with oligomenorrhea and severe acne has elevated free testosterone and multifollicular ovaries on ultrasound. Does she meet consensus pediatric criteria for PCOS? Formulate her pharmacological therapy and lifestyle plan.
- Common error: Diagnosing adolescent PCOS based on pelvic ultrasound findings alone, or performing immediate emergency cosmetic genitoplasty in a newborn with DSD without multidisciplinary review.
- Summary and retrieval practice: Revisit the 5 core pillars of Chapters 24 and 25; test active recall with flashcards `fc-024-1` through `fc-025-7`.
