# Module 7 Blueprint: Polyendocrine Syndromes, Cancer Late Effects, and Endocrine Emergencies

## Module identity

- Stable module ID and title: `MOD-07-POLYENDOCRINE` — Neurohypophysial Disorders, Polyendocrine Syndromes, Cancer Late Effects, Gender Affirmation, and Pediatric Critical Endocrine Emergencies
- Source chapter IDs and PDF pages: Chapters 34 to 41 (PDF pp. 922–1090, Printed pp. 925–1092)
- Audience and intended setting: Pediatric endocrinologists, pediatric intensivists, oncologists, adolescent medicine specialists, and emergency physicians.
- Need or problem addressed: Identifying and managing neurohypophysial water disorders (AVP-D vs AVP-R); recognizing life-threatening autoimmune polyglandular syndromes (APS-1, APS-2, IPEX) and multiple endocrine neoplasias (MEN1, MEN2A/B); managing GNAS mosaicism in McCune-Albright syndrome; long-term endocrine surveillance in childhood cancer survivors; providing evidence-based gender-affirming care under Endocrine Society/WPATH guidelines; executing rapid resuscitation in pediatric endocrine emergencies (acute adrenal crisis, thyroid storm, severe hypercalcemia, myxedema coma); and managing Critical Illness-Related Corticosteroid Insufficiency (CIRCI) and nonthyroidal illness in pediatric ICU shock.
- Prerequisites: Posterior pituitary physiology, autoimmune genetics (AIRE, FOXP3, HLA), RET/MEN1 oncogene biology, steroidogenesis, cancer radiation dosimetry, and septic shock hemodynamics.
- Estimated learning effort and basis: 6 to 8 hours of structured self-directed study across 8 source chapters, 40 pillars, 56 flashcards, and 40 authored single-best-answer MCQs.
- Teaching levels supported: Essentials, Clinical Application, and Deep Dive.
- Source edition/version: *Pediatric Endocrinology: A Practical Clinical Guide*, 4th Edition (2024), Springer Nature Switzerland AG. Editors: Sally Radovick & Madhusmita Misra.
- Evidence actually reviewed on / reviewer role / unresolved issues: Chapters 34–41 reviewed against ATA guidelines on Medullary Thyroid Carcinoma, Endocrine Society/PFS Guidelines on Gender Affirming Care, Endocrine Society Clinical Guidelines on Adrenal Insufficiency, and SCCM/ESICM Guidelines on CIRCI in Sepsis.
- Suggested review date: 2027.

## Guided sequence

- Opening problem or clinical hook: A 4-year-old child 3 days status-post resection of a suprasellar craniopharyngioma suddenly develops severe polyuria (urine output 6.5 mL/kg/hour), serum sodium 154 mEq/L, and urine specific gravity 1.002. Forty-eight hours later, without any medication changes, urine output abruptly plummets to 0.8 mL/kg/hour and serum sodium drops to 126 mEq/L. What is the precise pathophysiological cause of this biphasic swing, and why will giving desmopressin during this second phase prove fatal?
- Prior-knowledge check: Physiology of neurohypophysial magnocellular axonal tract, AIRE gene regulation of thymic central tolerance, RET tyrosine kinase activation, G-protein alpha-subunit (GNAS) activation, hypothalamic-pituitary radiation tolerance, and hemodynamic criteria for catecholamine-resistant septic shock.
- Unit sequence:
  1. Unit 1: Central Diabetes Insipidus: Arginine Vasopressin Deficiency and Resistance (Ch 34)
  2. Unit 2: Autoimmune Polyglandular Syndromes: APS-1, APS-2, and IPEX (Ch 35)
  3. Unit 3: Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia: MEN1, MEN2A, MEN2B, and FIPA (Ch 36)
  4. Unit 4: McCune-Albright Syndrome and Other Mosaic Endocrine Disorders (Ch 37)
  5. Unit 5: Acute and Late Endocrine Effects of Childhood Cancer Treatment (Ch 38)
  6. Unit 6: Gender Incongruence and Gender Dysphoria in Youth (Ch 39)
  7. Unit 7: Pediatric Endocrine Emergencies: Crisis Management and Stabilization (Ch 40)
  8. Unit 8: Endocrine Function in the Critically Ill Child: CIRCI and Nonthyroidal Illness (Ch 41)
- Application case: A 14-year-old boy with septic shock refractory to norepinephrine and epinephrine has a baseline total cortisol of 14 mcg/dL. He is started on stress-dose hydrocortisone (50 mg/m2/day). Within 4 hours, his vasopressor requirements decrease by 60%. His thyroid panel demonstrates TSH 0.8 mIU/L, Free T4 0.9 ng/dL, and Total T3 22 ng/dL (severely suppressed). Why should thyroid hormone replacement NOT be initiated?
- Common error: Administering scheduled desmopressin without requiring clinical breakthrough polyuria (causing water intoxication and fatal hyponatremic seizures), or prescribing levothyroxine for low T3 in critically ill ICU patients with euthyroid sick syndrome.
- Summary and retrieval practice: Revisit the 5 core pillars of Chapters 34 through 41; test active recall with flashcards `fc-034-1` through `fc-041-7`.
