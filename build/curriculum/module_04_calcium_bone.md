# Module 4 Blueprint: Pediatric Mineral, Bone, and Calcium Disorders

## Module identity

- Stable module ID and title: `MOD-04-MINERAL` — Pediatric Mineral Homeostasis, Rickets, Pseudohypoparathyroidism, and Bone Health
- Source chapter IDs and PDF pages: Chapters 19 to 23 (PDF pp. 485–665, Printed pp. 477–660)
- Audience and intended setting: Pediatric endocrinology fellows, pediatric nephrologists, pediatric orthopedic surgeons, and clinical geneticists.
- Need or problem addressed: Systematic management of acute tetany and hypocalcemic emergencies; differentiating benign familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia (FHH) from primary hyperparathyroidism; mastering FGF23 biology and modern monoclonal antibody therapy (burosumab) in XLH; managing nutritional and genetic rickets while avoiding hungry bone syndrome; understanding genomic imprinting in pseudohypoparathyroidism; diagnosing pediatric osteoporosis (ISCD criteria) and administering intravenous bisphosphonates.
- Prerequisites: Physiology of calcium and phosphate homeostasis, PTH regulation, vitamin D metabolism, and bone histomorphometry.
- Estimated learning effort and basis: 4 to 5 hours of structured self-directed study across 5 source chapters, 25 pillars, 35 flashcards, and 25 authored single-best-answer MCQs.
- Teaching levels supported: Essentials, Clinical Application, and Deep Dive.
- Source edition/version: *Pediatric Endocrinology: A Practical Clinical Guide*, 4th Edition (2024), Springer Nature Switzerland AG. Editors: Sally Radovick & Madhusmita Misra.
- Evidence actually reviewed on / reviewer role / unresolved issues: Chapters 19–23 reviewed against Global Consensus Recommendations on Nutritional Rickets, International Society for Clinical Densitometry (ISCD) Pediatric Official Positions, and Consensus Statement on Inactivating PTH/PTHrP Signaling Disorders.
- Suggested review date: 2027.

## Guided sequence

- Opening problem or clinical hook: A 14-month-old toddler presents with severe bowlegs and waddling gait. Serum calcium is 9.2 mg/dL, phosphate is 2.1 mg/dL, alkaline phosphatase is 980 U/L, and PTH is normal. Does this child need high-dose vitamin D, oral phosphate, or anti-FGF23 monoclonal antibody therapy?
- Prior-knowledge check: Vitamin D activation (CYP2R1, CYP27B1, CYP24A1), PTH receptor signaling via Gs-alpha, and physeal growth plate zones.
- Unit sequence:
  1. Unit 1: Calcium Homeostasis: Sensor Physiology & Hypo/Hypercalcemia (Ch 19)
  2. Unit 2: Phosphate Homeostasis, FGF23 Biology, and Targeted XLH Therapy (Ch 20)
  3. Unit 3: Nutritional and Genetic Rickets: Physis Histology & Staged Therapy (Ch 21)
  4. Unit 4: Pseudohypoparathyroidism: GNAS Genomic Imprinting & Multi-axis Resistance (Ch 22)
  5. Unit 5: Pediatric Osteoporosis: ISCD Diagnostic Criteria & Bisphosphonate Remodeling (Ch 23)
- Application case: A 12-year-old boy on long-term prednisone for nephrotic syndrome sustains an L2 vertebral wedge compression fracture. Formulate his diagnostic evaluation, pre-infusion vitamin D/calcium optimization, and intravenous zoledronic acid regimen.
- Common error: Diagnosing osteoporosis in a child based solely on a DXA Z-score <-2.0 without a documented fracture history, or performing parathyroidectomy on a patient with familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia.
- Summary and retrieval practice: Revisit the 5 core pillars of Chapters 19 and 20; test active recall with flashcards `fc-019-1` through `fc-020-7`.
